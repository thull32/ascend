Coding interviews at top-tier companies do not test whether you can invent an algorithm in 40 minutes. They test whether you recognise which of roughly thirty well-known patterns a problem belongs to, and whether you can execute that pattern cleanly while talking. Candidates who "grind" hundreds of problems without naming the patterns learn slowly; candidates who learn the patterns and then practise recognising them learn fast and stay calm when the problem is one they have never seen.

This track is organised around that recognition step. Every pattern lesson opens with the **signal**: the phrase in the problem statement, the constraint, or the shape of the input that tells you this pattern applies and another one does not. It then gives the **template** in Python and JavaScript, the skeleton you will write from memory, and walks two or three problems from the full practice collection through it step by step with real traces. Each lesson closes with the variations an interviewer uses to push a candidate off the template, the pitfalls that cost people the round, and the follow-up questions that separate senior from mid-level.

The track assumes the [Core Data Structures](/learn/data-structures) and [Algorithms](/learn/algorithms) tracks: you know what a hash table, heap and binary search *are*; here you learn when to reach for them without deliberating. The final module, on executing the coding round itself, turns the patterns into a minute-by-minute protocol for the interview.
