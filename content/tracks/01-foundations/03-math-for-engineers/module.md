You do not need a mathematics degree to be a senior engineer. You do need a dozen tools fluently enough that they never slow you down: knowing that 2³⁰ is about a billion, that a hash mod a table size behaves well only under certain conditions, that the number of ways to choose k items from n has a closed form whose division is exact at every step, that random 32-bit IDs collide after about 77,000 draws and not four billion, that sending a request to the less loaded of two random servers cuts the worst-case load from about 9 to about 4, and that XOR cancels pairs.

This module covers exactly that toolkit and nothing decorative, and every lesson holds itself to the same standard: the formula is derived from something you already believe, traced on concrete numbers you can check with a pencil, and, where a runtime is involved, measured (CPython 3.14 and Node 24 on one machine, with the caveats stated). Logarithms and powers of two give you the reflexes for complexity and capacity estimates. Modular arithmetic explains overflow-safe code and why hash tables and rolling hashes work. Counting and combinatorics let you size search spaces, test matrices and replica placements, and know when brute force will finish. Probability gives you expected values with linearity, the birthday bound, retry amplification, reservoir sampling with its proof for any k, and the balls-into-bins result behind two-choice load balancing. Bit manipulation traces every trick on bits, from two's complement to the hardware popcount instruction and the 3ⁿ cost of submask enumeration. Number theory traces Euclid forward and backward, the sieve, Miller–Rabin, binary exponentiation and the two ways to divide under a modulus, with the wrong one's output shown.

Each lesson also names what goes wrong in production (the overflowed LCM, the Fermat inverse on a composite modulus, the JavaScript residue rounded past 2⁵³, the retry storm, the hot partition under a "uniform" hash), has code you can run in the browser, and ends with what a senior engineer says when the topic appears in an interview or a design review.
