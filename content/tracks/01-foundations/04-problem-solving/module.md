Most engineers who fail a senior coding round do not fail because they lack knowledge. They fail because they start typing before they understand the problem, they cannot tell whether their loop is correct without running it, they do not recognise which of a dozen standard patterns the problem is hiding, they test only the happy path, and they go silent when they get stuck. Each of those is a skill, and each can be practised deliberately.

This module makes those skills explicit. The problem-solving loop (understand, examples, brute force, optimise, code, test) is the protocol used by every lesson and problem on the platform. Loop invariants and pre/post conditions turn "I think this works" into an argument you can state out loud, which is exactly what senior interviewers listen for. The pattern catalogue maps the signals in a problem statement to the technique that fits. The testing lesson gives you an edge-case taxonomy and the habit of tracing by hand. The final lesson covers how to think aloud, how to structure an explanation, and how to take a hint without losing the thread.

Nothing here requires a specific data structure or algorithm; the later tracks supply those. What this module supplies is the discipline that makes them usable under pressure.
