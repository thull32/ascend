The first module was about driving the tools. This one is about what a senior engineer does with them, and what they refuse to hand over. AI makes it cheap to produce a design document, a code review comment, a list of incident hypotheses or a working solution to an interview problem. It does not make any of them correct, and the cost of a confident wrong answer grows with seniority.

You will see how to use AI to widen the option space in design and review while keeping the decision and the accountability, and how to catch the confident errors (an overstated guarantee, a thousand-fold unit slip) by hand; how to use it to reduce logs, generate hypotheses and drive a bisect during an incident while keeping hard guardrails on production actions; how to learn with AI so that your understanding deepens instead of eroding, with the evidence for why answer mode stalls learning; what AI-native interviews measure and how this app's assisted mock interview grades direction, verification and critique, with two worked sessions in which the assistant introduces a plausible bug; and which skills you should keep practising by hand because every other skill depends on them, worked through a race condition that an agent's tests cannot see.

Each lesson carries the same equipment as the first module: traces on concrete data, runnable code, failure modes with symptoms and fixes, and the follow-up questions that decide a senior interview.
