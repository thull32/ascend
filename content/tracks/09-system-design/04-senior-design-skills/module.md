Mid-level engineers who fail senior design rounds rarely fail on knowledge. They know what a cache is, they can draw a queue, they have read about consistent hashing. They fail because the interviewer cannot see judgement: every option sounds equally good, failure is an afterthought, numbers are missing, the design is frozen at version one, and the conversation is driven by the interviewer rather than the candidate.

This module is about that judgement layer. It covers how to compare options so that your reasoning is visible and your rejections are as informative as your choices; how to think about failure as a first-class design input with blast radius and graceful degradation rather than a checklist at the end; how to turn growth assumptions into machine counts and dollars, and why cost is a senior engineer's problem; how to change a system that is serving traffic, with strangler figs, dual writes and backfills that do not corrupt data; and finally how to run the forty-five minutes of a design interview so that you, not the interviewer, are steering.

The case-studies module supplied the raw material. This module is the difference between reciting one of those designs and being trusted to own a system like it.
