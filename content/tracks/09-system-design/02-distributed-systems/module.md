A distributed system is one in which a machine you have never heard of can fail and take your work down with it. Everything hard about them follows from three facts: there is no shared clock, messages can be delayed, duplicated or lost, and a node that has stopped responding is indistinguishable from one that is merely slow. Replication, consensus, leases, fencing tokens, idempotent producers and CRDTs are all responses to those three facts, and the senior bar is being able to say which fact each one answers and what it costs.

The module builds the theory in the order the ideas depend on each other. Time and ordering come first, because happens-before underlies every later argument. Then replication and quorums, ending with the realisation that a quorum alone does not give you agreement. Raft follows in full, with Paxos and ZAB as the family it belongs to. Then distributed transactions and why two-phase commit is both indispensable and dangerous; partitioning and rebalancing; failure detection, leases and fencing; distributed locks and the Redlock argument; exactly-once semantics as they really exist; gossip and anti-entropy; and finally CRDTs and the collaboration systems built on them.

Every lesson works at the level of a trace, not a definition. You will stamp a three-process timeline with Lamport and vector clocks, run a Raft election through a split vote and repair a divergent follower's log probe by probe, walk a two-phase commit through a crash at every step, compute a phi-accrual suspicion level from a heartbeat series, compare two Merkle trees hash by hash, and merge concurrent edits in an RGA. Where a number matters (how often last-writer-wins drops a write at a given clock skew, how long elections take, how many rounds gossip needs for 10,000 nodes, how much data a rebalance moves per node) the lesson either simulates it and says what was simulated, or quotes the published default of the system that ships it.

Each lesson names the production systems that embody the idea (etcd, ZooKeeper, Kafka, Cassandra, Spanner, CockroachDB, Redis, Yjs), opens up how they implement it, shows how it fails in production with the symptom you would see and the fix, and ends with the follow-up questions a Netflix-level interviewer uses to find out whether you have actually run one of these things. Most lessons include an exercise that implements the mechanism: vector clocks, hybrid logical clocks, a quorum calculator, Raft's commit index and log repair, a Paxos acceptor, a saga orchestrator, a consistent-hashing ring, a broker-side deduplicator, a Merkle diff and CRDT merges.
