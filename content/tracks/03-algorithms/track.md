Data structures tell you where the data lives; algorithms tell you what to do with it. This track covers the algorithmic toolkit that every senior interview loop at a top-tier company draws from: sorting and searching, recursion and backtracking, dynamic programming, greedy choice, graph algorithms, divide and conquer, and the handful of techniques (sliding windows, two pointers, prefix sums, bit tricks) that turn a quadratic solution into a linear one.

The emphasis throughout is on *derivation*, not memorisation. You will see the invariant that makes binary search correct and the three places it goes wrong; the exchange argument that proves a greedy choice safe and the coin system where it fails; the state definition and transition that make a dynamic-programming table fill correctly, with the table actually being filled in front of you. A mid-level engineer remembers that "coin change is DP". A senior engineer can write the recurrence on a whiteboard, say why it is right, and say what it costs in time and memory before typing a line.

Every lesson has a steppable visualisation, an exercise you implement in Python or JavaScript in the browser, and a quiz that checks trade-offs rather than vocabulary. The track assumes [Core Data Structures](/learn/data-structures) and the complexity material in [Engineering Foundations](/learn/foundations); it feeds directly into [Coding Interview Patterns](/learn/interview-patterns), where these algorithms become reflexes.
