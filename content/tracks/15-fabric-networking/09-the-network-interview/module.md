Interviews at network and AI-infrastructure organisations test networking in a few recognisable shapes. "Tell us about the network you work on." "Walk me through what happens when..." "Server one can't ping its gateway." "Prefer ISP one, fail over to ISP two." Then a principal network engineer goes one level deeper on whatever you just said, and a design round asks for the software that watches or changes the network. Each shape rewards a particular structure, and each has slips that experienced interviewers listen for.

This module turns the previous eight into answers. It covers the two-minute network explanation and the one-story pattern that ties fabric, protocols, a packet walk and a failure together, the packet-walk drills with model answers and common slips, a rapid-fire bank of the questions principal engineers ask, and system design for network software (a visibility platform, a configuration pipeline, fleet lifecycle) with the numbers and push-backs that come with them.

It also covers the honest move: saying "I don't know that detail" and pivoting to what you would build or measure. From a software engineer, that reads as strength.
