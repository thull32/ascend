Technical skill gets you to the table; it does not get you the offer. Senior candidates who could do the job are turned down, or levelled below where they should be, because their resume reads like a list of duties, their behavioural stories describe the team's work rather than their own, they misread what a round was measuring, or they accept the first number they hear. Each of these is a separate, learnable skill, and none of them is taught in the problem-grinding that most interview preparation consists of.

This module covers the whole path. It starts with the machinery: how a large-company loop is structured, what each round measures, and how independent feedback, debriefs and hiring committees turn six conversations into a hire and level decision. It then covers the behavioural round, where level is most often decided, with a STAR+ worksheet and a story bank you can build in an evening. A dedicated lesson covers Netflix, Ascend's reference bar, using only what Netflix has published about its culture and the typical shape of its loop. The last three lessons cover the parts on either side of the loop: senior resumes, referrals and screens; levelling and negotiation, with a worked offer comparison and an email template; and the first 90 days in the new role, where the trust that the interview promised has to be earned.

The lessons are practical by design: templates, scripts and side-by-side weak and strong answers, with honest notes about where the process is noisy and where the advice varies by company and country. Practise the interview parts in Ascend's mock interviews at `/interviews`, which run timed coding, system design and behavioural rounds and grade them against the senior bar.
