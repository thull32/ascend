description: The non-coding half of the senior role, made concrete - scope and ownership, influence without authority, review, design docs, incidents, estimation and organisational impact.
---
Senior engineers are not hired to write more code than mid-level engineers. They are hired to own bigger, vaguer problems, to make the engineers around them more effective, and to make decisions that hold up months later. That half of the job is usually taught by osmosis, which is why so many strong coders stall below senior without being told exactly why.

This module makes it explicit, and treats it with the same rigour as an algorithm: worked artefacts, numbers, and decision procedures you can reproduce. It starts with a levels rubric and the same two projects done at mid-level and at senior scope, and with how promotion packets and calibration committees read evidence. It then follows one cross-team decision through seven annotated conversations; reviews a real change from this app's history twice, and uses Little's law to show what slow review costs a team; works a full RFC with its numbers, review meeting and lifecycle; runs an incident minute by minute and writes its blameless postmortem in full; estimates and prioritises with three-point estimates, a Monte Carlo schedule and RICE and cost of delay on a backlog; and ends with a platform-adoption plan, its funnel metrics and a technical document rewritten as a one-page executive summary.

Each lesson has an "under the hood" section on how the process actually works inside companies (calibration, decision rights, review tooling, paging policies, planning cycles, platform funding), failure modes with symptoms and fixes, the behavioural interview questions it prepares you for, and a small exercise that turns the procedure into code. These situations are also the raw material for behavioural interviews: the stories senior candidates tell are stories about exactly these moments.
