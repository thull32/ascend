Senior engineers are not hired to write more code than mid-level engineers. They are hired to own bigger, vaguer problems, to make the engineers around them more effective, and to make decisions that hold up months later. That half of the job is usually taught by osmosis, which is why so many strong coders stall below senior without being told exactly why.

This module makes it explicit and practical. It starts with what "senior" means on real levelling ladders, then works through the recurring situations where leadership shows: driving a decision across teams you do not manage, reviewing code so that it teaches, writing design docs and RFCs that surface mistakes while they are cheap, running an incident and a blameless postmortem, estimating and prioritising with numbers instead of hope, and creating impact beyond your own team.

Every lesson gives you templates you can copy (a design doc skeleton, an RFC, a decision brief, an incident status update, a postmortem), example dialogues you can adapt, and worked numbers. These are also the raw material for behavioural interviews: the stories senior candidates tell are stories about exactly these situations.
