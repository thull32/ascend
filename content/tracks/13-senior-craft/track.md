Algorithms and system design get you through the loop. What keeps you at the senior level is craft: drawing boundaries that survive five years of change, designing APIs and errors that other teams can depend on, testing so that a green build actually means something, shipping several times a day without paging anyone, and leaving behind documentation that lets a stranger operate your system at 3 a.m.

This track is deliberately concrete. The software craft module uses this repository, the Rust and React codebase that serves the lesson you are reading, as its running example: you will read its layer boundaries, error mapping, authentication, middleware, migrations, deploy pipeline and telemetry, and you will see where it makes a defensible trade-off and where a senior reviewer would push back. The later modules cover the languages you will be asked to read and write, technical leadership without authority, and the mechanics of getting the senior job itself.

It connects everything before it. System design tells you which boxes to draw; craft decides whether the code inside those boxes can be changed safely by someone who is not you.
