## 1

A behavioural interviewer has three assigned competencies to cover in 45 minutes. Why does a five-minute first telling of a story hurt you, even if the story is strong?

A, it gives the interviewer more detail than the anchored rubric can use. B, it exceeds a fixed time limit that rubrics treat as a communication failure. C, it uses up the probing time a competency needs, leaving it without evidence. D, it signals over-preparation, which rubrics score below spontaneity.

[think]

The answer is C: it uses up the probing time a competency needs, leaving it without evidence.

Each competency gets roughly ten to fifteen minutes, and most of the scoring evidence comes from the drill-down, not the first telling. A monologue spends that budget, so a competency can end the round with nothing written against it. Preparation is not penalised, and there is no fixed limit; the cost is lost probing time.

## 2

You tell a strong project story entirely in "we". How is it most likely recorded?

A, scored higher on collaboration, which offsets ownership. B, logged in the team's column, so your own part is unclear. C, credited to you, since interviewers assume you led it. D, treated as fabricated, since seniors always say "I".

[think]

The answer is B: logged in the team's column, so your own part is unclear.

Notes tag each action with who did it, and only your own column is scored for ownership. An all-"we" story leaves nothing to credit, and the interviewer spends probes asking what your part was. It is not treated as dishonest, and collaboration needs evidence of how you worked with others, not the pronoun.

## 3

Asked for a costly mistake, you describe a cache outage. The interviewer says: "Traffic beat the forecast, so why is this your mistake?" Which answer scores highest on growth?

A, agree it was mainly the forecast, since that is honest. B, say nobody could have predicted it, so nobody is to blame. C, say you now over-provision everything, so it cannot happen again. D, say the forecast was an input, but the sizing method was yours.

[think]

The answer is D: the forecast was an input, but the sizing method was yours.

Separating the trigger from the cause, and owning the cause, is what the growth anchors reward, especially when the trigger was external. Deflecting to the forecast under a direct probe is the classic failure. And over-provisioning everything is a cost adopted without judgement, not a changed method.

## 4

In the lesson's first story, a candidate argued that checkout should stay synchronous during a move to asynchronous messaging. Scoring it against the judgement anchors, which line lifts the strong version to the top score?

A, it described the platform, and the architecture group's history. B, it reported that the other teams moved over on schedule. C, it mentioned discussions in several meetings and on Slack. D, it stated what would have to be true for the asynchronous design to work.

[think]

The answer is D: it stated what would have to be true for the asynchronous design to work.

The top judgement anchor asks for alternatives with their costs, a decision under uncertainty with evidence, and what would have changed the decision. Stating the conditions under which asynchronous would work is that last element. Context, results and the volume of discussion are evidence for other dimensions, or for none.

## 5

Your strongest story is five years old. Your recent work is cross-team, but less dramatic. What is the best plan?

A, present the older story as recent, since dates are not checked. B, tell only recent stories, since older ones are never scored. C, lead with recent work, and pair the older story with it. D, tell only the old story, since scope is what sets the level.

[think]

The answer is C: lead with recent work, and pair the older story with it.

Recent stories hold up under the drill-down, because the details are fresh, and they answer "what have you done lately?". An older, larger story still adds level evidence when paired with them. Old stories are scored, but they fade under probing, and misdating a story is exactly the kind of inflation that collapses.

## 6

Why do behavioural interviewers redirect "what I would do is…" to "tell me about a specific time"?

A, hypotheticals are reserved for the system design round. B, real stories are easier for the candidate to keep short. C, the anchors describe behaviour, and hypotheticals show only intent. D, hypotheticals take longer to score than real stories do.

[think]

The answer is C: the anchors describe behaviour, and hypotheticals show only intent.

Competency-based interviewing rests on past behaviour predicting future behaviour. A hypothetical shows what you know is right, not what you did, so it cannot be matched to anchors that describe observed actions. After a second redirect, the note often reads: no example provided.
