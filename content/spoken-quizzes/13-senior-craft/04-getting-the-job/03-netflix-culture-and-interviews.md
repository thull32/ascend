## 1

Netflix's current culture memo asks managers to judge the whole record, rather than the bets that did not pay off. What does that imply for a failure story in a Netflix interview?

A, only failures older than five years are safe to discuss. B, frame the failure as a strength, since strengths count most. C, avoid failure stories, since any failure fails the keeper test. D, an owned failed bet is fine; hiding or deflecting it is not.

[think]

The answer is D: an owned failed bet is fine; hiding or deflecting it is not.

The published model separates the outcome of a bet from the judgement and ownership around it, and both the memo's candour and the book's "sunshining" reward talking about mistakes openly. A flawless story bank reads as edited, and deflection or a disguised strength fails the candour the culture names. Recency does not change that.

## 2

Two tellings of the same API deprecation differ only in who decided, and how objections were handled. Which is evidence for "context, not control"?

A, the candidate decided alone, and told nobody, to avoid delay. B, the team voted, and the majority view overruled the objectors. C, the director approved it, and a complaint was escalated upwards. D, the candidate decided, sought dissent first, and changed the plan.

[think]

The answer is D: the candidate decided, sought dissent first, and changed the plan.

Context, not control puts the decision with the person who has the context, who then farms for dissent and owns the result; the manager is informed rather than asked. Approval chains are control, acting without informing anyone is freedom without responsibility, and a vote is consensus, which the informed captain model deliberately avoids.

## 3

As a freedom story, a candidate says: "I shipped the change without telling anyone, because our review process was slow." How is it most likely read?

A, as strong ownership, since the culture removes approvals. B, as candour, since the candidate admits skipping the process. C, as freedom without responsibility, since nobody was informed. D, as neutral, since the speed of the process was not the candidate's fault.

[think]

The answer is C: as freedom without responsibility, since nobody was informed.

Removing approvals does not remove the duty to inform, consult, and own the consequences; the memo pairs freedom with an unusually responsible person. The story invites probes about who knew, and what happened when it broke, and without good answers it reads as going rogue.

## 4

Which feedback story best fits the four A guidelines described in the book No Rules Rules?

A, saving several concerns for the annual review, so they are complete. B, telling your manager the team felt demotivated, without examples. C, private, specific feedback aimed to help, then checking back. D, pointing out a peer's error in the design review, so everyone hears it.

[think]

The answer is C: private, specific feedback aimed to help, then checking back.

The four As are: aim to assist, and make it actionable, when giving; appreciate, and accept or discard, when receiving. Private, specific feedback with a follow-up meets both giving criteria. Public correction confuses candour with bluntness, vague feedback is not actionable, and saving it up defeats constant feedback.

## 5

Netflix publishes an aim of paying at the top of each person's market, for the role and location. Given that, what evidence matters most in the offer conversation?

A, competing offers, since they reveal your personal market. B, the size of the stock grant, since vesting sets total pay. C, your internal level, since pay is fixed by a level band. D, your current salary, since pay is set as a raise on it.

[think]

The answer is A: competing offers, since they reveal your personal market.

A personal-market model asks what the best competing offer for you would be, so competing offers, and market data for the role and location, are the evidence. The published model is salary-heavy, with optional stock options rather than a vesting grant, and it is described as personal, rather than a fixed band.

## 6

Candidates commonly report the hiring manager being involved from the first conversation to the decision. Which published idea is that most consistent with?

A, people over process, since interviews have no fixed rounds. B, informed captains, since the closest owner makes the call. C, the keeper test, since managers must approve every hire. D, highly aligned, since all teams must agree on each hire.

[think]

The answer is B: informed captains, since the closest owner makes the call.

The memo assigns significant decisions to an informed captain, who gathers input and decides, and it prides itself on how few decisions senior leaders make. A hire owned by the manager closest to it fits that. This is inference from public material, and it predicts more variation between teams than a committee model would.
