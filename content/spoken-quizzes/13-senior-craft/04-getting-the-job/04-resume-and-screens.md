## 1

Which resume bullet is strongest for a senior role?

A, owned the payments service, its on-call rotation and its roadmap. B, worked closely with the team to improve payment reliability. C, expert in Go, Kafka, Postgres, Kubernetes, Redis and AWS. D, redesigned retries with idempotency keys, taking duplicate payments from 40 a week to zero.

[think]

The answer is D: redesigned retries with idempotency keys, taking duplicates from 40 a week to zero.

It fills the formula's slots: an ownership verb, the key technical decision, and a measured result, and it opens a story. Owning a service is a duty, however large; working with the team hides your role behind the team; and a list of keywords shows no evidence of use.

## 2

What does an applicant tracking system mostly do with your resume?

A, stores and parses it, so recruiters can search it. B, rejects it if it uses two columns or any tables. C, holds it unread until a referral is attached. D, scores its format, and silently rejects low scores.

[think]

The answer is A: it stores and parses it, so recruiters can search it.

An applicant tracking system is a database with search: it parses the resume into fields, records the source, and tracks the stages. Automatic rejection usually comes from knockout questions, such as work authorisation. Formatting matters because a garbled parse is what the recruiter sees, and searches.

## 3

A former colleague offers to refer you. Why ask them to do it before you apply?

A, referrals are only valid for roles not yet posted. B, in many systems, the first recorded source sticks. C, applying first raises the bar for the referrer. D, recruiters delete cold applications after a week.

[think]

The answer is B: in many systems, the first recorded source sticks.

If your cold application creates the record, it may stay tagged as cold, and lose the referral's faster, more careful review. Referrals do not change the bar, and cold applications are not deleted; they are read in bulk.

## 4

In the recruiter screen, you are asked for a target level, and you say "whatever fits". What does the recruiter's note most likely lead to?

A, a delay until the hiring manager decides. B, consideration for the lower-level requisition. C, nothing, since level is set in the loop alone. D, a senior interview plan, to be safe.

[think]

The answer is B: consideration for the lower-level requisition.

The recruiter matches you to a requisition and an interview plan early, and without a stated target and evidence, the safer choice is the lower one. Level is argued in the loop, but the plan you are interviewed against is set before it.

## 5

An interviewer points at "about 300 engineer hours a month" on your resume, and asks how you know. What is the strongest answer?

A, the manager calculated it for the quarterly review. B, minutes saved per run, times runs a month, from CI data. C, it is an estimate, so the exact figure does not matter. D, it came from a report, so it should be accurate.

[think]

The answer is B: minutes saved per run, times runs a month, from CI data.

Giving the derivation and its source, 26 minutes saved times about 700 runs a month, from the dashboard, turns the number into evidence. Deferring to a report or to someone else's calculation, or waving it away, turns the bullet into a credibility question.

## 6

Early in a recruiter screen, you are asked for your salary expectations. What is a sound default?

A, name the highest figure you have heard, so the anchor is high. B, refuse to discuss pay until a written offer is in your hand. C, state your current salary, so the offer has a verified anchor. D, ask for the role's range, and if pressed, give a researched one.

[think]

The answer is D: ask for the role's range, and if pressed, give a researched one.

Deferring until you know the level, and asking for the range, avoids anchoring the offer to your past pay, and many jurisdictions require posted ranges or bar questions about salary history. Refusing outright creates friction, and an unfounded extreme number damages your credibility.
