## 1

A candidate's packet shows strong coding in both rounds, a design round that followed a standard template, and behavioural stories confined to their own team. What is the most likely outcome for a senior role?

A, hire at senior, because two strong coding rounds set the level. B, hire, one level below, because level rests on scope evidence. C, an automatic extra coding round to confirm the strong signal. D, no hire, because coding is weighted least of all the rounds.

[think]

The answer is B: hire, one level below, because level rests on scope evidence.

At many large companies, coding is largely a bar check: it clears the bar without establishing senior scope. Level is argued from design depth and behavioural scope, and a template-shaped design with stories scoped to one team gives the level case nothing to stand on. Coding does not count for nothing, though: it cleared the bar, which is why this is a hire rather than a no.

## 2

The hiring manager's form reads "great fit for our roadmap; very excited about the problem space", and rates strong hire. How much does it move a hiring committee that never met the candidate?

A, little, since the line records enthusiasm, not observed evidence. B, little, since committees ignore the hiring manager's round entirely. C, a great deal, since strong hire outranks every other rating. D, a great deal, since the manager owns the requisition.

[think]

The answer is A: little, since the line records enthusiasm, not observed evidence.

Committees judge the packet, and a form without an observed behaviour gives them nothing to weigh. The same line can carry a debrief led by the manager, where the manager has the most weight in the room. Committees do read the manager's form; what they discount is advocacy without evidence.

## 3

In an otherwise positive packet, one behavioural form says: "claimed to lead the migration; at the third probe, a colleague had designed it". What does this line most likely do?

A, triggers an extra coding round, since the concern is about depth. B, lowers the level by one, since only one round is affected. C, nothing, since the other five rounds still clear the bar. D, triggers a no hire, since credibility doubts taint the other stories.

[think]

The answer is D: it triggers a no hire, since credibility doubts taint the other stories.

A specific credibility concern is not a local weakness: it makes every other self-reported story less trustworthy, and companies prefer false negatives to false positives. A thin story lowers the level; an inflated one ends the loop. That is why exact attribution of "I" and "we" matters.

## 4

An illustrative timeline from application to offer runs 74 days at a company that hires first and matches people to a team afterwards. Which wait disappears at a company that hires for a specific team?

A, the team matching, since the requisition already names the team. B, the loop scheduling, since the team books the interviewers. C, the committee cadence, since companies that hire for a team have no review. D, the offer approval, since the team sets its own compensation.

[think]

The answer is A: the team matching, since the requisition already names the team.

Companies that hire for a team interview against a requisition that already belongs to that team, so there is no matching phase. Scheduling and offer approval still take time, because interviewers must be found, and compensation is approved outside the team. And some of these companies still add an independent review, such as a Bar Raiser.

## 5

What does an Amazon-style Bar Raiser add to a debrief, compared with a debrief led only by the hiring manager?

A, an outside interviewer who can block a hire below the bar. B, a senior leader who sets the level and approves the offer. C, a committee that decides from the packet, without the team. D, a second hiring manager who breaks ties between the forms.

[think]

The answer is A: an outside interviewer who can block a hire below the bar.

Amazon describes the Bar Raiser as a trained interviewer from outside the hiring team, who takes part in the loop and guides the debrief, and former Amazon executives describe a veto over the hire. It targets the bias a manager-led debrief is weakest on, the urgency to fill a seat, while keeping a debrief's speed. A committee that never met the candidate is the Google-style model, a different design.

## 6

The recruiter asks what level you are targeting. Which answer protects you best?

A, the highest level, to leave room to negotiate downwards. B, their name for senior, with two lines of scope evidence. C, your current title, since it reflects your existing scope. D, whatever level the loop decides fits, to seem open.

[think]

The answer is B: their name for senior, with two lines of scope evidence.

Naming the level on their ladder, with scope evidence, sets the requisition and the interview plan to assess you at that level. Deferring hands the anchor to the company, a title from another company does not map reliably, and overreaching invites an interview plan you will not meet.
