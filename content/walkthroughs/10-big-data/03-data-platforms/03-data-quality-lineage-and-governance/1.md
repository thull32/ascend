@0
The payments team has started recording amounts in cents instead of dollars, every job ran green, and now you need to know everything the bad numbers reached. On screen is the lineage graph. PAY is raw payments. STG is staging, FCT the payments fact table, DIM the customer dimension. Downstream of the fact table sit RPT, the finance report, EXP, a partner export, and FEAT, the churn features, which feed MDL, the churn model. WEB is web events. The tool is breadth-first search, downstream from PAY, with a first-in, first-out queue, so datasets are found in order of distance from the bug. PAY is at distance 0.

@1-2
Take PAY off the queue. Its only consumer is staging, STG, at distance 1.

@3-4
Take STG. It feeds the fact table, FCT, at distance 2. The customer dimension also feeds FCT, but edges only lead from DIM into the fact table, never from the fact table back to DIM, so the search will never reach it.

@5-8
Take FCT, the point where the damage fans out. It has three consumers, all at distance 3: the finance report, the partner export, and the churn features. All three join the queue.

@9-10
Take the finance report, then the partner export. Neither has any consumers, so nothing new is discovered. But one of them has already left the building: an external partner received that export.

@11-12
Take the churn features. They feed the churn model, at distance 4. Web events also feed the features, but only as an input, so WEB is never reached either.

@13
Take the model. Nothing depends on it, and the queue is empty.

@14
Each dataset was taken off the queue once and each edge examined once, so the cost is proportional to the size of the graph. The answer is the blast radius: staging, the fact table, the report, the export, the features and the model, with DIM and WEB untouched. The remediation becomes a plan rather than a search: fix the producer, backfill the fact table, rebuild everything downstream in dependency order, resend the export, and retrain the model. Column-level lineage narrows it further, because a report that reads the fact table but never reads the amount column is not affected at all.
