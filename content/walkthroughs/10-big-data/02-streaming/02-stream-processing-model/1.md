@0
A source, a window operator, and a sink. Twelve events will arrive, at seconds 1, 3, 6, 9, 12, 14, 17, 22, 25, 27, 31 and 33. Windowing turns an endless stream into finite groups, and we will group these same twelve events three ways. First, tumbling windows of 10 seconds: fixed size, never overlapping, so every event belongs to exactly one window.

@1-4
The events at 1, 3, 6 and 9 seconds all fall between 0 and 10, so they go into the first window, and its count climbs to 4. The window stays open, because a later event might still belong to it.

@5
The event at 12 seconds belongs to the next window, 10 to 20. Only now is the first window complete: the operator emits a count of 4 to the sink and throws that window's state away. A window's result exists only once the next window has started.

@6-7
Fourteen and 17 join the window from 10 to 20, which reaches 3.

@8
Twenty-two opens the window from 20 to 30, so the window from 10 to 20 fires with 3.

@9-10
Twenty-five and 27 join it, bringing it to 3.

@11-12
Thirty-one opens the window from 30 to 40, and the window from 20 to 30 fires with 3. Then 33 joins the new window, which holds 2 and stays open. One bucket per window, one result per window, and the cheapest state there is.

@13
Second, sliding windows: still 10 seconds long, but a new one starts every 5 seconds, so they overlap and each event is counted in two of them. The window from 0 to 10 holds 4; 5 to 15 holds 4; 10 to 20 holds 3; 15 to 25 holds 2; 20 to 30 holds 3; 25 to 35 holds 4; and 30 to 40 holds 2. A result every 5 seconds instead of every 10: smoother output, for twice the state.

@14
Third, session windows, which have no fixed size. A session keeps growing while events keep arriving within a 4 second gap, and closes after the silence. The only silence longer than that is between 17 and 22 seconds, so the same twelve events form just two sessions: seven events from 1 to 17 seconds, and five from 22 to 33. The size comes from the data, so a key that is never silent never closes its session, and its state grows without bound.

@15
Tumbling is cheapest, sliding trades memory for smoothness, and sessions fit user behaviour but need merging. All three share one assumption, which this picture quietly made: that the operator knows when a window is complete. Here the events arrived in order. In real streams they do not, and deciding completeness is the job of the watermark.
