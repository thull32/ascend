@0
Producers, a topic called playback events with two partitions, and one consumer group with two consumers. Inside a group, Kafka behaves like a work queue: each partition is owned by exactly one member, so each record is handled by one consumer. Nothing is deleted on read. A member's progress is the offset it commits, and lag, per partition, is the log-end offset minus the committed offset.

@1
The producers append four records to each partition, offsets 0 to 3. Lag is 4 on both: four records written and not yet processed by the group.

@2-3
Consumer 1 polls partition 0 and processes offsets 0 and 1. Then it commits offset 2, the next record it wants. Committing after processing is at-least-once: a crash before the commit replays the batch. Partition 0's lag drops to 2.

@4
Consumer 2 polls partition 1 and processes offsets 0 and 1, calling the handler for each.

@5
Then consumer 2 crashes before committing. The group's committed offset for partition 1 is still 0, although records 0 and 1 have already been processed.

@6
After the session timeout the coordinator rebalances and gives partition 1 to consumer 1, which resumes from the committed offset, 0. Records 0 and 1 are processed a second time. That is the unacknowledged record coming back, and why the handler must be idempotent.

@7
Now the backlog of the title. A spike: producers append 8 more records to each partition, while consumer 1, alone, gets through only 4 in total. Lag climbs to 8 and 10, and the oldest unprocessed record is 4 to 5 seconds old. Nothing fails; results just arrive later and later.

@8
It keeps growing. Another 8 records per partition, and consumer 1 manages 4 more on each. Lag is 12 and 14 records, the oldest about 10 seconds old. A record count means nothing on its own: 100,000 records is nothing on one topic and an hour on another. So alert on lag in time, the age of the oldest unprocessed record.

@9
Consumer 2 restarts and the group rebalances back to one partition each. With two consumers working, each gets through 6 records while only 2 more arrive, and the lag falls to 8 and 10. A third consumer would sit idle: with 2 partitions, 2 members is the most the group can use, so the partition count is the ceiling on catch-up speed.

@10
The trade: the log gives a work queue whose backlog is durable and replayable, at the price of per-partition parallelism and at-least-once redelivery after every crash or rebalance. Size partitions for the catch-up rate you need, and make handlers idempotent.
