@0
One partition, replicated three times: the leader on broker 1 and followers on brokers 2 and 3, each with a log ending at offset 100. Two facts make Kafka replication different from a database's. Followers are not pushed to; each one runs a fetcher that asks the leader for the next offsets. And the high watermark is the offset every in-sync replica has reached: consumers only ever read below it.

@1-2
First, acks equals 1, the asynchronous mode. The producer sends a record, and the leader appends it at offset 100, to the page cache, not even to disk. It acknowledges at once. Neither follower has the record yet.

@3
A consumer asks for offset 100. It is above the high watermark, still 100, so the leader returns nothing yet. A Kafka consumer never sees a record that is not on every in-sync replica, so there is no stale read from a lagging follower. The risk is somewhere else.

@4-5
The leader's broker dies before either follower fetched offset 100. The controller elects broker 2, an in-sync follower, as the new leader, and its log ends at 100. As far as the partition is concerned, the acknowledged record never existed, and when broker 1 comes back it truncates its log to match the new leader. acks equals 1 lost a write the producer was told had succeeded.

@6
Rewind, and use acks equals all, with min in-sync replicas set to 2. Now the leader parks the produce request until the high watermark passes the record, which means every member of the in-sync set has it.

@7-8
The leader appends offset 100, and the producer waits. Both followers' fetchers ask for offset 100 and append the record. The leader learns their new log ends only from their next fetch, which costs one extra round trip.

@9
The high watermark is the smallest log end in the in-sync set, now 101, past the record. Only now is the producer acknowledged, and only now may consumers read offset 100. It is slower by one replication round trip, and a single broker failure can no longer lose the record.

@10
Why the floor of 2? If both followers fall behind and drop out of the in-sync set, all in-sync replicas would mean the leader alone. With min in-sync replicas at 2, the next write is refused with a not enough replicas error instead of being accepted on one machine.

@11
So the standard triple, replication factor 3, min in-sync replicas 2 and acks equals all, survives one broker down with no loss, and refuses writes rather than lose data when two are down. Consumers keep reading up to the high watermark throughout.
