@0
A producer, a topic called events with three partitions, and a consumer group, g1, with two consumers. Each partition is an ordered, append-only log. Hold onto one rule: the producer hashes the message key to choose the partition, and ordering is guaranteed only within a partition. So every event for one key stays in order, and nothing else is promised.

@1-2
The key user 1 hashes to partition 1 and is appended at offset 0. The key user 7 also hashes to partition 1, so it lands right behind, at offset 1. The broker never reorders or rewrites the log.

@3
The key order 9 hashes to partition 2, at offset 0. Partition 0 is still empty.

@4
Now the consumer group. The group coordinator assigns each partition to exactly one member: consumer 1 gets partitions 0 and 2, consumer 2 gets partition 1. Partitions, not messages, are the unit of parallelism.

@5
Consumer 1 polls partition 0, which is still empty in this run. The point is who keeps the position: the consumer tracks its own offset per partition, and the broker deletes nothing on read, so another group could read the same log independently, at its own speed.

@6
Consumer 1 commits its position for partition 0. Where you commit decides the guarantee. Commit after processing and a crash in between replays records: at least once, the normal choice, which needs idempotent handling. Commit before processing and a crash skips them: at most once.

@7
Now consumer 2 dies. After its session timeout the coordinator rebalances, and consumer 1 takes over partition 1 as well. It resumes from consumer 2's last committed offset. Consumer 2 never committed here, so user 1 and user 7 will be delivered again, which is exactly why handling must be idempotent. In the classic protocol the whole group pauses during a rebalance.

@8
Two limits are built into this design. With three partitions, a group can use at most three consumers; a fourth would sit idle, so the partition count you choose at creation caps your parallelism. And a hot key sends all of its traffic to one partition, which one consumer must handle alone.

@9
So keys buy per-key order and horizontal scale on a simple, durable broker, at the price of no global order, parallelism fixed by the partition count, and rebalances that pause consumption. Choose keys that spread load while keeping together the events that must stay in order.
