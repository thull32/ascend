@0
Two map tasks on the left, three reduce partitions on the right, P0, P1 and P2. This is a shuffle, and its rule is one line: every row goes to partition hash of its key, mod 3, so all the rows for one key meet in one task. There are six title IDs, and one of them, title 17, is a new hit with 36 of the 56 rows.

@1
Map task 1 hashes each row's key. Title 17 goes to P2, all 36 rows of it. Titles 54 and 91 go to P0, four rows each.

@2
Map task 2 does the same with its keys. Titles 128 and 239 go to P1, and title 350 joins the hit on P2. Now P0 has 8 rows, P1 has 8, and P2 has 40.

@3
Look at what hashing did. The keys spread perfectly: two per partition. The rows did not, because hashing can place a key but cannot split one. P2 is the straggler with 40 rows, and the stage finishes only when its slowest task does. That is the one task that never finishes.

@4
More partitions do not help: the hot key still lands in one of them. And changing the count from 3 to 4 sends all six of these keys to a different partition, so the whole shuffle is redone.

@5
So the fixes split the key, not the hash. Salt the hot key into several sub-keys and combine the results afterwards, let adaptive query execution split the skewed partition, or broadcast the small side of a join so the big side never shuffles at all. When a stage's slowest task takes ten times the median, suspect one key, not the cluster.
