@0
Six nodes, A to F, joined by weighted edges, and the question is the shortest distance from A to every node, F in particular. Before anything moves, do what the lesson asks: pause, and write down two things. The order in which Dijkstra's algorithm will finalise the nodes, and the final distance to F. The gap between your prediction and what follows is exactly what you did not understand, made visible. The rule you need: always settle the closest unsettled node, because with no negative weights its distance can never improve. A starts at 0; everything else at infinity.

@1-3
Settle A, at 0. Relax its edges: B becomes 4, through the edge of weight 4, and C becomes 2.

@4-5
The closest unsettled node is C, at 2, so C is settled. Now the first surprise for many predictions: the edge from C to B has weight 1, so B improves from 4 to 3. The direct edge was not the shortest path.

@6-7
C also reaches D, at 2 plus 8, which is 10, and E, at 2 plus 10, which is 12.

@8-9
B, at 3, is now the closest, so it is settled. Its edge to D, weight 5, gives 3 plus 5, which is 8, beating 10.

@10-12
The heap first gives up B's old entry at 4, which is discarded because B is already settled. Then settle D at 8. Its edge to E gives 8 plus 2, which is 10, beating 12. Its edge to F gives 8 plus 6, which is 14, F's first finite distance.

@13-14
A stale entry for D is discarded the same way, then settle E at 10. Its edge to F gives 10 plus 3, which is 13, beating 14.

@15
One more stale entry, E at 12, is discarded, and finally F is settled, at 13.

@16
The shortest-path tree is highlighted: A to C, C to B, B to D, D to E, E to F. The finalisation order was A, C, B, D, E, F, and the distance to F is 13. Now compare with what you wrote. If you predicted 14, through D directly, or settled B before C, explain the difference to yourself before moving on. That explanation, not the animation, is where the learning happens.
