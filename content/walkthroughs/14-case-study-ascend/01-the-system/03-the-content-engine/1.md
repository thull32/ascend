@0
Ascend's search finds Dijkstra when you type d, i, j, k, by comparing that prefix against every token in its vocabulary. That is fine at today's size, but it costs time in proportion to the whole vocabulary for every query term. A trie answers the same question by walking only the prefix. Here it starts as a single empty root.

@1
Insert dijkstra. Nothing is shared yet, so it creates a chain of 8 new nodes, one per letter, and the last one is marked as the end of a word.

@2-5
Insert dfs: it shares the d and adds 2 nodes. Divide shares d and i and adds 4. Dp shares the d and adds just 1. Diameter shares d and i and adds 6. Five words now hang off one d, and every shared prefix is stored only once.

@6-9
Now the query: dijk. Walk from the root one letter at a time: d, then i, then j, then k. Each step follows an edge that already exists. Four nodes touched, no matter how big the vocabulary is.

@10
We have reached the node for dijk. Every word in the subtree below it starts with dijk, so the answer is simply that subtree, enumerated depth-first with children in sorted order.

@11-14
Dijk is not a word itself, only a prefix, so descend: s, t, r. None of those ends a word either.

@15-16
A ends a word: dijkstra, the first and only suggestion. The cost was the prefix length plus the size of the subtree below it.

@17-19
A shorter query, di. Walk d, then i, and we are at a node with three children, so there will be more to enumerate.

@20-26
Children are visited in sorted order, so the a branch comes first. Descend through a, m, e, t, e and r, and diameter is the first suggestion.

@27-32
Back up to di and take the j branch. Down through j, k, s, t, r and a, and dijkstra is the second.

@33-37
Then the v branch: v, i, d, e, and divide is the third. The suggestions are diameter, dijkstra and divide, already in alphabetical order because children are visited sorted.

@38
The trade is clear. A trie costs the prefix length plus the size of the matching subtree, instead of a pass over every token. A large subtree is still expensive to enumerate, which is why production autocomplete caches the top few completions at each node. And replace the scan only when a measurement says so: at this size it is well under a millisecond. Then reach for a trie for prefixes, or tantivy in-process, before a separate search service.
