@0
Today Ascend's deploy gate is readiness: the new container goes live once its readiness check answers. That proves it can start, not that it behaves. At a hundred times the traffic, you would want a canary: a small slice of real traffic to the new version, compared with the stable version serving the rest, and widened only while the comparison stays clean. On screen: a load balancer, nine stable instances on version 1, one canary slot, and the metrics that judge it, with a gate of error rate and 99th percentile latency within 1 percent of stable's.

@1
Version 2 starts on the canary instance. Health checks pass, which is all readiness can tell you, and it takes no traffic yet.

@2-3
Stage 1: 2 of the next 20 requests go to the canary. Stable answers in 118 milliseconds at the 99th percentile, the canary in 120, with no errors on either side. Compared on the same window, the canary is within tolerance, and the gate passes.

@4
At half the traffic, 10 requests each, the canary reaches a code path the small slice never exercised. It errors, and its 99th percentile jumps to 410 milliseconds, while stable stays clean.

@5-6
The gate fails, and because stable served the same traffic without errors, the blame lands on the release. Automatic rollback sends everything back to stable. The bad version reached part of the traffic for one window, not everyone for as long as it took a human to notice.

@7-8
The fixed version, 2.1, starts over at 10 percent, then passes at half, 121 milliseconds against stable's 118.

@9
It is promoted, and the remaining instances roll to 2.1.

@10
The caveats: 10 canary requests a window is too few to see a 1 percent error rate, so the gate needs a minimum count; a canary that gets only easy traffic passes and then fails at full rollout; and sessions need sticky routing.

@11
The price is slower rollouts, two versions live for hours, with the same schema compatibility rule as any rollback, per-version metrics, and honest statistics. For Ascend, the per-replica metrics already exist. What is missing is the comparison and the promotion: one replica on the new version, an automated check of its error rate and latency against stable, and only then the rest.
