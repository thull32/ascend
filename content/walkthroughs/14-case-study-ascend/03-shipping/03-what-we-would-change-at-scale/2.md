@0
The lesson proposes a global spend breaker for the AI bill, and this is it: the AI routes on the left, the model API on the right, and a breaker between them. It is the classic three-state circuit breaker with a different signal. Instead of counting failures, it sums today's priced usage across all users. Closed while the total is under a daily threshold, here 500 dollars, an example figure. Open once it is crossed, sending AI features down the existing disabled path. Half-open when the next UTC day begins.

@1
Morning. Calls pass straight through. After each one the breaker prices the tokens it used, input, output, cache reads and writes, and adds them to today's total: 120 dollars of 500 after the first 2,000 calls. A closed breaker costs one addition per call.

@2
Afternoon. A spike, a viral post, a scripted abuser spread over many accounts, or a prompt change that tripled output, pushes the total to 485 dollars. Every user is still inside their own daily budget. It is the sum that is running away, and only a global breaker can see it.

@3
Call 9,001 takes the total to 502 dollars, over the threshold. The breaker trips open and pages someone. Money already spent stays spent; the breaker bounds what comes next.

@4
Call 9,002 never reaches the model. The route takes the existing AI-disabled path and answers at once with a graceful message that AI features are paused for today, while lessons, practice and everything else keep working. Cost of the call: nothing.

@5
For the rest of the day every AI call is refused the same way, 16 more here, and the total stays at 502 dollars. The open breaker is a hard ceiling on the bill, not a slowdown.

@6
Midnight UTC. A new day starts a new total at zero, and the breaker goes half-open: the first calls of the day are let through as trials, because whatever drove yesterday's spike may still be running.

@7
The trial calls cost what calls normally cost, 15 dollars so far, so the breaker closes. Had the spike still been running, the total would have climbed back to the threshold and the breaker would have opened again.

@8
Fuse and bill are different wires. The per-user budgets bound one learner's day; multiplied by every user, they bound almost nothing, about a million dollars a day at a hundred thousand daily users on a worst-case day. The spend breaker is the global fuse, and because it reuses a failure path the product already has, tripping it degrades the app instead of breaking it.
