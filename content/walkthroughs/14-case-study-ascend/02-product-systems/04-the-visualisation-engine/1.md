@0
This is the engine's core idea in four frames. A generator runs binary search for 24 over ten sorted values, from 1 to 31, and records every step as a complete snapshot of what to draw: the values, their colours, the pointers and the variables. The player never re-runs the algorithm; it only changes which snapshot it shows. Frame 0 states the invariant: if 24 is present, it lies between positions 0 and 9.

@1
Frame 1. The middle is position 4, which holds 9. Nine is less than 24, so the target can only be to the right, and low becomes 5. Everything this frame needs, the pointers, the highlighted cell and a count of one comparison, is stored inside the frame itself.

@2
Frame 2. The middle of 5 to 9 is position 7, which holds 20. Still less than 24, so low becomes 8, and the five cells already ruled out on the left are greyed.

@3
Frame 3. The middle is position 8, which holds 24. Found, after 3 comparisons, where 10 elements need at most 4. Now drag the slider back to frame 1. Nothing is undone or recomputed: the player just shows snapshot 1 again, because no frame knows how it was reached. That is what makes stepping backwards free, and it is also why every frame costs memory, which is the reason the engine needs a cap.
