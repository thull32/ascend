@0
A shift moves every bit sideways by some number of positions. A left shift fills with zeros on the right, and anything that falls off the left end of the 8-bit word is lost.

@1
Our number is 5, which in 8 bits is five zeros followed by 1 0 1.

@2
Shift left by one. The pattern 1 0 1 moves up one place and a zero fills in on the right: 1 0 1 0, which is 10. Exactly 5 times 2. Every bit is now worth twice what it was.

@3
Shift left by two instead, and the pattern becomes 1 0 1 0 0, which is 20: 5 times 4. Shifting left by k multiplies by 2 to the power k.

@4
Now shift right by one. The lowest bit, a 1, falls off the right end and is gone, leaving 1 0, which is 2. That is floor division by 2: 5 divided by 2, rounded down.

@5
Shifts are constant time, and they are how you multiply and divide by powers of two, build the mask 1 shifted left by k for bit k, and pack several fields into one word. One caution from the lesson: on negative numbers a right shift can either copy the sign bit or fill with zeros, and languages differ on which one you get.
