@0
Seven empty buckets, numbered 0 to 6. Each one is the head of a chain of entries. To place a key, we turn it into a big integer with the polynomial hash from the lesson, base 31 over the characters, the way Java's string hash code does it. Then we take that integer modulo 7. The remainder is always between 0 and 6, so it is always a valid bucket. That one operation is the whole bridge from an arbitrary key to an array index.

@1-2
First, set apple to 1. The hash of apple is 93,029,210, which leaves a remainder of 1 when divided by 7. Bucket 1 is empty, so the entry goes straight in. One entry in seven buckets: a load factor of 0.14.

@3-4
Next, banana. Its hash is about 2.9 billion, and the remainder modulo 7 is 3. Bucket 3 is empty, so banana goes in there.

@5-7
Now cherry. A completely different hash, but its remainder modulo 7 is also 3. Bucket 3 already holds banana, so we walk the chain: banana is a different key, keep going. We reach the end with no match and append cherry. Bucket 3 now has a chain of 2. That is a collision, and with chaining it costs nothing more than a longer list.

@8-9
Date has a remainder of 4, lands in an empty bucket, and goes straight in. Four entries, a load factor of 0.57.

@10-13
Elder hashes to bucket 3 as well. We compare it with banana, then with cherry, find no match, and append it. Bucket 3 now holds a chain of 3, and five entries share seven buckets, a load factor of 0.71. Three of five keys in one bucket is what a tiny table does: remainders modulo 7 have only seven places to go.

@14-17
A lookup follows exactly the same path. Get cherry: same hash, same remainder, bucket 3. Compare with banana, no. Compare with cherry, yes. Found after 2 comparisons in a chain of 3, and the value is 3.

@18-21
Delete banana. Bucket 3 again, and banana is the first entry, found after one comparison. Removing it from a linked chain is a single pointer change. Four entries remain, the load factor is back to 0.57, and nothing else in the table moved.

@22-25
Now get banana again. The hash still sends us to bucket 3. We compare with cherry, then elder, and the chain of 2 runs out with no match. Banana is absent, and we only had to look in one bucket to know it.

@26
Four keys in seven buckets. The load factor, entries divided by buckets, is 0.57, and that is the expected chain length. A lookup costs one step to find the bucket plus the chain walk, so it stays constant time as long as resizing keeps the load factor bounded. The modulus does the routing; the quality of the hash decides whether the chains stay short.
