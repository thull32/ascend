@0
main calls factorial of 3. Every call needs its own frame, holding the argument, the return address, which says where to resume in the caller, and any locals. The stack pointer marks the top, and it is the only bookkeeping the machine needs.

@1-3
factorial of 3 cannot finish yet, because it needs factorial of 2 first. So it pushes another frame and waits, with its own n of 3 parked in its frame. factorial of 2 does the same for factorial of 1, and factorial of 1 for factorial of 0. Each frame's return address points back into the call above it.

@4
factorial of 0 is the base case: it makes no further call and will return 1. This is the deepest the stack gets, five frames counting main. Recursion depth is live memory.

@5-6
Now the returns. factorial of 0 returns 1 and its frame is popped. The value lands in the caller, which resumes right after the call and multiplies by its own n: factorial of 1 is 1 times 1, which is 1, and its frame is popped too.

@7-8
factorial of 2 returns 2, then factorial of 3 returns 6. Each return pops exactly one frame, in the reverse of the order they were pushed.

@9
factorial of 3 is 6. Four factorial frames went on and came off, last in, first out, and the multiply happened on the way back up, which is why no frame could be discarded early. The cost is frame size times depth, and that is the number to check against the stack limit before recursing over user-sized input.
