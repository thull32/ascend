@0
factorial of 5 is 5 times factorial of 4. The catch is in the order: every call pushes a frame and waits for the smaller answer, so nothing at all is multiplied until the base case returns.

@1-4
factorial of 5 needs factorial of 4, so it pushes a frame and recurses. Then 4 needs 3, 3 needs 2, and 2 needs 1. Four frames are waiting, each holding its own n and a multiply it cannot do yet.

@5
factorial of 1 is the base case. It returns 1 without recursing, and at this moment the stack is five frames deep.

@6
Its frame is popped, and 1 goes back to factorial of 2, which can now finish its multiply.

@7-10
The pending multiplies complete on the way back up, one frame at a time. 2 times 1 is 2. 3 times 2 is 6. 4 times 6 is 24. And 5 times 24 is 120.

@11
factorial of 5 is 120, after 5 calls and a maximum depth of 5. That is order n time and order n stack. The stack holds about 8 mebibytes on Linux by default, so at 32 bytes a frame, an unoptimised factorial crashes somewhere near n of 260,000. That is why, for large n, you write the loop.
