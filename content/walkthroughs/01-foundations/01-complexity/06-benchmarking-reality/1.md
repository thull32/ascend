@0
Six values: 9, 4, 7, 1, 8 and 2. Insertion sort grows a sorted prefix from the left, taking the next element and sliding it back into place. On paper it is the quadratic one. Watch what each step actually touches: a neighbour, then the next neighbour, always adjacent, and nothing is ever allocated.

@1-3
Take 4. The 9 before it is larger, so 9 shifts one place right, and 4 drops into position 0. The first two elements, 4 and 9, are sorted.

@4-6
Take 7. Only the 9 is larger, so it shifts right and 7 goes into position 1: 4, 7, 9.

@7-11
Take 1, the worst case so far. It is smaller than everything in the prefix, so 9, 7 and 4 each shift right, one at a time, and 1 lands at the front.

@12-14
Take 8. One shift, the 9, and 8 settles at position 3.

@15-20
Take 2. Four elements are larger, so 9, 8, 7 and 4 all shift right, and 2 goes in at position 1, just after the 1.

@21
Sorted: 1, 2, 4, 7, 8, 9, after ten shifts and five inserts. Worst case this is order n squared, but on nearly sorted input it is order n, and each step is a tight, adjacent memory access. That is why, in the lesson's CPython measurements, insertion sort beats merge sort four times over at 8 elements and stays ahead until about 64, where they tie. Hold that count in mind for the merge sort on the same six numbers.
