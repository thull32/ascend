@0
Ten sorted numbers, from 2 up to 91, and we are looking for 56. Two markers, low and high, start at positions 0 and 9. The rule that keeps this honest: if 56 is in the array at all, it lies between low and high.

@1
The first probe is the middle, position 4, which holds 16. Sixteen is less than 56, and the array is sorted, so the target can only be to the right. Low jumps to position 5, and half the range is gone after one comparison.

@2
Between positions 5 and 9 the middle is position 7, which holds 56. Found after 2 comparisons. The worst case for ten elements is four probes: start at n, halve until you reach one, and that takes the floor of log base 2 of n, plus one, steps. A million elements would need at most twenty. Any loop that halves its range has this shape: binary search, the height of a balanced tree, and the number of times a dynamic array doubles.
