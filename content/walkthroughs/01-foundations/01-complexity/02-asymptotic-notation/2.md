Seven sorted numbers: 1, 3, 4, 6, 8, 11 and 14, and we want two of them that add up to 15. One pointer starts at the left end and one at the right. The sum tells you which pointer to move: too small, move the left one up; too big, move the right one down.

@1
Here the very first sum, 1 plus 14, is exactly 15, so the pair is found at positions 0 and 6 straight away. The point of the picture is the bound, not this lucky case. The two pointers start six positions apart, and every step moves one of them inward by one, so the gap shrinks by exactly one each time. Whatever the data, the loop runs at most n minus one times. That argument, that every iteration makes progress on a bounded quantity, is the standard way to bound a loop whose counter is not obvious. Put it after a sort, and the whole function is n log n.
