@0
Sixteen sorted numbers, from 3 up to 104, and the target is 85. Low starts at position 0 and high at position 15. Keep one question in mind, because it is what log base 2 of n means: how many times can you halve this range before one element is left?

@1
The middle of 0 to 15 is position 7, which holds 38. Thirty-eight is less than 85, so the target can only be to the right. Low moves to position 8, and eight elements are gone.

@2
The middle of 8 to 15 is position 11, which holds 70. Still too small, so low moves to position 12. Four elements remain.

@3
The middle of 12 to 15 is position 13, which holds 93. That is bigger than 85, so this time high moves left, to position 12. Low and high now point at the same element.

@4
Position 12 holds 85. Found after four probes. Sixteen halves to eight, four, two and one, which is four halvings because log base 2 of 16 is 4. In the worst case there is one more probe to check the last element left standing, so sixteen elements need at most five. The base of the logarithm does not matter inside Big-O, and the number grows slowly: every factor of a thousand adds about ten probes, so a billion sorted elements need at most about 30.
