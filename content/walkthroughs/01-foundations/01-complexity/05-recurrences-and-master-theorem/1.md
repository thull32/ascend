@0
Eight values: 38, 27, 43, 3, 9, 82, 10 and 5. Merge sort splits the range in half until every piece is a single element, then merges sorted halves on the way back up. Each node in the tree is one call. The question to keep asking is how much merging work sits on each level of the tree, because adding up the levels is the whole method.

@1-3
The splits go straight down the left side. Positions 0 to 7 split into 0 to 3 and 4 to 7, then 0 to 3 splits into 0 to 1 and 2 to 3, then 0 to 1 splits into two single elements. That is three levels of splitting to get from eight down to one.

@4-6
A single element is already sorted, so 38 returns as it is, and so does 27. These are the leaves, the base cases.

@7-9
Now the first merge. 27 is smaller than 38, so 27 is written first, then 38. The pair comes back as 27, 38, after 2 writes.

@10-17
The same happens on the right of this branch: 43 and 3 are split, returned, and merged into 3, 43, again with 2 writes.

@18-22
Now the two sorted pairs merge into four. Compare the fronts: 3 beats 27, then 27 beats 43, then 38 beats 43, and 43 goes last. The result is 3, 27, 38, 43, after 4 writes.

@23-39
The whole left half is sorted, so the right half gets the same treatment. 9 and 82 merge into 9, 82, and 10 and 5 merge into 5, 10. Two writes each.

@40-44
Those two pairs merge into 5, 9, 10, 82, with 4 writes.

@45-53
And the final merge, at the root. Each step writes the smaller of the two fronts: 3, then 5, 9 and 10 from the right half, then 27, 38 and 43 from the left, and 82 last. Eight writes, and the array is 3, 5, 9, 10, 27, 38, 43, 82.

@54
Sorted, after 15 calls. Now add up the merging level by level. The bottom level merged four pairs, 2 writes each: 8. The middle level merged two runs of four: 8. The root merged all eight: 8. Every level does n work, and there are log base 2 of n levels, so the total is n log n. That is the recursion tree method: when the work is the same on every level, multiply it by the depth.
