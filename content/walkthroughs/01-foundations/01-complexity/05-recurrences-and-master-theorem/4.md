@0
Naive recursive Fibonacci of 6. Every call to fib of k makes two calls, fib of k minus 1 and fib of k minus 2. It is the same shape as Hanoi, but with an uneven split, so the tree is lopsided. Watch for the same arguments coming back again and again.

@1-6
The calls run straight down the left edge, from fib of 6 through 5, 4, 3 and 2, until fib of 1 hits a base case and returns 1.

@7-12
fib of 0 returns 0, so fib of 2 is 1. Then fib of 3 calls fib of 1 and returns 1 plus 1, which is 2.

@13-19
Here is the first repeat. fib of 4 needs fib of 2, whose value is already known to be 1, but naive recursion has no memory, so the whole subtree is computed again. fib of 4 comes back as 2 plus 1, which is 3.

@20-30
fib of 5 now needs fib of 3, for the second time, and inside it fib of 2 runs for the third time. fib of 5 comes back as 3 plus 2, which is 5.

@31-48
And at the root, fib of 6 needs fib of 4, which is being computed for the second time. That one call drags in fib of 3 for the third time and fib of 2 for the fourth and fifth. Every one of these subtrees is an exact copy of work already done.

@49-50
fib of 6 is 5 plus 3, which is 8. It took 25 calls, though there are only 7 distinct arguments, and the stack never held more than 6 frames. The recurrence is T of n equals T of n minus 1, plus T of n minus 2, plus 1. The tree is not full, so it grows as the golden ratio, about 1.6, to the n, rather than 2 to the n, but it is still exponential: fib of 25 makes 242,785 calls. The repeated subtrees are exactly what memoisation removes. Store those 7 results and the work drops to order n.
