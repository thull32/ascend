@0
Naive recursive Fibonacci of 5. Each call to fib of k makes two calls, fib of k minus 1 and fib of k minus 2. You are watching two things at once: the tree of every call ever made, which is the time, and the call stack of the calls still running, which is the space. Keep them apart, because they grow at very different rates.

@1-5
The first move is straight down the left edge. fib of 5 calls fib of 4 and waits, fib of 4 calls fib of 3, fib of 3 calls fib of 2, and fib of 2 calls fib of 1, which is a base case and returns 1 at once. That is the deepest the stack ever gets: five frames, from fib of 5 down to fib of 1.

@6-8
Back in fib of 2, which now calls fib of 0, another base case returning 0. So fib of 2 is 1 plus 0, which is 1, and its frame is popped.

@9-11
fib of 3 calls fib of 1 for its second half, gets 1, and returns 1 plus 1, which is 2. Notice the stack shrinking as frames finish, even while the tree keeps growing.

@12-18
Now fib of 4 needs fib of 2. Its value, 1, was computed a moment ago, but naive recursion has no memory, so the whole subtree runs again: fib of 1, fib of 0, add, return. Then fib of 4 is 2 plus 1, which is 3.

@19-28
Back at the top, fib of 5 calls fib of 3, which is also being computed for the second time, and inside it fib of 2 runs for the third time. Every one of those repeated subtrees is wasted work, but the stack never gets deeper than it did at the start.

@29-30
fib of 5 is 3 plus 2, which is 5. That took 15 calls, yet there are only 6 distinct arguments, and the live stack never held more than 5 frames. The node count grows exponentially, and it is the time. The depth grows with n, and it is the space. Remember those 6 results instead of recomputing them and the time drops to order n as well.
