@0
The string is six brackets: open round, open square, close square, open curly, close curly, close round. We scan left to right with one rule. Every opener is pushed onto a stack. Every closer must match the opener on top of the stack. The most recently opened bracket is the only one the next closer can match, last in, first out. And the string is balanced exactly when the stack is empty at the end.

@1-2
The round opener is pushed: depth 1. Then the square opener is pushed on top of it: depth 2. Whatever closes next has to close the square one first.

@3
The square closer arrives. The top of the stack is the square opener from position 1, so it matches, and we pop. Depth is back to 1, with the round opener still waiting.

@4-5
The curly opener is pushed, depth 2, and the curly closer right after it matches the top and pops it. Depth 1 again.

@6
Finally the round closer. The top is the round opener from position 0. It matches, we pop, and the depth is 0.

@7
End of input with an empty stack: every opener was closed, in the right order, so the string is balanced. Linear time, and a stack as deep as the input in the worst case. Now test it the way the lesson does. A single closer finds an empty stack, so check for that before you pop. A lone opener leaves something on the stack, so check for leftovers after the loop. And open round, open square, close round, close square has matching counts but is not balanced, which is the input that separates a stack from a set of counters.
