@0
A sorted array: 1, 2, 4, 7, 11 and 15, and we want two values that sum to 15. One pointer starts at each end. Here is the invariant, and it is the whole proof: if any valid pair exists, some valid pair still lies between the two pointers. Every move must discard only an index that cannot possibly be part of an answer.

@1
The ends are 1 and 15, which sum to 16. Too big. Could 15 be in a valid pair? Its partner would be at or after the left pointer, so at least 1, and 15 plus anything at least 1 is at least 16. No partner works, so the right pointer moves left, and the invariant still holds.

@2
Now 1 plus 11 is 12. Too small. Could 1 be in a pair? Its partner would be at most 11, because the array is sorted, so the best it can do is 12. Discard it: the left pointer moves right.

@3
2 plus 11 is 13, still too small, by the same argument. The left pointer moves right again.

@4
4 plus 11 is 15. Found the pair, at positions 2 and 4. Each step that did not find the answer shrank the gap between the pointers by exactly one, which is why the loop ends in at most n minus 1 steps. And the loop condition is low strictly less than high, because a pair needs two different indices. Low less than or equal to high would add a value to itself, the "same element twice" bug.
