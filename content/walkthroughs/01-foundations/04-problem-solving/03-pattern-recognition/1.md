@0
Eight daily temperatures: 73, 74, 75, 71, 69, 72, 76 and 73. For each day we want the next warmer day to its right. The signal in the statement, for each element the next greater one, points straight at a monotonic stack. The stack holds the indices of days still waiting for a warmer day, and their temperatures stay in decreasing order from bottom to top. Each index is pushed once and popped at most once.

@1-3
Day 0, 73, has nothing to compare with yet, so it is pushed to wait. Day 1 is 74, warmer than the 73 on top, so day 0 is popped: its next warmer temperature is 74, one day later. Then day 1 is pushed.

@4-5
Day 2 is 75, warmer than 74, so day 1 is popped with its answer, 75. Day 2 is pushed and waits.

@6-7
Day 3 is 71, colder than 75, so it simply joins the stack. Day 4 is 69, colder again, and joins too. The stack reads 75, 71, 69, decreasing from bottom to top. Three days are waiting, and only the top one ever needs comparing with a new day.

@8-10
Day 5 is 72, and it resolves days in bulk. It is warmer than 69, so day 4 is popped with answer 72. It is warmer than 71 as well, so day 3 is popped with answer 72 too. It is not warmer than 75, so it stops there and is pushed. The stack reads 75, 72.

@11-13
Day 6 is 76, warmer than everything waiting. It pops day 5, answer 76, and then day 2, answer 76. Day 2 waited four days for that. The stack is empty, and day 6 is pushed.

@14
Day 7 is 73, colder than 76, so it is pushed and waits.

@15
The input runs out. Days 6 and 7 are still on the stack, so they have no warmer day to their right, and they get the "none" value. Every index went on the stack once and came off at most once, so the total work is linear, against the quadratic brute force that rescans the same days over and over.
