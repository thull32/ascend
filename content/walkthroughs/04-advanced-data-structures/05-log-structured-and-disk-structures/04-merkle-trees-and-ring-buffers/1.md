@0
One producer, one consumer, and a ring buffer between them with room for three items. Two conditions govern everything: the producer cannot add to a full buffer, and the consumer cannot take from an empty one. This animation shows the classic blocking version, where each side sleeps on a condition variable until the other wakes it. In the lock-free version from the lesson, the same two checks are made by reading the other side's index instead.

@1-3
The producer puts item 1, then item 2, then item 3. The buffer holds three of three.

@4
The producer wants to add item 4, and finds the buffer full. It has to wait. That wait is backpressure: a fast producer is held to the pace of its consumer, and memory stays bounded.

@5-6
The consumer takes item 1, and a slot frees up. The producer wakes and puts item 4, back to three of three, while the consumer takes item 2.

@7-9
The consumer drains items 3 and 4, and the buffer is empty. Now the consumer is the one that waits, until there is something to take.

@10-12
The steady state. The producer puts item 5 and the consumer takes it straight away, then items 6 and 7 the same way. The buffer hovers between zero and one, and neither side waits.

@13
The buffer decouples the two sides: the producer only waits when the buffer is full, the consumer only when it is empty, and the capacity bounds memory. That is every work queue, pipe and channel you will use. Restrict it to exactly one producer and one consumer, let only the producer write the tail index and only the consumer write the head, and the waiting can be replaced by a simple full or empty check, with no lock and no compare-and-swap at all.
