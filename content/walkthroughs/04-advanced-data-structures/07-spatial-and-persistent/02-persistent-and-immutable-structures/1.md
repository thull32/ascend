@0
First we build version 1: the perfect search tree of the keys 1 to 15, inserted level by level, starting with 8, then 4 and 12, and so on. Then we insert 16. The animation updates the tree in place, as an ordinary insert would. Your job is to watch one thing, the path the last insert walks, because in a persistent tree that path is exactly what gets copied.

@1-3
8 is the root. 4 goes to its left and 12 to its right.

@4-11
The next level: 2 and 6 under 4, then 10 and 14 under 12. Each insert compares at the root, then one level down, and attaches.

@12-23
The bottom level fills from the left: 1 and 3 under 2, then 5 and 7 under 6. Each walks two comparisons before it attaches.

@24-35
Then 9 and 11 under 10, and 13 and 15 under 14. Fifteen keys, four full levels, and every leaf is three links below the root. That is version 1.

@36-39
Now insert 16. It goes right at 8, right at 12, right at 14, and attaches to the right of 15. The path is 8, 12, 14, 15.

@40
Sixteen keys, and every insert cost the height of the tree. Now the persistent version of that last insert. Instead of changing any node on the path, it allocates a fresh copy of 8, 12, 14 and 15, each pointing at the new child below it and at its old, untouched other child, plus the new leaf 16. Five new nodes. Everything else, the whole subtree under 4, the subtree under 10, and the leaf 13, eleven nodes in all, is shared with version 1. Both roots stay valid: searching from the old 8 never meets 16, and searching from the new 8 finds it. Two versions cost 20 nodes instead of 31, and an update costs the depth of the tree, not its size.
