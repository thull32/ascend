@0
We will insert six keys into an empty AVL tree: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 and 25. Each node will carry two labels, its height h, counted in nodes as in this lesson, so a leaf has height 1, and its balance b, which is the height of its left subtree minus the height of its right. Hold onto one rule, because it is the whole data structure: after every insert, every balance must be minus one, zero or plus one.

@1-4
10 becomes the root, a leaf with height 1 and balance 0. 20 is bigger, so it goes right and attaches as a leaf. Now we unwind back up the path: 10 has height 2 and balance minus one. Leaning right, but within the rule, so nothing happens.

@5-8
30 goes right at 10, right again at 20, and attaches under 20. Unwinding, 20 is height 2 with balance minus one. Still fine.

@9
Back at 10, though, the left side is empty and the right side is two high: balance minus two. The rule is broken. The new key went into the right child's right side, the RR case, and its fix is a single left rotation about 10.

@10
The rotation is done: 20 is now the root, with 10 on its left and 30 on its right, every balance zero. Its height is 2, the same as before 30 arrived. In-order, the keys read the same as before, so it is still a search tree.

@11-15
Now 40 goes right at 20, right at 30, and attaches. Unwinding, 30 has balance minus one and the root 20 has height 3 and balance minus one. No rotation.

@16-20
50 walks right three times, past 20, 30 and 40, and attaches under 40. 40 unwinds with balance minus one, which is allowed.

@21
But 30 is now minus two: nothing on its left, a chain of two on its right. RR again, so one left rotation, this time about 30, deep inside the tree.

@22
40 has taken 30's place as the root's right child, with 30 and 50 as its children, and that subtree is back to height 2, its height before 50 arrived.

@23
So when we unwind to the root, 20 is height 3 with balance minus one. The repair stayed local.

@24-27
The last key, 25, is the interesting one. It goes right at 20, then left at 40, then left at 30, and attaches as 30's left child.

@28-29
Unwinding: 30 is now height 2 with balance plus one, and 40 is height 3, also plus one. Both lean left, both are legal.

@30
At the root, 20 has height 4 and balance minus two. It is right-heavy, but its right child 40 leans the other way, to the left. This is the zig-zag, the RL case, and a single left rotation at 20 would not fix it. First straighten the child: rotate 40 right.

@31
Now 30 sits as 20's right child, with 25 on its left and 40 on its right, and 40 keeps 50. The shape is a plain right-right lean, so one left rotation about 20 finishes the job.

@32
30 is the new root, with 20 on its left holding 10 and 25, and 40 on its right holding 50. Its height is 3, exactly the height the tree had before 25 arrived, which is why the repair never has to travel further up.

@33
Height 3 for six keys, which is as good as it gets; a plain search tree fed the same order would be height 5. The rule to take away: if the heavy child leans the same way as its parent, one rotation; if it leans the opposite way, straighten the child first. That keeps the height under 1.44 times log n, so every operation is logarithmic.
