@0
A cache of three keys, under the LFU rule: least frequently used. Every key carries a count of how often it has been accessed. When the cache is full, the key with the smallest count is evicted, and a tie goes to the least recently used of those keys. Popularity beats recency. The requests are A, A, B, C, A, D, B, E, A, D.

@1-2
A misses and is loaded with a count of 1. A is read again, a hit, and its count rises to 2. Every read makes it harder to evict.

@3-4
B misses and enters at 1. C misses and enters at 1. The cache is now full, and two keys share the lowest count.

@5
A is read a third time, and its count rises to 3.

@6
D misses, and the cache is full, so someone must go. The lowest count is 1, held by B and C. The tie goes to the less recent of the two, B, last used at step 3, so B is evicted and D enters at 1. A, with its count of 3, was never a candidate.

@7
Now B comes back, and it misses: eviction threw away its history. It enters at 1, and the victim is chosen the same way. C and D tie at 1, and C, from step 4, is older, so C goes.

@8
E misses. D and B tie at 1, and D, from step 6, is the older, so D is evicted. Notice the pattern: the new keys keep evicting each other at the bottom, while A sits at the top.

@9
A is read a fourth time, a hit, and its count rises to 4.

@10
D returns, misses, and evicts B, the older of the two keys at count 1. Ten requests, three hits, all of them on A. That is the protection LRU lacks: a run of one-off keys churns through the bottom of the cache, and the key with the high count is never touched.

@11
And that protection is also the failure mode. Without decay, a key that was hot last week keeps its high count and can never be evicted, while every new key enters at 1 and is the first to go. A shifting working set churns at the bottom and never displaces the stale top. Real LFUs age their counters, by halving them periodically, or with a logarithmic counter that decays, as Redis does.

@12
So LFU costs a counter per key and adapts slowly when popularity shifts. The constant-time version keeps keys in buckets by count, with a pointer to the lowest non-empty bucket. And W-TinyLFU, the policy Caffeine runs, combines a tiny frequency sketch with a small LRU window, to get the scan resistance of frequency and the quick adaptation of recency.
