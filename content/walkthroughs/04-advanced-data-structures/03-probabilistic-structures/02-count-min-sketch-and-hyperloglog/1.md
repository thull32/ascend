@0
Before the sketch, the thing it replaces. This is an exact frequency map: a hash table with four buckets, each the head of a chain. We will count hits on six URL paths. Watch one thing: every new distinct key adds an entry, and nothing ever makes the table smaller.

@1-2
The path slash hashes to 47, and 47 mod 4 is 3, so it goes into bucket 3 with a count of 1. One key, one entry.

@3-6
Slash login lands in bucket 2, and slash feed in bucket 1. Three keys, three entries, and the load, keys per bucket, is three quarters.

@7-9
Slash about also hashes to bucket 2. The table walks the chain, finds slash login, a different key, and appends slash about behind it. That is a collision: two keys sharing one bucket.

@10-12
Slash pricing collides in bucket 3, behind slash. Five keys in four buckets.

@13-14
Slash api slash me lands in the empty bucket 0. Six keys, six entries, a load of 1.5.

@15-17
Reading a count is still fast. Slash login hashes to bucket 2, the first entry in the chain is the match, and the answer is 1 after a single comparison.

@18
Six keys in four buckets, an average chain of 1.5, and lookups stay constant time as long as the table keeps growing to keep chains short. That growth is the problem. An exact counter needs one entry for every distinct key, so at a hundred million distinct keys it is several gigabytes, per counter, per time window. A count-min sketch replaces all of those entries with a fixed grid of counters that never grows, and trades exactness for an estimate that is never below the truth.
