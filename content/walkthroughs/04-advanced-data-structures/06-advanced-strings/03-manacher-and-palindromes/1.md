@0
The answer almost everyone gives for the longest palindromic substring. The text is abacabad, eight characters. Every palindrome has a centre: either one of the 8 characters, for odd lengths, or one of the 7 gaps between them, for even lengths. So try all 15 centres, exactly as the lesson's loop does, and grow each one outward while the two ends match.

@1-3
The first centre is the a at position 0. A single character is a palindrome of length 1, but it already touches the start of the text, so it cannot grow. The gap between positions 0 and 1 fails at once: a and b differ, so no even palindrome sits there.

@4-7
The b at position 1. Grow outward: position 0 is a, position 2 is a, they match, so a b a is a palindrome of length 3. It has reached the left edge, so it stops there, and it is the best so far.

@8-11
The gap after it fails, b against a. The middle a at position 2 tries to grow, but b and c differ, so it stays at length 1. The next gap fails too.

@12-17
Then the centre at position 3, the c. a and a match: length 3. b and b match: length 5. a and a match: length 7, all the way to the left edge. The whole of abacaba is a palindrome, the new best.

@18-24
The rest of the centres find nothing longer. Every gap fails on its first comparison. The a at position 4 stops at once, c against b. The b at position 5 grows to a b a, length 3, then stops on c against d.

@25-30
The last a stops on b against d, the last gap fails, and the final d sits against the right edge. All 15 centres have been tried.

@31
The answer is abacaba, length 7. The cost is the sum of every expansion. On ordinary text most centres stop after a step or two, so this feels linear. But on a run like a repeated ten thousand times, every centre grows to the nearest edge, about fifty million comparisons, which is quadratic. And notice the waste you just watched: once abacaba was known to be a palindrome, its right half mirrors its left, so the centres to the right of c already had known answers before we tried them. Manacher's algorithm uses that mirror to do the whole job in linear time.
