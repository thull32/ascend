@0
The answer almost everyone gives for the longest palindromic substring. The text is abacabad, eight characters. Every palindrome has a centre: either one of the 8 characters, for odd lengths, or one of the 7 gaps between them, for even lengths. So try every centre, and grow outward while the two ends match.

@1
The gap between positions 0 and 1: a and b differ, so no even palindrome sits there.

@2-4
The character at position 1, b. Grow outward: position 0 is a, position 2 is a, they match, so a b a is a palindrome of length 3. The best so far.

@5-7
The gap after it fails at once, b against a. The centre at position 2, the middle a, tries to grow, but b and c differ, so it stays at length 1.

@8-13
The gap after that fails too. Then the centre at position 3, the c. a and a match: length 3. b and b match: length 5. a and a match: length 7, all the way to the left edge. The whole of abacaba is a palindrome, the new best.

@14-15
One more gap fails, and the remaining centres are skipped, because none of them sits far enough from the end to grow past seven. The answer is abacaba, length 7.

The cost is the sum of every expansion. On ordinary text most centres stop after a step or two, so this feels linear. But on a run like a repeated ten thousand times, every centre grows to the nearest edge, about fifty million comparisons, which is quadratic. And notice the waste you just watched: once abacaba was known to be a palindrome, its right half mirrors its left, so the centres to the right of c already have known answers. Manacher's algorithm uses that mirror to do the whole job in linear time.
