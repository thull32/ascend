@0
Aho-Corasick's failure links are KMP's failure function, applied to a whole trie. So here is KMP on a single pattern, hers, which is a trie with just one branch. Phase one builds the failure table: for each prefix of the pattern, the length of the longest proper prefix that is also a suffix of it. The first entry is zero by definition.

@1-4
e is not h, r is not h, s is not h. No prefix of hers ever reappears inside it, so every entry is zero: the table is 0, 0, 0, 0. After a mismatch, there is never any partial progress to keep.

@5-6
Phase two scans the text, u s h e h e r s h e r s, once. The text pointer never moves backwards. u does not match h, nor does s, so the pointer just steps forward.

@7-8
At position 2, h matches, and so does the e after it. Two characters of the pattern are matched.

@9
Then position 4 holds another h, where the pattern wants r. Here is the moment the caption promised. A naive search would move the text pointer back to position 3 and start over. KMP keeps the text pointer at position 4 and slides the pattern instead, to the failure value for the two matched characters, which is 0. The h at position 4 is then compared with the start of the pattern, without re-reading anything.

@10-13
And it matches: h, e, r, s at positions 4 to 7. A full match. The pattern slides by its border, which is zero, so matching starts over from the pattern's first character, while the text pointer stays where it is.

@14-17
At position 8, h, e, r, s again. A second match, at positions 8 to 11.

@18
Two matches, at 4 and 8. The total work is linear in the text plus the pattern, because the text pointer only advances, and the pattern pointer can only fall back as far as it rose. Aho-Corasick asks the same question of every trie node across all patterns at once, what is the longest proper suffix of this string that is also in the trie, and that is how it reads each byte of a packet once against forty thousand signatures.
