@0
Three tokens, the, cat and sat, each with a tiny two-number embedding. Real models use thousands of dimensions. Self-attention lets every token gather information from the others, weighted by how relevant each one is. Think of it as a soft dictionary lookup: compare a query against every key, and instead of returning the single best match, return a blend of values.

@1
Three learned projections turn each embedding into three vectors. A query: what am I looking for. A key: what do I offer. A value: what I pass on if someone attends to me. For the, the query is 0.27 and 0.86, and the key is minus 0.07 and 0.80.

@2
Start with the row for the. Its query is dotted with every key, then divided by the square root of the dimension, here the square root of 2, about 1.41. The scores come out at 0.47 against the, 0.48 against cat, and minus 0.04 against sat. The scaling matters in real models: without it, dot products grow with the dimension and the softmax saturates.

@3
A softmax turns those scores into weights that are positive and sum to 1: 0.38, 0.39 and 0.23. The attends most to cat, but only just.

@4-5
The row for cat. Its scores are 0.04, 0.43 and 0.33, and after the softmax the weights are 0.26, 0.39 and 0.35. Cat attends most to itself, then to sat.

@6-7
The row for sat. Its query points away from the key of the, giving a score of minus 0.42, then minus 0.08 for cat and 0.33 for itself. The weights are 0.22, 0.31 and 0.47. Sat attends most to itself, then to cat.

@8
Each output is the weighted sum of the values. For the, 0.38 of its own value, plus 0.39 of cat's, plus 0.23 of sat's, gives a new vector, 0.41 and 0.07. The token now carries context from the whole sequence, in proportions the model chose.

@9
But a language model must not see the future. A decoder masks every later position, setting its score to minus infinity before the softmax, so its weight becomes exactly zero. Now cat may only attend to the and to itself, with weights 0.40 and 0.60. That mask is what lets one forward pass over a document train every next-token prediction at once.

@10
Here every token scored every token: 3 squared, 9 scores. At a 4,000-token context that is about 16.8 million scores per head, per layer. That square is the reason for KV caches, FlashAttention and the cheaper attention variants. Multi-head attention runs several of these in parallel, each with its own projections, so each head can choose where to look on its own.
