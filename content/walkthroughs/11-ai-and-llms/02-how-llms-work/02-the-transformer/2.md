@0
Follow one token, cat, through one transformer block, with a model dimension of 4 where GPT-2 small uses 768. Its input is its token embedding plus a position embedding, giving the vector 0.80, minus 0.30, 0.50 and 0.10. Remember one sentence for the whole block: attention moves information between positions, and the MLP transforms information within a position.

@1
First a LayerNorm. Subtract the mean of the four numbers, 0.28, and divide by their standard deviation, 0.41. The vector becomes 1.27, minus 1.39, 0.54 and minus 0.42. Normalising keeps activations on a stable scale, so a deep stack of blocks can train.

@2
Now self-attention, using the weights from the masked attention example: 0.6 of cat's own normalised vector, plus 0.4 of a context vector gathered from the other tokens. The result is 0.80, minus 0.63, 0.25 and minus 0.13. This is the only place in the block where tokens exchange information.

@3
The residual connection: add the attention output back onto the original input. The vector becomes 1.60, minus 0.93, 0.75 and minus 0.03. The attention sub-layer only has to learn a correction, and gradients get a direct path back through the addition.

@4
A second LayerNorm, this time with mean 0.35 and standard deviation 0.94, gives 1.34, minus 1.36, 0.43 and minus 0.40.

@5
Then the MLP. It expands the vector to 4 times the width, 16 units, applies a ReLU, under which 11 of the 16 fire, and projects back down to 4: 0.73, minus 0.30, 0.34 and 0.95. It runs on each position alone, with no mixing. It is where most of the parameters live, and by most accounts much of what a model knows.

@6
Residual again: the vector from before the MLP plus the MLP's output gives 2.33, minus 1.23, 1.09 and 0.92. The token leaves the block as four numbers in the same space it entered, ready for the next block.

@7
That is one block: attention to move information, an MLP to transform it, each wrapped in a norm and a residual add. A block has about 12 times d squared parameters, two thirds of them in the MLP. GPT-2 small stacks 12 blocks with d of 768, and a 70-billion-class model about 80 blocks with d of 8,192. After the last block, a final linear layer over the vocabulary turns each position into next-token scores.
