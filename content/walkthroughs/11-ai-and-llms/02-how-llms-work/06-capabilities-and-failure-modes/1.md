@0
The same six logits for "The capital of France is": Paris 6.1, a 4.0, the 3.6, Lyon 3.1, not 2.5 and famous 2.2. Watch Lyon. It is plausible, it is a French city, and it is wrong. The question is how it can end up in the answer.

@1
The softmax at temperature 1 gives Paris 0.77, a 0.09, the 0.06, and Lyon about 0.04. Greedy decoding would always pick Paris.

@2
Now raise the temperature to 1.5. Dividing the logits by 1.5 shrinks the gaps between them, so the distribution flattens: Paris falls from 0.77 to 0.58, and the tail grows, with famous doubling from 0.02 to 0.04.

@3
Top-p at 0.9 keeps the smallest set of tokens whose mass reaches 0.9. At temperature 1 that set had three tokens. Now it needs four, and the fourth is Lyon. After renormalising, Paris has 0.64, a 0.16, the 0.12, and Lyon 0.09. A wrong but plausible city is now a live option, about one draw in eleven.

@4
The draw is 0.74. Walking the cumulative distribution, Paris covers up to 0.64 and a covers up to about 0.79, so this draw lands on a, which is harmless here. But any draw above about 0.91 would have produced Lyon, said in exactly the same confident register as Paris.

@5
That is hallucination in miniature: the model has no step that checks a fact, and sampling always has to emit something. For a famous fact, the top choice is right and only sampling can go wrong. For an obscure fact, the top choice itself may be invented, which is why lowering the temperature does not cure hallucination. It only makes the model consistently give its best guess. The fix is checking the output against something that does not share the model's failure modes: a source document, a tool, a test, or a person.
