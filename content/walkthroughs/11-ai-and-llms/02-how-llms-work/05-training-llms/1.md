@0
A pretrained 7-billion-parameter model, adapted to one task. A full fine-tune would update all 6.7 billion weights and carry about 80 gigabytes of optimiser state for them. LoRA freezes every original weight and instead trains small low-rank adapters, two thin matrices of rank 8 whose product is added to a weight matrix. Here they sit on the attention matrices: 4.2 million trainable parameters, 0.06 percent of the model, and about 16 gigabytes in total, so one GPU is enough.

@1
The data is about 2,000 prompt and response pairs, such as "Summarise the ticket", followed by the ideal summary. The loss is computed only on the response tokens. The prompt is masked out, so the model learns to answer, not to predict what users type.

@2-5
Epoch 1 runs the loop once. Forward: every batch goes through the frozen base plus the adapters, producing next-token predictions for the response. The cross-entropy on the response tokens falls from 2.31 to 1.42, and on the held-out eval set it is 1.51. Backward: gradients flow through the frozen weights but are kept only for the adapters, which is why memory stays small. Then AdamW updates the adapters with a learning rate of 0.0002. Full fine-tunes use a much smaller rate, around 0.00001.

@6-9
Epoch 2, the same four steps. The training loss falls from 1.42 to 0.98, and the eval loss improves too, to 1.12.

@10-13
Epoch 3. Training loss 0.81, eval loss 1.06. Both are still falling, but the gap between them is widening.

@14-17
Epoch 4, and here is the moment to watch. The training loss keeps falling, to 0.74. The eval loss turns upward, from 1.06 to 1.09. The model has started memorising its 2,000 examples instead of learning the task. This is ordinary overfitting, and the fix is ordinary too: a held-out set, and stop at the best eval checkpoint.

@18
Over four epochs the training loss fell from 2.31 to 0.74, and that number alone would have hidden the problem. Watch the eval loss, not the training loss. For serving, the adapter product is merged into the original weights, with no extra inference cost. And use fine-tuning for what it is good at: format, tone and narrow skills. For facts that change, put them in the context with retrieval.
