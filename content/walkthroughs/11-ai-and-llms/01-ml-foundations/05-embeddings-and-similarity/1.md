@0
Six words, each turned into a vector of four numbers. Real models use hundreds or thousands of dimensions that are learned and unnamed; here they are labelled royal, male, female and food, so you can see why words land where they do. King is high on royal and male. Queen is high on royal and female. Man and woman are low on royal and high on their gender. Apple and banana are almost all food.

@1
First, each vector's length: king and queen 1.21, man and woman 0.93, apple 0.92, banana 0.86. Length is not what we care about. Similarity should compare direction only, so we use cosine: the dot product divided by both lengths. One means the same direction, zero means unrelated.

@2-7
Now fill the grid one row at a time. King against queen: a dot product of 0.98, over 1.21 times 1.21, gives 0.67. But king's nearest neighbour is not queen. It is man, at 0.81, because in this space king and man share the strong male dimension. Queen's nearest is woman, also at 0.81. Man's nearest is king, and woman's is queen. Across the topics, similarities stay low, about a quarter: woman against apple is 0.25, banana against king also 0.25. And apple against banana is 1, as close as two vectors get, because both point almost entirely along food.

@8
Now the famous trick. Take king, subtract man, add woman. The male dimension cancels and the female one appears, giving 0.9 royal, 0 male, 0.9 female and 0.1 food. The nearest word to that vector is queen, with a cosine of 0.99. Directions in the space can encode relationships, not only closeness. Treat it as an illustration, though: published analogy results are selected examples, and many analogies fail.

@9
And that is the whole of semantic search and retrieval for a chatbot: embed the query, compare it with every stored vector, and return the top few by cosine. Normalise every vector to length one when you store it, and cosine becomes a plain dot product, so dot product, cosine and Euclidean distance all rank the same way. The trap is comparing vectors from two different model versions: same dimension, unrelated coordinates.
