@0
Eight points in two clusters: four of class 0 in the lower left, four of class 1 in the upper right. The model is logistic regression: a weighted sum of the two coordinates plus a bias, squashed by a sigmoid into a probability of class 1. The boundary is wherever that probability is exactly one half. With every weight at zero, every point gets one half, the cross-entropy loss is 0.69, and there is no boundary yet.

@1
Step 1, with a learning rate of 0.5. Every class 1 point is under-predicted, so the gradient pushes both weights up, to 0.5 and 0.36, while the bias stays at zero. But look where the boundary lands: below every point, so the model now calls all eight class 1. The loss rises to 0.97, and accuracy is still 4 out of 8. The step was too big, and it overshot.

@2
Step 2. The gradient flips sign and throws the weights back, to 0.08 and minus 0.09, and the bias drops to minus 0.21. The loss is 0.68, and accuracy is still 4 out of 8.

@3-7
Watch the next five steps, because the pattern is the lesson. The gradient flips sign every step, and the weights swing back and forth. The loss zig-zags: 1.03, then 0.56, then 0.69, 0.66, and back up to 1.07. Accuracy jumps to 7 out of 8 at step 4 and falls straight back to 4 out of 8. Each step lands on the far side of the minimum. Meanwhile the bias drifts downward, from minus 0.19 to about minus 0.5.

@8-12
From step 8 the swings die down. The loss settles around 0.43, and the weights hover near 0.5 for the first coordinate and about minus 0.1 to minus 0.2 for the second, while the bias keeps creeping down, to minus 1.04. Accuracy wobbles between 6 and 7 out of 8, and only at step 12 does the boundary finally separate all eight points.

@13
The boundary is the line where the weighted sum plus the bias is zero, and the further a point sits from it, the more confident the model is. The loss is convex, so gradient descent does reach the global optimum, but these twelve steps were dominated by a step size too large for the data. The longer-run point the lesson makes is not visible yet: once separable data is perfectly split, the loss can only keep falling by scaling the weights up without limit, which is why L2 regularisation is standard.
