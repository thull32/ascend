@0
The same four batch jobs, and this time no formula. We start with the worst possible guess, slope 0 and intercept 0: a flat line along the bottom, with a mean squared error of 44.75. The rule for every step is one sentence: compute the gradient, which points uphill, and step the opposite way, scaled by the learning rate of 0.05.

@1
Step 1. Every prediction is far too low, so both gradients are large and negative: minus 36.5 for the slope and minus 12.5 for the intercept. Stepping against them lifts the slope to about 1.83 and the intercept to about 0.63. The line swings up towards the points, and the loss falls from 44.75 to 1.4 in a single step.

@2
Step 2. The errors are smaller now, so the gradients are too: minus 6 for the slope and about minus 2.1 for the intercept. The slope reaches about 2.13, the intercept 0.73, and the loss drops to 0.22. Nobody lowered the learning rate. The steps shrink on their own, because the gradient is proportional to the remaining error.

@3
Step 3. Gradients of about minus 1 and minus 0.4 nudge the slope to 2.17 and the intercept to 0.75. The loss is 0.19, within about 6 percent of the best possible. On the loss curve, this is where it flattens.

@4-10
Steps 4 to 10 look almost identical on the plot, and that is the interesting part. The slope's gradient falls to nearly zero and then flips slightly positive, so the slope settles near 2.18. The intercept's gradient stays at about minus 0.07, so b crawls upward: 0.76, 0.77, 0.78. The loss sits at 0.18 the whole time. This is the long, narrow valley at work. The descent across it was dramatic, and the walk along its nearly flat floor is painfully slow.

@11
After 10 steps the slope is 2.17 and the intercept 0.78, with the loss down from 44.75 to 0.18. The exact optimum is 2.1 and 1, and the intercept will take hundreds more steps to get there. Each step costs one pass over the data. Too large a learning rate overshoots and diverges, too small a one crawls, and that is why optimisers like Adam adapt the step size for every parameter. Standardising the input fixes the slow valley at its source.
