@0
A small network: two inputs, three hidden units, one output. The input is 1 and 0.5, and the number on each edge is a weight. Every hidden unit does the same two things: a weighted sum of its inputs plus a bias, then a ReLU, which keeps a positive number and turns a negative one into zero. That bend is the whole point. Without it, any stack of layers collapses into a single straight line.

@1
Hidden unit 1. Its weights are 0.5 and minus 0.4, and its bias is 0.1. So 0.5 times 1, minus 0.4 times 0.5, plus 0.1, gives 0.4. That is positive, so the ReLU passes it through unchanged: 0.4.

@2
Hidden unit 2. Weights 0.3 and 0.8, bias minus 0.2. That is 0.3, plus 0.4, minus 0.2: 0.5. Positive again, so its output is 0.5.

@3
Hidden unit 3. Weights minus 0.6 and 0.2, bias 0.05. Minus 0.6, plus 0.1, plus 0.05, gives minus 0.45. Negative, so the ReLU clips it to zero and the unit is switched off for this input. Whatever its outgoing weight is, it contributes nothing.

@4
Now the output. Its weights are 0.7, minus 0.5 and 0.9 on the three hidden units, plus a bias of 0.1. That is 0.7 times 0.4, minus 0.5 times 0.5, plus 0.9 times zero, plus 0.1, which comes to 0.13. A sigmoid squashes that into a probability: 0.53.

@5
The prediction is 0.53, the probability of class 1. The whole forward pass was two matrix multiplies, two by three and then three by one, with a non-linearity after each. That is the shape of every network up to a large language model: matrix multiplies to mix, bends to break the straight line, and about two floating-point operations per parameter per example. Keep an eye on the switched-off third unit, because it matters for training.
