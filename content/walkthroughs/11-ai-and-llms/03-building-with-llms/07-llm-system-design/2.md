@0
A token bucket, the shape behind the coach's per-session rate limit. This bucket holds at most 5 tokens and gains 1 every second. Each request takes a token; if none is left, the request is rejected. Two numbers, two jobs: capacity sets the largest burst, and the refill rate sets the sustained rate.

@1-5
Five requests arrive at the same instant, at time zero. Each takes a token and is forwarded: 4 left, then 3, 2, 1, and finally 0. The bucket has absorbed a burst of 5, its full capacity.

@6-7
Requests 6 and 7 arrive in that same instant, and the bucket is empty. Both are rejected with a 429 and a Retry-After hint, telling the client when to come back. The client pays for the overload, not the model provider.

@8
Request 8 arrives at two seconds. Two tokens have refilled since time zero, so it takes one and is forwarded, leaving 1.

@9-10
At three seconds, one more token has refilled, so 2 are available. Requests 9 and 10 both arrive then, and both are forwarded, emptying the bucket again. After the first burst, the sustained rate is exactly the refill rate, one per second.

@11
Sizing is the failure mode in both directions. A capacity too large lets one client dump several times the sustained rate onto the backend in one instant. A capacity too small rejects legitimate bursts, such as a page that fires several calls at once.

@12
The token bucket allows bursts up to its capacity while enforcing an average rate, and it needs only two numbers per client: tokens left and the time of the last refill. The coach's limiter is the same shape at a different size: a burst of 20, refilled at one request every three seconds, keyed by session, with its state in Postgres so every replica shares the allowance.
