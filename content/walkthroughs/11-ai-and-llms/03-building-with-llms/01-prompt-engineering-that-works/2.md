@0
Two turns of a conversation, in miniature. Request 1 is the prompt The cat sat. Prefill runs it through the model in one pass, computing a key and a value for every prompt token in every layer: three pairs here, thousands for a real system prompt. Keep your eye on this work, because it is exactly what a prompt cache hit skips. The last position predicts the first output token, on.

@1
Decode step 1. Only on runs through the layers; its pair is appended, four cached, and it attends over all of them to emit the. Within one request, keys and values are computed once.

@2
Decode step 2 does the same for the, five cached, and emits mat.

@3
Request 1 ends with the reply, on the mat. Its working cache would normally be thrown away. With prompt caching, the provider keeps the prompt's three pairs as a cache entry for that exact prefix, 5 minutes on the lesson's Anthropic figures, refreshed on each hit.

@4
Turn 2 resends everything: the original prompt, the reply, and the new message, nine tokens. The first three are byte-identical to the cached prefix, so the lookup hits and those three pairs are read instead of computed.

@5
Prefill runs only over the six new tokens: six pairs computed instead of nine. That is the whole mechanism: the next turn prefills only what is new, which also shortens the wait for the first token.

@6
The billing follows the mechanism: writing the cache costs 1.25 times the input price and a hit costs a tenth of it on most models, so with a long stable prefix most of every turn is cheap. And the rule that breaks it: the match is exact and in order, so one changed byte invalidates everything after it. Static content goes first, and anything volatile, such as a timestamp or a user's name, goes last.
