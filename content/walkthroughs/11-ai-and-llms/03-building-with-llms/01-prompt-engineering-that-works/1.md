@0
The context is "The capital of France is", and the model's last layer gives a score to every token in its vocabulary. These are the top six: Paris 6.1, a 4.0, the 3.6, Lyon 3.1, not 2.5 and famous 2.2. Hold onto where these numbers come from: the context. Everything in the prompt shapes them. Everything after this frame only picks among them.

@1
The softmax turns the scores into probabilities: Paris 0.77, a 0.09, the 0.06, and small amounts for the rest. Greedy decoding would always pick Paris.

@2
Now a low temperature, 0.3. Dividing the logits by 0.3 stretches the gaps so far that Paris rises from 0.77 to essentially 1, and famous falls to essentially 0.

@3
Top-p at 0.9 keeps the smallest set of tokens whose mass reaches 0.9. At this temperature that is a single token, Paris, holding all the mass.

@4
The draw is 0.74, and with Paris covering the whole range, Paris is the next token. Draw again and you get Paris again: low temperature makes the output repeatable.

@5
Repeatable is not the same as correct. Here the top candidate happens to be right. When a prompt is ambiguous, the most likely reading may be the wrong one, and a low temperature simply gives you that wrong reading more consistently. That is why sampling settings cannot fix a bad prompt. The fix is upstream, in the prompt itself: state the task, the output's shape, the edge cases, and test it against labelled examples.
