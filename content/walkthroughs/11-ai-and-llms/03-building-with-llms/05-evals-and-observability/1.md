@0
One retrieval-augmented request, stage by stage: the question is what is the refund window? A log line per model call cannot tell you why an answer was slow or wrong. A trace can: one tree of timed spans, one span per stage, each carrying the attributes that explain it. As each stage appears, ask what its span should record. The frames show the stages, not the timings, so the numbers to log come from the lesson.

@1
Stage one: embed the query, with the same embedding model that indexed the documents, into a vector of 1,536 numbers in practice. Its span records the embedding model and version, because vectors from two models are not comparable and a silent model change is a classic retrieval failure.

@2
Stage two: search the index, 12,400 chunks, for the nearest neighbours by cosine. Policy section 3 scores 0.91, returns section 1 0.63, warranty section 2 0.55, shipping section 4 0.42. The retrieval span records its latency and the ids it returned.

@3
Stage three: keep the top 2, policy section 3, which answers the question, and returns section 1, which does not. Those chunk ids on the span are what let you separate a retrieval failure from a generation failure later. This is where a recall metric attaches.

@4
Stage four: assemble the prompt, instructions plus the chunks with their ids plus the question, about 227 tokens. Record the prompt version and its size, so a bad answer traces back to the prompt that produced it.

@5
Stage five: the model answers from the context it was given. This span is the expensive one. In the lesson's example trace, the chat span took 1,755 of 2,480 milliseconds. Record time to first token, input, cached and output tokens, and the finish reason.

@6
The answer: refunds are accepted within 30 days of delivery, citing policy section 3. With every stage a span, the right metric attaches to the right place: recall at retrieval, faithfulness at generation. And the tree shows things an average hides. In that same example, the user waited 710 milliseconds before the model was even called, 385 of them in a query rewrite, the cheapest win in the whole request, and only the span tree showed it.
