@0
One retrieval-augmented request, stage by stage: the question is what is the refund window? A log line per model call cannot tell you why an answer was slow or wrong. A trace can: one tree of timed spans, one span per stage, each carrying the attributes that explain it. The request opens a trace, and as each stage runs, its span appears in the panel at the bottom.

@1
Stage one: embed the query, with the same embedding model that indexed the documents, into a vector of 1,536 numbers in practice. Its span, embed query, runs from 0 to 18 milliseconds and records the model and the dimension, because vectors from two models are not comparable and a silent model change is a classic retrieval failure.

@2
Stage two: search the index, 12,400 chunks, for the nearest neighbours by cosine. Policy section 3 scores 0.91, returns section 1 0.63, warranty section 2 0.55, shipping section 4 0.42. The vector search span runs from 18 to 41 milliseconds and records k, 2, and the ids it returned. Those ids are what let you separate a retrieval failure from a generation failure later.

@3
Stage three: keep the top 2, policy section 3, which answers the question, and returns section 1, which does not. This is where a recall metric attaches, measured against the ids on that span.

@4
Stage four: assemble the prompt, instructions plus the chunks with their ids plus the question, about 227 tokens. The build prompt span takes 2 milliseconds and records the prompt's size and version, so a bad answer traces back to the prompt that produced it.

@5
Stage five: the model answers from the context it was given. The chat span runs from 45 to 1,420 milliseconds, by far the longest, and records the time to first token, 610 milliseconds after the call, the input and output tokens, and the stop reason.

@6
The answer: refunds are accepted within 30 days of delivery, citing policy section 3, and the root span closes at 1,425 milliseconds. Now the tree answers questions an average cannot: the chat span is 1,375 of those milliseconds, and the answer saw exactly two chunks. Each metric attaches to its own span: recall at vector search, faithfulness at chat. In the lesson's longer example, the same kind of tree showed the user waiting 710 milliseconds before the model was even called, 385 of them in a query rewrite, the cheapest win in the whole request.
