@0
One question, followed through the online half of retrieval-augmented generation: what is the refund window? Instead of hoping the model memorised your help centre, you fetch the relevant passages at query time and put them in the prompt. Almost all of the engineering, and almost all of the failures, sit in the retrieval half, so watch that half closely.

@1
First, embed the question into a vector, with 1,536 dimensions in practice. It must be the same embedding model that indexed the documents. Vectors from two different models live in unrelated spaces, and comparing them is meaningless.

@2
Search the vector index, an HNSW graph over 12,400 chunks of about 300 tokens each, for the nearest neighbours by cosine similarity. The scores: policy section 3 at 0.91, returns section 1 at 0.63, warranty section 2 at 0.55, and shipping section 4 at 0.42.

@3
Keep the top 2. Policy section 3 says refunds are accepted within 30 days of delivery. Returns section 1 is about the return address and the authorisation process: topically close, but it does not answer the question. Similarity measures closeness of topic, not whether a passage answers, which is why a reranker often re-scores these candidates.

@4
Assemble the prompt: the instructions, the retrieved chunks with their ids, and the question, about 227 tokens in all. The ids are what make a citation possible. And the chunks are data, not instructions; say so in the system prompt, to blunt any injection hidden in a document.

@5
The model answers from the context it was given rather than from memory, so the answer can be current, and it can point at its source.

@6
The answer: refunds are accepted within 30 days of delivery, citing policy section 3. Updating knowledge now means updating an index, not retraining a model. But if retrieval had missed policy section 3, no prompt could have saved the answer. Most failures here are retrieval failures, so measure recall on a labelled set of questions before tuning the prompt, and re-index whenever the documents change.
