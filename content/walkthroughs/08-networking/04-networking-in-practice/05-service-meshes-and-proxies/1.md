@0
This is the classic circuit breaker, and the reason it is here is that a mesh splits it in two. On screen, a checkout service, a breaker, and the payments API. Closed passes calls and counts failures. After 3 consecutive failures it opens and fails every call instantly. After 5 seconds it goes half-open and lets a single trial call decide.

@1
Call 1 succeeds in 40 milliseconds, and the failure count stays at zero.

@2-3
Payments starts timing out. Calls 2 and 3 each hold a checkout thread for the full 2 second timeout before failing. Failures 1 and 2 of 3. Under load, this is how one slow dependency drains a caller's whole thread pool.

@4
Call 4 times out as well, the third failure, and the breaker trips open.

@5
Call 5 is rejected by the breaker in under a millisecond, without touching payments. A slow failure has become a fast one.

@6
Over the next 5 seconds the breaker sheds calls 6 to 20, 15 more, the same way. Payments gets quiet time instead of a queue of retries, and checkout can serve a fallback.

@7-8
The timer elapses, and the breaker goes half-open. The single trial, call 21, times out, so the breaker opens again for another 5 seconds.

@9-10
Payments recovers. The breaker goes half-open again, the next trial, call 22, returns 200 OK, and the breaker closes and resets its count. Two probe calls in all, instead of 15 more timeouts.

@11
The pitfalls: a raw count trips on one flaky burst, so use a failure rate over a window; one breaker shared across endpoints lets a broken refund endpoint take down charges; and without a fallback the user still fails, just faster.

@12
Now map it onto a mesh. What Envoy calls circuit breakers are really concurrency caps, a bulkhead for the whole cluster: when 100 requests are already waiting for a connection, the next is rejected immediately. The behaviour you just watched, stop calling the thing that keeps failing and try again later, is outlier detection, applied per endpoint: after five consecutive server errors, the proxy ejects that one host and readmits it after a cooling-off period. Same idea, split between two settings with confusing names.
