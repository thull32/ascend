@0
On screen is a plain token bucket: at most 5 tokens, refilled at 1 per second, and each request takes one. A retry budget has exactly this shape, with two substitutions. Instead of the clock, successes refill the bucket, slowly. And instead of every request, it is retries that need permission. Watch the mechanics here, then map them onto retries.

@1-5
Five requests at time zero each take a token: 4 left, 3, 2, 1, none. A full bucket lets a burst through.

@6-7
Two more, in the same instant, find it empty and are refused.

@8-10
At 2 seconds, two tokens have refilled, and request 8 takes one. At 3 seconds, there are two again, and requests 9 and 10 take both.

@11
The cap is what matters: capacity sets the burst, the refill sets the sustained rate, and nothing beyond either gets through.

@12
Now the retry budget, as gRPC defines it. The bucket starts full, at 10 tokens. Every failed attempt removes 1, every success adds a tenth, and a retry is allowed only while the bucket holds more than half, 5. In a total outage, retrying stops after a handful of failures, and every call after that makes one attempt: amplification falls to exactly one times. When the server heals, it takes 51 successes to lift the bucket back above 5. And in a partial outage, the bucket drains whenever more than about 9 percent of attempts fail, so retries switch themselves off exactly where they stop helping and start hurting.
