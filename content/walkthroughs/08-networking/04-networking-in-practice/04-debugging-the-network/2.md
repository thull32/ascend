@0
In a packet capture, loss has a signature you can learn to spot at a glance: a run of duplicate acknowledgements all carrying the same number, then a retransmission with the original sequence number, then an acknowledgement that jumps forward. Here it is, packet by packet.

@1
Four segments leave the sender. The one at sequence 1001 is lost.

@2
Sequence 1 arrives, and the receiver acknowledges 1001.

@3-5
Sequences 2001 and 3001 arrive and are buffered behind the gap. The receiver answers with 1001, then 1001 again, then a third time. In a capture, that is three acknowledgements in a row with the same ack number, and with selective acknowledgement on, each carries a SACK block listing what is held beyond the hole.

@6
The sender retransmits sequence 1001, the original sequence number, and Wireshark flags it as a retransmission.

@7
The receiver's acknowledgement jumps to 4001, past everything it had buffered.

@8
Retransmissions, duplicate acknowledgements and SACK blocks together mean loss on the path, and the next question is how they correlate with load. Compare the other signatures in the lesson's table. A SYN repeated about a second apart means nothing is answering. A SYN answered by an immediate reset means nothing is listening. A zero window from the receiver means its application is not reading. And a request acknowledged at once with a response hundreds of milliseconds later is server think time: the network is innocent. In Wireshark, the filter for this one is tcp dot analysis dot retransmission.
