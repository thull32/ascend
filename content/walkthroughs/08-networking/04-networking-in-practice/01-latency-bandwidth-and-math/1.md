@0
For short requests, bandwidth hardly matters. What dominates is how many times a packet has to cross the distance and come back before useful data flows. Here is the first of those crossings: the TCP handshake, between a closed client and a listening server.

@1
The client sends a SYN with its initial sequence number, 100.

@2
The server answers with a SYN-ACK: acknowledging 101, and offering its own number, 300.

@3
The client acknowledges 301 and is established. It may put its request in this very packet, but a full round trip has already passed.

@4
And the server cannot send its first byte of response before that request reaches it, so the earliest reply arrives two round trips after the client started. Count the whole cold request in the lesson: a DNS lookup, this handshake, TLS, then the request itself, four round trips. At 70 milliseconds each, that is 280 milliseconds of floor before a server's 20 millisecond response time even starts to matter. State latency as round trips first and milliseconds second: three round trips is a fact about the protocol, 210 milliseconds depends on where the user is sitting.
