@0
No router tells a TCP sender how fast it may go, so the sender keeps its own guess: the congestion window, the number of segments it believes the path can hold in flight. The chart will plot that window once per round trip. For readability this picture starts at one segment; real stacks start at ten. The slow start threshold here is 16. Below it the window doubles every round trip, and above it, it grows by one.

@1-2
Round trip 1: the sender sends one segment. The ACK comes back, and the window grows by one segment for every acknowledged segment, so it doubles to 2.

@3-6
Round trip 2 sends two segments, and both acknowledgements bring the window to 4. Round trip 3 sends four, and the window becomes 8. A full window of ACKs always doubles it. Slow start is not slow at all: it is exponential. The name only contrasts it with the old behaviour of blasting a whole receive window at once.

@7-8
Round trip 4 sends 8 segments, and the window doubles to 16, which reaches the threshold.

@9-10
From here the sender is in congestion avoidance. Round trip 5 sends 16 segments, the picture shows 8 of them, and when they are all acknowledged the window grows by just one, to 17.

@11-16
And it keeps going like that: 17 segments, then 18, then 19, one more each round trip. The chart bends from a steep doubling curve into a gentle straight line. That is additive increase, the cautious probe for spare capacity once the sender is near what it believes the path can hold.

@17
This flow never saw a loss, so the window only grew: doubling up to the threshold of 16, then one segment per round trip, ending at 20. The first loss would halve it, and the next visualisation shows that. Two numbers from the lesson make the doubling matter. Filling a 1 gigabit per second path with a 70 millisecond round trip takes about ten round trips of slow start, roughly 700 milliseconds, and moves about 15 megabytes on the way. Almost every API response finishes before the connection ever runs at link speed, which is why round trips, not bandwidth, decide most latencies.
