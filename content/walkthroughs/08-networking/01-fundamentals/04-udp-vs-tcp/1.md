@0
A sender and a receiver, and nothing between them that remembers anything. UDP adds eight bytes to an IP packet: two ports, a length and a checksum. There is no handshake, no acknowledgement and no sequence number, so every datagram is independent of every other.

@1
The sender fires four datagrams back to back, with zero setup cost: the very first packet already carries data. Datagram 3 is lost on the way.

@2
The receiver gets 1, 2 and 4, and nothing at the transport layer notices the gap. The receiver has no sequence numbers to spot it, and the sender gets no acknowledgement to miss, so nobody will resend datagram 3, and nothing guarantees the order of the ones that did arrive. Whether that matters is the application's decision. For a voice call, a lost packet is better than a late one. For a file transfer, the application has to build its own retries. That silence is also why a UDP collector can drop most of its traffic when its socket buffer fills while every send call still reports success.
