@0
Once connected, TCP numbers every byte, not every packet. Acknowledgements are cumulative: an ACK of N means I have everything before byte N. Here each segment carries 1,000 bytes, and the receiver advertises a window of 4,000 bytes, which caps how much the sender may have in flight without an acknowledgement.

@1
The sender does not wait after each segment. It pipelines four of them, starting at sequence 1, 1001, 2001 and 3001. That is 4,000 bytes in flight, exactly the receiver's window.

@2
The receiver answers with one cumulative ACK, 4001: every byte below 4001 arrived, in order. With delayed acknowledgements, a receiver often coalesces several segments into a single ACK like this one.

@3
All 4,000 bytes are acknowledged, and the window is free again. Here is the formula to remember: throughput is at most the window divided by the round trip time. With a 64 kilobyte window and a 100 millisecond round trip, you get at most 640 kilobytes a second, no matter how fast the link is. That ceiling is why TCP has window scaling, and the real limit is the smaller of the receive window and the congestion window.
