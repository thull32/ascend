@0
Routers spread flows across a pool of L4 balancer machines, and a change in that pool can send the next packet of an established connection to a machine that has never seen it. That machine must still pick the same backend, without asking anyone. The answer is consistent hashing: every balancer computes the backend from the same hash, so they agree without coordination. Here, four backends sit evenly around a ring: N1 at 47 degrees, N2 at 137, N3 at 227 and N4 at 317. A flow, identified by its client address and port, belongs to the first backend at or after its position, walking clockwise.

@1
The first flow, from 10.1.4.7 port 51220, hashes to 190 degrees. The next backend clockwise is N3.

@2-3
The next flow lands at 264 degrees and goes to N4. The one after lands at 224, just before N3, so it goes to N3.

@4
The fourth flow comes from the same client as the first, on port 51221 instead of 51220, and lands somewhere else entirely, at 70 degrees: it belongs to N2. It is the whole flow that is hashed, not just the client.

@5-6
The last two flows land at 248 and 255 degrees, and both walk on to N4.

@7
All six flows are placed: none on N1, one on N2, two on N3 and three on N4. Six hashed flows on four evenly spaced backends still come out uneven, which is the imbalance real systems smooth out with many virtual positions per backend.

@8
Now a fifth backend, N5, joins at 251 degrees, between N3 at 227 and N4 at 317. Only the flows on that arc change owner: the flow at 248 moves from N4 to N5. That is 1 of 6, and no flow moves between the old backends, so the other five connections keep their backend. With hash mod N, going from four backends to five would have moved 5 of these 6 flows, and nearly every established connection would break.

@9
Google's Maglev uses the same idea with a lookup table instead of a ring, sized to a prime well over 100 times the number of backends so shares differ by at most 1 percent. Each machine also keeps a local connection table, so flows that were already established keep their backend even while the table changes.
