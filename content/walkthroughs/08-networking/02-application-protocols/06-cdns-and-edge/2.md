@0
Inside one point of presence there are many cache servers. If requests were spread randomly, every popular object would end up on every server, and the PoP would hold one server's worth of distinct content. Instead, the front layer hashes the cache key to the one server responsible for it. Servers and keys share a circle of positions from 0 to 359 degrees, and a key belongs to the first server at or after its position, walking clockwise. Four servers sit evenly around this ring: N1 at 33 degrees, N2 at 123, N3 at 213 and N4 at 303.

@1
The trailer page hashes to 131 degrees, just past N2, so it walks on to N3, at 213.

@2
The poster image lands at 16 degrees. Walking clockwise, the first server is N1, at 33.

@3-4
The JavaScript bundle lands at 136 and the API page at 167. Both walk on to N3 as well.

@5
The video segment lands at 120 degrees, just before N2, so N2 owns it.

@6
The stylesheet lands at 242 and goes to N4, at 303.

@7
All six keys are placed: one each on N1, N2 and N4, and three on N3. Six keys on four evenly spaced servers still come out uneven, because the keys themselves land where their hashes put them. Each server owns the arc that ends at its own position.

@8
Now add a fifth server, N5, which joins at 133 degrees, between N2 at 123 and N3 at 213. It claims only that arc. The trailer page, at 131, moves from N3 to N5, and that is the only key that moves: 1 of 6. No key moves between the old servers, so every other server keeps its cache warm. With hash mod N, going from four servers to five would have remapped 5 of these 6 keys, and the whole PoP would go cold at once.

@9
The ideal is that a newcomer takes its fair share, about a fifth of the keys, and nothing else moves. With one point per server, how much it takes depends on where it lands, which is why real systems give each physical server around 100 to 200 virtual positions on the ring: the arcs even out, a new server takes a little from everyone, and a failed server's keys spread across many neighbours. The price of one owner per key is a hot key: one viral object lands on one server, which is why CDNs also keep extra copies and coalesce requests for it.
