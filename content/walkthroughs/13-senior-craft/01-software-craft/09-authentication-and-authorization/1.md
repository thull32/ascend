@0
The question is a Zanzibar check: may Bo view the roadmap document? Each node on screen is an object and a relation, and each edge is either a rewrite rule or a stored tuple. A document's viewers include its editors and its parent folder's viewers; its editors include its owner; Ana owns the document; the folder's viewers include the members of the platform group; and Bo is a member of that group. The check succeeds if a search starting from the document's viewer relation reaches Bo. The search here is breadth-first, with a first-in, first-out queue, so nodes are explored in order of distance.

@1-3
Dequeue the document's viewer relation, at distance 0. It has two edges: to the document's editors, and to the parent folder's viewers. Both are discovered at distance 1 and enqueued.

@4-5
Dequeue the document's editors. Editors include the owner, so the owner relation is discovered at distance 2.

@6-7
Dequeue the folder's viewers. Its stored tuple names a userset, the platform group's members, discovered at distance 2.

@8-9
Dequeue the document's owner. It leads to Ana, at distance 3. That branch is the dead end of the trace: Ana is not the user we asked about.

@10-11
Dequeue the platform group's members. It leads to Bo, at distance 3. That discovery is the answer: Bo is reachable, so the check is allowed, via the roadmap, the engineering folder and the platform group.

@12-13
The breadth-first search drains the rest of its queue, Ana and then Bo, and stops.

@14
Every node is dequeued once and every edge examined once, so the work is linear in nodes plus edges, and the labels are shortest-path lengths. In production each step is an indexed read, branches run in parallel, and subproblems are cached. The subtle part is time: check against a stale replica after Ana removes Bo, and Bo can still read what she adds next. That is the "new enemy" problem, and it is why a check runs at a snapshot at least as new as the content it guards.
