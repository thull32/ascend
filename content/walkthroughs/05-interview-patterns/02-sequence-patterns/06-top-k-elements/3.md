@0
Last Stone Weight: smash the two heaviest stones together, and if they differ, the difference goes back as a new stone. Repeatedly take the biggest is the signal for the greedy engine, a max-heap. In the array, every parent is at least as large as its children, so the heaviest stone is always at index 0.

@1-5
The stones go in one at a time. 6 becomes the root and 3 its left child, which stays put. Then 9 is appended on the right, and because 9 is larger than its parent 6, it sifts up and swaps. 9 is the root.

@6-10
2 goes in under 3 and stays. 5 goes in under 3 as well, beats it, and swaps up, but stops under 9. The array is now 9, 5, 6, 2, 3.

@11-13
Round one. Pop the heaviest, 9. The last leaf, 3, moves into the root to keep the tree complete, then sinks: its larger child is 6, so they swap.

@14-16
Pop again: 6. The last leaf, 2, moves to the root and sinks below 5. The two heaviest were 9 and 6.

@17-19
Their difference, 3, goes back in. It is appended under 2, beats it, swaps up, and stops under 5. The heap is 5, 3, 3, 2.

@20-24
Round two pops 5, with 2 sinking below a 3, and then 3. The pops always lift the next heaviest to the root.

@25-26
5 minus 3 is 2, pushed back. The heap holds 3, 2 and 2.

@27-30
Round three pops 3 and then 2.

@31-32
Their difference, 1, goes back in, under the remaining 2.

@33-35
Round four pops 2, then 1, and the heap is empty for a moment.

@36
2 minus 1 is 1, and that last push is the only stone left. The answer is 1.

@37
The popped order was 9, 6, 5, 3, 3, 2, 2 and 1. Each push and pop touches one root to leaf path, so it costs log n. And each round removes at least one stone, two out and at most one back, so there are at most n minus 1 rounds. The whole simulation is n log n.
