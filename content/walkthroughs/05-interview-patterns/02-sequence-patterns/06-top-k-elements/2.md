@0
The greedy engine starts with every candidate in a heap, and the fast way to build that heap is not to push them one by one. Take the array as it is, here 3, 9, 2, 7, 5, 8 and 1, and fix it from the bottom up. Leaves are already valid heaps, so the work starts at the last internal node, index 2, and moves back towards the root, sifting each node down.

@1-3
Index 2 holds 2. Both of its subtrees are already heaps, so one sift down is enough to make this subtree a heap. Its children are 8 and 1. The larger, 8, beats 2, so they swap, and 2 lands on a leaf and stops.

@4-5
Index 1 holds 9. Its children are 7 and 5, and 9 is already larger than both, so nothing moves.

@6-7
Now the root, 3. Its children are 9 and 8, the larger is 9, and it beats 3: swap. 9 is now the root.

@8-9
3 keeps sinking. At index 1 its children are 7 and 5. 7 is larger and beats 3, so they swap, and 3 stops at a leaf.

@10
The array is a max-heap, with 9 at the root. Here is why the build is linear and not n log n. Most nodes sit near the leaves and sift only a level or two; only the root can travel the full height. So the total sift work is a geometric series bounded by about 2n. For the greedy engine that means building the heap of all candidates costs linear time, before the first pop.
