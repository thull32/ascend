@0
Two sorted lists: A is 1, 3, 5, 7, and B is 2, 4, 6. Because both are sorted, the smallest value not yet merged is always one of the two heads. So the loop is: compare the heads, splice the smaller onto the merged list's tail, and advance that list. This is the merge primitive with take A meaning A's head is less than or equal to B's.

@1
One is at most 2: take 1 from A and advance A, which now points at 3.

@2
Three is more than 2: take 2 from B. B now points at 4.

@3-6
The two lists keep alternating. Three is at most 4, take 3. Five is more than 4, take 4. Five is at most 6, take 5. Seven is more than 6, take 6. Six comparisons, and the merged list reads 1 through 6.

@7
B is exhausted. Whatever is left in A is already sorted and larger than everything merged, so it is attached with one pointer assignment, the tail's next set to whichever list remains. Here that is just 7, with no comparison.

@8
Seven nodes merged with six comparisons: linear in the total length. Done in place, it allocates nothing and only rewrites next pointers. The less than or equal rule matters when the heads tie: taking from A first keeps equal values in their original order, which makes the merge stable. Change the rule to alternate between the lists and the same loop is the interleave step of Reorder List.
