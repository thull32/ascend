@0
Before k lists, two. List A is 1, 4, 5 and list B is 1, 3, 4. Both are sorted, so the smallest value not yet merged is always one of the two heads. Compare the heads, take the smaller, advance that list. That one rule is the merge step of merge sort, and the k-way merge is this rule with a heap doing the comparing.

@1
Both heads are 1. On a tie, take from A, which keeps the merge stable: equal values come out in their original order. A's 1 goes first, and A's head moves to 4.

@2
Now it is 4 against B's 1. B's 1 is smaller, so it is taken, and B's head moves to 3.

@3
4 against 3: B's 3 is taken. B's head is now 4.

@4
4 against 4, another tie, so A's 4 goes first. A's head moves to 5.

@5
5 against B's 4: B's 4 is taken, and that empties list B.

@6
With one list exhausted there is nothing left to compare. The rest of A, just the 5, is already sorted, so it is attached to the tail in one pointer assignment.

@7
Six nodes merged with 5 comparisons, in time linear in the two lengths. Done in place, the merge allocates nothing and only rewrites next pointers. With k inputs, the question at every step becomes which of k heads is smallest, and that is what a min-heap answers in log k time instead of k comparisons.
