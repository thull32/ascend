@0
Kth Largest in a Stream is the same boundary problem as the running median, placed 3 from the top instead of in the middle. The upper side is the 3 largest, kept in a min-heap whose root is the answer. The lower side is everything else, and here is the question that matters: does it need to be stored at all?

@1-5
The first three values, 6, 1 and 9, fill the heap. 1 is smaller than its parent 6, so it sifts up to the root. 9 stays where it lands. The root, 1, is the third largest so far.

@6-7
3 arrives and beats the root 1, so 1 leaves the top three. 3 overwrites the root and is already smaller than both its children, 6 and 9.

@8-10
11 beats the root 3. It overwrites the root and sinks below the smaller child, 6. The heap holds 6, 11 and 9, and the answer is 6.

@11
4 is compared with the root 6, and rejected in one comparison.

@12-14
12 beats 6. It overwrites the root and sinks below the smaller child, 9. The answer becomes 9.

@15
7 is not larger than 9: rejected.

@16-17
10 beats 9, takes the root, and is already smaller than its children 11 and 12. The answer is 10.

@18
The heap holds 12, 11 and 10. Look at what was thrown away along the way: 1, 3, 6, 4, 9 and 7. None of them was ever needed again, because in an insert-only stream the boundary only moves up, so nothing below it can return. That is why the lower heap disappears and two heaps become one. Each value costs log k, with only k values of memory. Add deletions and the argument fails: delete 12, and the new third largest is 9, which was already evicted. Then you are back to two heaps.
