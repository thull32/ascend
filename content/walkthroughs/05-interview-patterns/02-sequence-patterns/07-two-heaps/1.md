@0
A stream of numbers arrives, 5, 15, 1, 3, 8 and 7, and after every one we want the median. The stream is split into two halves. Low is a max-heap holding the smaller half, so its root is the largest of the small values. High is a min-heap holding the larger half, so its root is the smallest of the large values. Two invariants: every value in low is at most every value in high, and low holds the same number as high, or one more. Keep both, and the median is always sitting at a root.

@1-3
5 arrives. Every value goes into low first. Then low's largest, which is 5 itself, crosses to high. That one move is what guarantees the order: whatever crosses is the biggest thing low had. Now high has more than low, so high's smallest, 5 again, comes back.

@4
Low has one extra element, so the median is low's root: 5.

@5-6
15 goes into low, and low's largest, 15, crosses to high. Low holds 5, high holds 15. The sizes are equal, so no rebalance.

@7
With equal sizes, the median is the average of the two roots: 5 and 15 give 10.

@8-10
1 goes into low next to 5. Low's largest is 5, so 5 crosses to high, which now holds 5 and 15. High is bigger, so its smallest, 5, comes straight back. Notice that 5 went over and came back. The template never compares the new value with anything; the cross and the rebalance alone put every value on the right side.

@11
Low holds 1 and 5, high holds 15. The median is low's root, 5.

@12-13
3 goes into low. Low's largest is 5, and it crosses. Now low holds 1 and 3, high holds 5 and 15.

@14
Two each: the median is the average of 3 and 5, which is 4.

@15-17
8 goes into low, which makes 8 low's largest, so 8 crosses to high. High now has three, 5, 8 and 15, against low's two, so high's smallest, 5, moves back to low.

@18
Low holds 1, 3 and 5. The median is 5.

@19-20
Finally 7. It enters low, is immediately the largest there, and crosses to high, which now holds 7, 8 and 15.

@21
Three each: the median is the average of 5 and 7, which is 6. Sorted, the six values are 1, 3, 5, 7, 8 and 15, and the middle pair is indeed 5 and 7.

@22
Each value makes at most three moves: into low, across to high, and back. Each move costs log n, so an insert is log n, and the median is read from the roots in constant time. Re-sorting after every arrival would cost n log n per sample. The step with 8 shows why the unconditional sequence earns its keep: 8 belonged in high, and it got there because pushing it into low made it low's maximum. Routing each value by comparing it with low's root is also correct and does fewer heap operations, but it needs a guard for an empty low.
