@0
The count signature from Group Anagrams, put to a second use. The pattern is a b c, and its signature is one a, one b and one c. Now slide a window of three characters across the text c b a e b a b a c d. A window is an anagram of the pattern exactly when its counts equal the signature, whatever order the letters are in.

@1-3
Fill the first window one character at a time: c, b, a. Its counts are one of each, the same signature, so c b a at position 0 is an anagram.

@4
Slide: one decrement for the c that leaves, one increment for the e that enters. The c and e counts are now off, so no match.

@5-8
Each slide is the same two updates. b leaves and b enters, then a leaves and a enters: the e is still inside, so neither matches. Then the e leaves and a b enters, giving two b's and no c, and the next slide gives two a's and still no c.

@9
a leaves and c enters: the window is b a c, one of each again. A second anagram, at position 6.

@10
b leaves and d enters. The d is not in the pattern at all, so no match.

@11
Anagrams at positions 0 and 6, found in one pass. Each character is counted once on the way in and once on the way out, so the whole scan is linear in the text, with a count table the size of the alphabet. It is the grouping key and the sliding window working together: a frequency map that you update incrementally instead of rebuilding for every window.
