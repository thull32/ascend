@0
The input is a brace, a square bracket, a parenthesis, their three closers, and then an open parenthesis followed by a closing square bracket. We scan left to right once. Every opener is pushed; every closer must match the top of the stack, or the answer is no. The invariant: the stack holds exactly the opened but not yet closed brackets, most recent on top.

@1-3
The brace, the square bracket and the parenthesis are all openers, so all three are pushed. Depth 3, with the parenthesis on top: it was opened last, so it must be closed first.

@4-6
Now the closers. The closing parenthesis matches the top, so pop it. The closing square bracket matches the new top, pop. The closing brace matches the brace, pop. Depth 0: the first six characters are balanced on their own.

@7
Another open parenthesis: push it. Depth 1.

@8
A closing square bracket, but the top of the stack is an open parenthesis. The most recent opener must be the first to close, so this is a mismatch, and the scan stops right here with the answer not balanced. No lookahead is needed, and nothing after this point could repair it. That is why one pass is enough.

There are three ways to fail, and all three need a line of code: a closer that does not match the top, as here; a closer arriving when the stack is empty, which must be checked before popping; and input that ends with openers still on the stack. Return whether the stack is empty, not merely whether a mismatch was seen.
