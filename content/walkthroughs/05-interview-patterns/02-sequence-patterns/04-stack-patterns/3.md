@0
This is Evaluate Reverse Polish Notation, shown as nothing but stack operations. The tokens are 7, minus 3, divide, 2, multiply, 10, plus, which means 7 divided by minus 3, times 2, plus 10. The rule fits in one sentence: operands are pushed, and each operator pops two values, the right operand first and then the left, and pushes its result.

@1-2
7 is pushed. Then minus 3 is pushed, and this is the first trap: minus 3 is an operand, not the minus operator. The test for an operator has to compare the whole token, never just its first character. Two values are now waiting.

@3-4
The divide sign arrives. The first pop gives minus 3, the right operand. The second pop gives 7, the left operand. Popping in the other order would compute minus 3 divided by 7. The stack is empty for a moment.

@5
The quotient goes back on: minus 2. That is the second trap. The problem says division truncates towards zero, so 7 over minus 3 is minus 2. Python's floor division would give minus 3 here and carry the wrong value through the rest of the expression.

@6
2 is pushed on top of it.

@7-8
Multiply pops 2 as the right operand, then minus 2 as the left.

@9
And pushes their product, minus 4.

@10
10 is pushed.

@11-12
Plus pops 10, then minus 4.

@13
And pushes the sum, 6. That is the answer. With floor division the same expression would have ended at 4.

@14
Every step touched only the top of the stack, so each token costs constant time and the whole evaluation is linear. The stack depth at any moment is the number of values waiting for an operator, and a well formed expression leaves exactly one. When you write this in an interview, say the two traps out loud: pop the right operand first, and truncate towards zero rather than flooring.
