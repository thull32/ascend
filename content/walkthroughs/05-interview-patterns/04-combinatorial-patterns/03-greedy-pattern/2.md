@0
Make 6 with coins of 1, 3 and 4, using as few coins as possible. Largest-coin-first takes the 4, which leaves 2, and 2 costs two 1 coins: three coins in total. The table does not guess. The best for amount a is 1 plus the best of the amounts one coin below it, filled from 0 upwards.

@1-3
Amount 0 needs no coins. Amount 1 is a single 1 coin. Amount 2 can only be built from 1 coins, so it costs two. That cell is exactly what the greedy pays for after taking the 4.

@4-5
Amount 3 is one coin, the 3, and amount 4 is one coin, the 4.

@6
Amount 5 costs two coins: a 1 on top of the 4.

@7
Now amount 6. It can reach back to 5, worth two coins, to 3, worth one, or to 2, worth two. The 3 wins, so 6 costs two coins: a 3 on top of 3.

@8
Two coins, 3 plus 3, where the greedy needed three. On random three-coin systems that include a 1, largest-coin-first failed on 291 of 2,000 tries, and a two-minute random tester finds such failures in seconds. When a greedy rule has no argument behind it, fall back to the table, at the cost of the amount times the number of coins.
