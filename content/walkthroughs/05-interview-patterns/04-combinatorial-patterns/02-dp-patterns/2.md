@0
The longest common subsequence of abcde and ace. Rows are prefixes of abcde, columns are prefixes of ace, and each cell holds the answer for that pair of prefixes. Two rules fill the grid. If the last characters match, extend the diagonal: one plus the cell up and to the left. If they do not, drop one character from either string and take the larger of the cell above and the cell to the left.

@1
An empty prefix shares nothing with anything, so the first row and the first column are all 0.

@2-4
Row a. The a matches the a of ace, so that cell extends the diagonal: 0 plus 1 is 1. The next two cells are mismatches, a against c and a against e, and they copy the 1 from their left.

@5-7
Row b. B matches nothing in ace, so every cell copies the larger neighbour, and the whole row stays at 1.

@8-10
Row c. Against a it is a mismatch, and copies 1. Against c it matches, so it extends the diagonal from 1 to 2: a and c are now a common subsequence. Against e it copies the 2 from its left.

@11-13
Row d matches nothing either, and simply carries the 1, 2 and 2 down from the row above.

@14-16
Row e. Against a and c, it copies 1 and 2. Against e it matches, and extends the diagonal from 2 to 3.

@17
The answer is 3, and the subsequence is ace. To recover it, walk back from the bottom-right cell: a match is a diagonal step that emits its character, and a mismatch moves toward the larger neighbour. The cost is the product of the two lengths in time, and since each row reads only the row above, two rows are enough for the length. Edit distance, distinct subsequences and interleaving string are this same grid with different choices per cell.
