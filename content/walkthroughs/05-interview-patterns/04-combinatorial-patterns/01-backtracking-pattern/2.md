@0
Choose 3 of the numbers 1 to 5. Two rules shape the tree. Pick in increasing index order, so each combination appears once and never as a reordering. And prune: a branch that cannot still collect 3 items is never explored.

@1-3
The root needs 3 more and may pick from all five. Pick 1; now it needs 2 more, from 2 onward. Pick 2; it needs 1 more, from 3 onward.

@4-7
Each remaining candidate completes a combination: 1, 2, 3, then 1, 2, 4, then 1, 2, 5. Every candidate after 2 has been tried, so return.

@8-11
Back at 1, pick 3 next. One more is needed, from 4 onward: record 1, 3, 4 and 1, 3, 5.

@12-14
Pick 4: only 5 is left after it, which is exactly enough, giving 1, 4, 5. Combination number 6.

@15
Now the prune. Picking 5 as the second item would leave nothing after it, but one more item is still needed. This branch can never fill up, so it is cut before it is entered.

@16-26
Everything starting with 1 is done. Starting with 2, the same pattern gives 2, 3, 4, then 2, 3, 5, then 2, 4, 5, and picking 5 second is pruned again.

@27-32
Starting with 3, only one combination fits: 3, 4, 5. Combination number 10. Picking 5 second is pruned once more.

@33-34
Back at the root, starting with 4 would leave only one element after it when two are needed, and starting with 5 would leave none. Both are pruned without a single call.

@35-36
All 10 combinations, which is 5 choose 3, from 20 calls; the pruned branches were never called. The prune is one bound on the loop: stop where there are not enough elements left to fill the remaining slots. Without it, the search wanders into dead branches; with it, the cost is close to the number of answers, instead of all 2 to the 5 subsets.
