@0
This is the input from Subarray Sum Equals K: 3, 4, 7, 2, minus 3, 1, 4 and 2, and we want to count the subarrays that sum to 7. Below it, the prefix row starts with P of 0, which is 0: the empty prefix, already seen once. The idea to hold onto: two prefixes that differ by 7 bracket a subarray that sums to 7. So for each new prefix, look up the prefix minus 7 among the earlier ones, and only then record the new one.

@1
The first prefix is 3. Three minus 7 is minus 4, which no earlier prefix has. The count stays 0.

@2
3 plus 4 gives 7. Seven minus 7 is 0, and P of 0 is exactly 0: the seed. So the first two elements, 3 and 4, sum to 7. The count is 1. Without that seed, a subarray starting at index 0 would never be counted.

@3
7 plus 7 gives 14. Fourteen minus 7 is 7, which is P of 2, so the single element 7 is another. The count is 2.

@4-5
Adding 2 gives 16, and 16 minus 7 is 9: not seen. Then the minus 3 pulls the total down to 13, and 13 minus 7 is 6: not seen either. The count stays 2.

@6
The 1 brings the total back to 14. That is the step a sliding window can never take: because of the negative, a prefix repeats. Fourteen minus 7 is again 7, P of 2, so 7, 2, minus 3 and 1 sum to 7 as well. The count is 3. And since 14 has now been seen twice, the map stores a count for it, not just a yes.

@7-8
Adding 4 gives 18, and 11 is not there. The last 2 gives 20, and 20 minus 7 is 13, which is P of 5. So the final three elements, 1, 4 and 2, are the fourth subarray.

@9
Four subarrays: 3 and 4, the lone 7, 7 through 1, and 1 through 2. All found in one left-to-right pass, one lookup and one insert per element, so linear time. Two details carry the whole pattern: the seed for the empty prefix, and doing the lookup before the insert, so a prefix never pairs with itself.
