@0
This is the input from Subarray Sum Equals K: 3, 4, 7, 2, minus 3, 1, 4 and 2, and we want to count the subarrays that sum to 7. Below it, a second row will fill with prefix sums, each one the total of everything up to and including that position. The idea to hold onto: two prefixes that differ by 7 bracket a subarray that sums to 7.

@1-3
The prefix row grows by adding one element to the previous total. Before the array starts the total is 0, so the first prefix is 3. Then 3 plus 4 is 7, and 7 plus 7 is 14. Already two hits. The prefix 7 is exactly 7 more than the empty start, 0, so the first two elements, 3 and 4, sum to 7. And 14 is exactly 7 more than the prefix 7, so the single element 7 is another.

@4-6
Two more makes 16. Then the minus 3 pulls the total down to 13, and the 1 brings it back to 14. That 14 is the step a sliding window can never take: because of the negative, a prefix repeats. The second 14 is again 7 more than the prefix 7, so 7, 2, minus 3 and 1 sum to 7 as well. That is why the counting version stores how many times each prefix has been seen, not just whether it has.

@7-8
Four more gives 18, and the last 2 gives 20. Twenty is 7 more than 13, so the final three elements, 1, 4 and 2, are the fourth subarray. Four in total, all found in a single left-to-right pass.

@9
With the row built, any range sum is one subtraction. The sum of positions 1 to 6 is the prefix at 6, which is 18, minus the prefix at 0, which is 3: that gives 15, in constant time, however long the range. Build once in linear time, answer every query in constant time. And when the question asks how many ranges hit a target, turn the subtraction around: for each prefix, look up how many earlier prefixes equal it minus the target.
