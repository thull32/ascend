@0
Eight values: 2, 1, 5, 1, 3, 2, 7 and 1, and a window that is always exactly three wide. We want the largest sum of any three consecutive values. The first window, 2, 1 and 5, sums to 8. The invariant for the fixed shape: after each slide, the running sum is the sum of exactly the three elements ending at the right edge.

@1-3
Each slide admits one element and evicts one. Admit 1, evict 2: the sum is 7. Admit 3, evict 1: 9, the new best. Admit 2, evict 5: 6.

@4-5
Admit 7, evict 1: 12, the best yet, for the window 3, 2 and 7. Admit the last 1, evict 3: 10.

@6
The best window sums to 12 and starts at position 4. Every slide was one addition and one subtraction, so the scan is linear, instead of re-adding three elements for every window. The bug to avoid is evicting at the wrong moment: admit first, then evict the element that is now three positions behind, so the window never holds four elements when you read its sum.
