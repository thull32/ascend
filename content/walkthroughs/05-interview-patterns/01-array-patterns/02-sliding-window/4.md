@0
The task is the maximum of every window of 3 consecutive values in 1, 3, minus 1, minus 3, 5, 3, 6 and 7. The window itself is trivial; the interesting part is the state, a deque of indices whose values decrease from front to back. Hold onto one sentence: the front of the deque is always the maximum of the current window.

@1-3
Index 0, value 1, is pushed at the back. Then 3 arrives. Three is at least as large as the 1 at the back, so the 1 is evicted: while 3 is in the same window, 1 can never be the maximum. The deque now holds just the 3, at index 1.

@4-5
Minus 1 is smaller than 3, so it is pushed behind it, and the deque reads 3, minus 1, still decreasing. The first window, positions 0 to 2, is now complete, and its maximum is simply the front: 3.

@6-7
Minus 3 joins at the back, giving 3, minus 1, minus 3. The window slides to positions 1 to 3, the front is still 3, and 3 is recorded again.

@8-11
Now 5 arrives, and the evictions cascade from the back. Five beats minus 3, so minus 3 goes. Five beats minus 1, so minus 1 goes. Five beats 3, so 3 goes too. None of them could ever be a window maximum while 5 is around. The deque holds 5 alone.

@12
Window 2 to 4 is complete, and its maximum is the front, 5.

@13-14
Three is smaller than 5, so it waits behind it. Window 3 to 5 records 5 again.

@15-17
Six arrives and evicts the 3, then the 5, and is pushed alone.

@18
Window 4 to 6 records 6.

@19-21
Seven evicts the 6, takes the deque alone, and window 5 to 7 records 7.

@22
The result is 3, 3, 5, 5, 6 and 7. The second rule, evicting from the front an index that has slid out of the window, never fired on this input: every value was evicted from the back by a larger newcomer before it could age out. Either way, each index is pushed once and popped at most once, so the whole pass is linear, not n times k. The mechanics are the monotonic stack's, with that one extra move at the front.
