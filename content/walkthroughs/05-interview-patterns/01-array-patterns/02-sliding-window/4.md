@0
The task is the maximum of every window of 3 consecutive values in 4, 2, 12, 3, 8, 5, 1 and 6. The window itself is trivial; the interesting part is the state, a deque of indices whose values decrease from front to back. Hold onto one sentence: the front of the deque is always the maximum of the current window.

@1-2
Index 0, value 4, is pushed at the back. Then 2, smaller, is pushed behind it.

@3-5
Now 12 arrives, and the evictions cascade from the back. Twelve beats 2, so 2 goes. Twelve beats 4, so 4 goes. Neither could ever be a window maximum while 12 is around. The deque holds 12 alone.

@6
The first window, positions 0 to 2, is complete, and its maximum is the front: 12.

@7-8
Three is smaller than 12, so it waits behind it. Window 1 to 3 records 12 again.

@9-11
Eight evicts the 3 from the back and is pushed behind 12. Window 2 to 4 records 12 a third time.

@12-14
Five joins the back: 12, 8, 5. But the window has slid to positions 3 to 5, and index 2, the 12, is no longer in it. Nothing larger ever arrived to evict it; it aged out, so it leaves from the front. The new front, 8, is the maximum of 3, 8 and 5.

@15-16
One joins the back, and window 4 to 6 records 8.

@17-19
Six evicts the 1, then the 5, and is pushed behind 8.

@20-21
The window is now 5 to 7, and 8, at index 4, has slid out, so it too leaves from the front. Window 5 to 7 records 6.

@22
The result is 12, 12, 12, 8, 8 and 6. Two rules kept the front honest: a newcomer evicts smaller values from the back, and an index that slides out of the window leaves from the front. Each index is pushed once and popped at most once, so the whole pass is linear, not n times k. The mechanics are the monotonic stack's, with that one extra move at the front.
