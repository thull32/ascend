@0
Nine values, each a 0, a 1 or a 2: the Sort Colors problem. Three pointers split the array into four regions. Everything before low is a 0, everything from low up to mid is a 1, everything after high is a 2, and the region from mid to high is still unknown. Each step examines the value under mid and shrinks the unknown region by one.

@1
Mid finds a 2 at position 0. It swaps with the value under high, at the far end, and high moves left: the last position now holds a 2. Mid does not move, because the 1 that just arrived has not been examined yet.

@2
And it is a 1, already in the middle region, so mid simply advances.

@3
At position 1, mid finds a 0. It swaps with the 1 under low. That 1 was already examined, so both low and mid advance. The array now begins 0, 1.

@4-5
At position 2, mid finds a 2 and swaps it with the value under high, which is another 2, so high moves left and mid waits. The new arrival is a 2 again, so it swaps once more, this time bringing a 0 in from position 6, and high moves left again. The last three positions are now 2s.

@6
That 0 swaps down to low, past the 1, and both pointers advance. The array begins 0, 0, 1.

@7-8
The next two values are 1s, so mid advances twice.

@9
At position 5, mid finds the last 0. It swaps with the first 1, at position 2, so the 0s grow to three and the band of 1s shifts right by one. Mid passes high, and nothing unknown is left.

@10
Three 0s, three 1s, three 2s, in one pass with constant extra space. The asymmetry is the whole bug surface: a value swapped in from low has already been seen, so mid advances; a value swapped in from high is a stranger, so mid waits. Advance mid after the high swap, and the 0 that arrived at step five would have been skipped and left among the 1s.
