@0
Eight sorted values: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 11 and 15, and we want two that add up to 10. One pointer starts at each end. The invariant to say out loud: if a matching pair exists, both of its indices lie between the two pointers. Every move must keep that true while it shrinks the range.

@1
The ends sum to 1 plus 15, which is 16, too big. And 15 is too big with anything: the smallest partner still in play is 1, and even that overshoots. So every pair that uses 15 is ruled out at once, and the right pointer moves left.

@2
1 plus 11 is 12, still too big, by the same argument. The 11 is dropped, along with every pair it belongs to.

@3
1 plus 8 is 9, too small this time. Now the 1 is useless: its largest possible partner is 8, and that falls short. So the left pointer moves right.

@4
2 plus 8 is 10. Found, at positions 1 and 5, after four sums. A brute force would have checked up to 28 pairs. Each move here discarded a whole row or column of that pair table in one step, and the gap between the pointers shrinks by exactly one per move, so the loop runs at most n minus 1 times. The whole trick depends on sorted input: without order, a sum that is too big tells you nothing about which element to drop.
