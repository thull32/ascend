@0
This is a binary search tree with nine nodes: 8 at the root, 3 and 10 below it, then 1, 6 and 14, and at the bottom 4, 7 and 13. Breadth-first search walks it with a first-in, first-out queue, which starts holding just the root. Hold onto one sentence, because it is the whole pattern: at the top of the outer loop, the queue holds exactly one level, in left-to-right order.

@1
Level 0 has one node in the queue, so the loop records that size, 1, and will dequeue exactly that many. Fixing the size before any child arrives is what keeps one level from bleeding into the next.

@2
Dequeue 8 and visit it. Its children, 3 and 10, go onto the back of the queue.

@3
The size loop is done, and the sentence holds again: the queue holds exactly level 1, which is 3 and then 10. The size is now 2.

@4-5
Dequeue 3 and enqueue its children, 1 and 6. Dequeue 10 and enqueue its only child, 14. Children always join behind the rest of their own level, so left-to-right order survives.

@6
Level 2 is in the queue: 1, 6 and 14. Three nodes, so the loop will drain exactly three.

@7-9
1 is a leaf and adds nothing. 6 adds 4 and 7. 14 adds 13.

@10
Level 3 is in the queue: 4, 7 and 13, three nodes again.

@11-13
All three are leaves. Each one is dequeued and visited, and nothing new arrives, so the queue empties.

@14
The output is 8, 3, 10, 1, 6, 14, 4, 7 and 13, in four levels. Every node is enqueued once and dequeued once, so the time is linear. The space is the widest level, because the queue never holds more than one level and part of the next. Anything per level, its size, its rightmost node, its sum, is a one-line change at the end of that inner loop, which is why the words level, row, nearest and width point to a queue.
