@0
A search tree built by inserting 4, 2, 6, 1, 3, 5 and 7: 4 at the root, 2 and 6 below it, and four leaves, 1, 3, 5 and 7. We turn it into the comma-separated format every test harness uses, with a plain breadth-first search. The queue starts with the root. Each node that comes off the front writes its value and puts both of its children on the back, including the empty ones, and an empty child writes a hash marker when its turn comes.

@1
Dequeue 4: write 4, and enqueue its children, 2 and 6.

@2-3
Dequeue 2: write 2, enqueue 1 and 3. Dequeue 6: write 6, enqueue 5 and 7. The tokens so far are 4, 2, 6, one level at a time.

@4-7
The four leaves come off in turn, 1, 3, 5 and 7, and each writes its value. A leaf still enqueues two children, and both are empty, so by the time 7 is written the queue holds nothing but eight markers.

@8-15
Each of those eight empty children reaches the front and writes a marker: two for 1, two for 3, two for 5, two for 7. The queue empties.

@16
The encoding is 4, 2, 6, 1, 3, 5, 7, followed by eight markers. Fifteen tokens for seven nodes: every node writes itself, and every one of the n plus one missing children writes a marker, so the length is always two n plus one. Now we decode, and the decoder is the same loop run backwards: a queue of nodes waiting for their children, each one consuming the next two tokens.

@17
The first token, 4, becomes the root, and it goes into the queue, because its children have not been read yet.

@18
Dequeue 4. It takes the next two tokens, 2 and 6: create them as its left and right children and enqueue both.

@19-20
Dequeue 2: it takes 1 and 3. Dequeue 6: it takes 5 and 7. The tree is complete in shape already, and the queue holds the four leaves, the nodes whose children are still unread.

@21-24
Each leaf in turn comes off the queue and takes the next two tokens, a pair of markers, so it gets no children. The queue and the token index advance in lockstep, two tokens per dequeued node.

@25
The decoded tree is the original. That lockstep is the invariant to say out loud in an interview: the queue holds, in order, exactly the nodes whose children have not been read yet. Both directions are linear. The markers are what let the decoder consume tokens in fixed pairs, and this format is worth knowing precisely because it is the bracketed array notation your tests are already written in.
