@0
In-order means recurse left, visit the node, then recurse right. On a binary search tree that produces the keys in sorted order. This tree has 5 at the root, 3 and 6 below it, 2 and 4 under 3, and 1 under 2.

@1-4
The walk goes as far left as it can, pushing each node on the stack: 5, then 3, then 2, then 1. The stack now holds the whole left spine, and 1 has no left child.

@5-6
With its left side finished, 1 is visited first: the output is 1. It has no right child, so it is popped off the stack.

@7-8
Back at 2, its left side is done, so 2 is visited. The output is 1, 2. No right child, so 2 is popped.

@9
At 3 the left side is done, so 3 is visited, and the output is 1, 2, 3. If the question were the third smallest key, the walk could stop right here, without ever touching 4, 5 or 6.

@10-13
This time there is a right child, 4. Enter it, find nothing to its left, and visit it: 1, 2, 3, 4. Then 4 and 3 both return and leave the stack.

@14
Only 5 remains on the stack. Its left subtree is finished, so it is visited: 1 to 5. Now it recurses right, into 6.

@15-18
6 has no children. It is visited, completing 1 to 6, and then 6 and 5 return.

@19
The output is 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, in sorted order. Every node is entered and left once, so the time is linear, and the stack never holds more than the height. That is why the kth node visited is the kth smallest key, and why stopping early costs only the height plus k.
