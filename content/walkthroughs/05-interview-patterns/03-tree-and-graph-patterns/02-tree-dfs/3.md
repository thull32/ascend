@0
In-order means left subtree, then the node, then the right subtree. On a binary search tree that produces the keys in sorted order. This tree has 5 at the root, 3 and 6 below it, 2 and 4 under 3, and 1 under 2. We walk it the way the kth smallest code does, with an explicit stack instead of recursion. One loop, three moves: push the left spine, pop the top and visit it, then step to its right child.

@1-4
The walk goes as far left as it can, pushing each node on the stack: 5, then 3, then 2, then 1. The stack now holds the whole left spine, and 1 has no left child, so the spine ends.

@5
Pop the top, 1. Nothing smaller can be left anywhere, so 1 is the first key in sorted order. It has no right child, so the next pop comes straight from the stack.

@6
Pop 2: second smallest, and again no right child.

@7
Pop 3: the output is 1, 2, 3. That third pop is the lesson's trace with k equal to 3: the code would return 3 right here, after three pops, and 4, 5 and 6 would never be visited.

@8
To finish the walk, 3 has a right child, 4. Step to it and push its left spine, which is just 4 itself, since 4 has no left child.

@9-10
Pop 4 and visit it: 1, 2, 3, 4. Then pop 5, the last node on the stack: everything in its left subtree is done, so it is fifth. Step to its right child, 6.

@11-12
Push 6, which has no left child, and pop it straight away: the output is 1 through 6, and the stack is empty, so the walk is over.

@13
The output is 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, in sorted order. Every node is pushed once and popped once, so the time is linear, and the stack never held more than four nodes, one root-to-leaf path. That is why the kth pop is the kth smallest key, and why stopping early costs only the height plus k.
