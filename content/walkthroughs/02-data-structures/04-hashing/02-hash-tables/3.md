@0
Four buckets with chaining, FNV-1a as the hash, and a load-factor bound of 0.75: as soon as the number of entries divided by the number of buckets goes above three quarters, the table doubles. A key's bucket is its hash modulo the bucket count, so when the count changes, every key's bucket has to be worked out again.

@1-6
Keys a, b and c land in buckets 0, 1 and 2. Three entries in four buckets is a load factor of exactly 0.75: at the bound, not over it.

@7-8
Key d lands in bucket 3. Four entries in four buckets, a load factor of 1.

@9
That is over the bound, so the table allocates 8 buckets and rehashes every entry. The hash values do not change; only the modulus does, from 4 to 8.

@10-11
Key a was in bucket 0, and its hash modulo 8 is 4. Key b was in bucket 1 and goes to 5. Both move, and both move up by exactly 4, the old size.

@12-13
Key c: its hash modulo 8 is 2, the same bucket number as before. Key d stays in 3. Both are still copied into the new array, but neither changes place. With doubling there are only two possibilities for every key: keep the bucket number, or move up by the old size. Java's HashMap uses exactly this to split each bucket into a low list and a high list by one bit of the hash.

@14
The rehash is complete. All four entries were recomputed, a linear amount of work, and two of the four changed bucket: the half that doubling moves on average. The load factor is back to 0.5, and spread over the inserts that filled the table, the resize costs a constant amount per insert.

@15-16
Key e now goes straight into bucket 0 of the bigger table. Five entries in eight buckets, a load factor of about 0.63, and no resize.

@17
Doubling keeps the load factor bounded, so lookups stay constant time on average. The price is an occasional pause that touches every entry, and that pause lands on whichever request made the unlucky insert: in a table of a million entries it is a few milliseconds. That is why latency-sensitive systems such as Redis rehash a little at a time, and why you pre-size a table when you know how big it will get.
