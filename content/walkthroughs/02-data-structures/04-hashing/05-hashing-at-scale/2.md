@0
A ring of positions from 0 to 359 degrees, with four servers on it: N1 at 40 degrees, N2 at 130, N3 at 220 and N4 at 310, a quarter of the ring apart. Keys are hashed onto the same ring, and the rule is a single sentence: a key belongs to the first server you reach walking clockwise from it, wrapping around past the top.

@1-2
User 1 hashes to 159 degrees. Walking clockwise, the first server is N3 at 220, so N3 owns it. User 7 lands at 124, just before N2 at 130, so N2 owns it.

@3-4
Video 9 lands at 35, a few degrees before N1: N1 owns it. Session 3, at 73, walks on to N2.

@5
Cart 5 lands at 322, past N4, the last server before the top. Walking clockwise it wraps around past zero, and the first server it meets is N1 at 40.

@6-12
The other seven keys follow the same rule, one at a time: feed 2 at 175 and user 3 at 187 walk to N3; user 2 at 278, user 5 at 236 and user 12 at 307 walk to N4; user 6 at 114 goes to N2; and user 9 at 25 goes to N1.

@13
All twelve keys are placed, three per server. Each server owns the arc that ends at its own position: N1 owns everything from just after 310 round to 40, N2 from 41 to 130, and so on.

@14
Now a fifth server joins. N5 lands at 30 degrees, between N4 at 310 and N1 at 40. It claims one arc, the stretch from 310 round to 30, and only the keys on that arc change owner: cart 5 and user 9, both moving from N1 to N5. That is 2 of 12 keys. No key moves between the old servers. With hash modulo N, going from 4 servers to 5 would have remapped 9 of these 12 keys.

@15
The ideal is that the newcomer takes its fair share, about a fifth of the keys, and nothing else moves. Here it took two. But with one point per server, how much moves depends on where the new point happens to land, and four random points rarely sit this evenly. That is why real rings place each server at many points, its virtual nodes: about 100 points per server brings each one within roughly 10 percent of its fair share, a new server takes a little from everyone, and a failed server's load spreads across all the others instead of one neighbour. Memcached's ketama ring uses 160 points per server, and Cassandra used 256 tokens per node until version 4.
