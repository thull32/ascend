@0
Three users, u1 to u3, and four items, i1 to i4. Each edge joins a user to an item they interacted with: u1 to i1 and i2, u2 to i2 and i3, u3 to i3 and i4. Edges only ever join a user to an item, never two users or two items, so the graph is bipartite. Breadth-first two-colouring checks it with one rule: every neighbour gets the opposite colour.

@1-2
Start at u1. Its items, i1 and i2, get the opposite colour.

@3-4
Through i2, the search reaches u2, which takes the colour opposite to i2, the same as u1. From u2, i3 gets the item colour.

@5-6
Through i3 the search reaches u3, on the user side, and from u3, i4 on the item side.

@7
Every edge joins two different colours, so the graph is bipartite, and the colours came out exactly as users and items. That success is guaranteed by construction. What makes the shape useful is the walks. Items liked by users who liked what you liked is a walk of length 3 that alternates sides: from u1 through i2 to u2, and on to i3. Recommenders count those walks at scale as a sparse matrix multiplication, which is the same computation.
