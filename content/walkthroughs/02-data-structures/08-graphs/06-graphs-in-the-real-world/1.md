@0
A tiny social graph. You are friends with ana and ben. Ana and ben are both friends with cy, ben is also friends with dee, cy with eli, and dee with fay. People you may know is a breadth-first search from you: level 1 is your friends, and level 2 is the friend-of-friend suggestions.

@1-3
You come off the queue at distance 0. Ana and ben are discovered at distance 1. That is level 1, the people you already know.

@4-5
Ana comes off the queue, and through her, cy is discovered at distance 2: a friend of a friend.

@6-7
Ben comes off. Ben is friends with cy too, but cy is already discovered, so cy is not added a second time. BFS discovers each vertex once, so cy is suggested once, however many mutual friends lead there. Dee is new, at distance 2.

@8-11
Cy comes off and leads to eli, at distance 3. Dee comes off and leads to fay, also at distance 3.

@12-13
Eli and fay come off with nothing new to find, and the queue is empty.

@14
The labels are the distances from you: two friends, two suggestions at distance 2, two people at distance 3. In production the depth is capped at 2, and the suggestions are ranked by how many mutual friends lead to each one. The real cost is not the average user but the supernodes. A user with 300 friends, each with 300 friends, has up to 90,000 candidates before de-duplication. One celebrity friend with 10 million followers and the same two-hop query touches 10 million vertices. So production systems cap or sample per neighbour, precompute candidate lists, or skip supernodes when expanding.
