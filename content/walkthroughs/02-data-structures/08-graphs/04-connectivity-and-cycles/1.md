@0
Seven vertices, A to G, and five undirected edges: A to B, B to C, D to E, E to F, and F to G. A component is a maximal set of vertices with a path between every pair of them. The method is a loop with a traversal inside it: walk over the vertices, and whenever you hit one that has no label yet, traverse from it. Everything that traversal reaches is one component.

@1-4
The loop starts at A, which has no label, so A starts component 1. The traversal paints A, then B, then C with that label. C has no edge further, so component 1 is complete: three vertices.

@5-9
Back in the outer loop, B and C are skipped because they are already painted. D is unlabelled, so it starts component 2, and the traversal paints D, E, F and G, following the chain of edges from D.

@10
Two components. The outer loop touched every vertex once, and the traversals together scanned every adjacency list once, so the cost is V plus E. The label array doubles as the visited set, and once it is built, asking whether two vertices are in the same group is a single comparison of their labels.
