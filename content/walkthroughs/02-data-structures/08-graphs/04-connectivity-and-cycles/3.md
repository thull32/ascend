@0
Seven vertices and eight directed edges. A points to B and to D, B and D both point to C, D also points to E, and E, F and G form a loop: E to F, F to G, G back to E. This graph holds both cases that matter: a diamond, which is not a cycle, and a real cycle. Three colours tell them apart: grey means on the current path, black means finished.

@1-3
Enter A, then follow its edge to B, then B's edge to C. A, B and C are grey.

@4-5
C has no outgoing edges, so it finishes and turns black. Then B has nothing left, and finishes too.

@6-7
Back in A, the next edge leads to D. Enter D. D points to C, but C is black: finished, and not on the current path. That is the diamond, A to B to C and A to D to C. A check that only asks whether C was visited would report a cycle here, and it would be wrong. The colour check lets it pass. D's other edge leads to E, so enter E.

@8-9
E leads to F, and F to G. The current path is now A, D, E, F, G, all grey.

@10
G's edge leads to E, and E is grey: it is on the current path. That is a back edge, and a real cycle, E to F to G and back to E. The search stops and reports it. This is the same check a database deadlock detector runs on its wait-for graph, and the same one a build tool runs to reject a circular dependency.
