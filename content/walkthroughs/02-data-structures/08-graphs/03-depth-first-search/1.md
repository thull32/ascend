@0
Eight vertices and twelve directed edges, built to contain every kind of edge depth-first search can meet. DFS commits: it follows one edge as deep as it can, and backs up only when stuck. The call stack holds exactly the current path, and that is the idea to hold onto. A vertex on the stack is grey, still being explored. Reaching a grey vertex again means you have walked in a circle.

@1-3
One clock stamps every event. Enter 0 at time 1. Its first edge leads to 1, so enter 1 at time 2. The only edge out of 1 leads to 3, so enter 3 at time 3. The path is now 0, 1, 3, and nothing has been finished yet.

@4
Now 3 examines its edge to 0. Vertex 0 is still on the path, grey, an ancestor of 3. That is a back edge, and a back edge means a cycle: 0, 1, 3, and back to 0.

@5-6
3's other edge leads to 4, which is new, so enter 4 at time 4. Vertex 4 has no outgoing edges, so it finishes at once, at time 5.

@7-8
With nothing left to explore, 3 finishes at time 6, then 1 at time 7. They are black now: finished, and off the path.

@9
Back in 0, the next edge is the one to 3. It is examined only now, after 1's whole subtree is done, and 3 is black. It was discovered at time 3, after 0 at time 1, so 3 is a descendant that has already finished. This is a forward edge: a shortcut down the tree, not a cycle.

@10-11
0's last edge leads to 2, which is new. Enter 2 at time 8. Its first edge points at 1, which is black and was discovered at time 2, long before 2. Vertex 1 sits in a subtree that finished before 2 even started, so this is a cross edge, and again not a cycle.

@12-14
2's next edge dives again: enter 5 at time 9, 6 at time 10, and 7 at time 11. The path is five vertices long: 0, 2, 5, 6, 7.

@15
7's edge leads back to 5, which is still on the path. Second back edge, second cycle: 5, 6, 7, and back to 5.

@16-17
7 finishes at time 12, then 6 at time 13.

@18
Back in 5, the edge to 4 remains. Vertex 4 is black and was discovered at time 4, before 5 at time 9: another cross edge, into the subtree that finished first.

@19-21
Now everything unwinds. 5 finishes at time 14, 2 at time 15, and finally 0 at time 16, the first vertex in and the last one out. Sixteen events for eight vertices, the same numbers as the lesson's table.

@22
Each label shows a vertex's discovery and finish times, and they nest like parentheses: 1's interval, 2 to 7, sits inside 0's, 1 to 16, because 1 is 0's descendant, while 1 and 2 have disjoint intervals because they are siblings. Each vertex was entered once and each list scanned once, so the cost is V plus E. Two takeaways. A cycle exists exactly when there is a back edge, an edge to a grey vertex; treating any visited vertex as a cycle is the classic false positive, and the cross edge from 2 to 1 would report one. And sorting by decreasing finish time gives a topological order, when the graph has no cycles.
