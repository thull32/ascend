@0
Six nodes, 1 through 6, and we want the middle without counting the length first. Two pointers start at the head. Slow moves one node per step; fast moves two. When fast runs out of list, slow has covered half the distance.

@1
Step 1: slow moves to 2, fast to 3. Fast has followed two links, slow one.

@2
Step 2: slow moves to 3, fast to 5. Four links against two, still exactly double.

@3
Step 3: slow moves to 4. Fast steps from 5 to 6, the tail, and then follows 6's next pointer, which is null: it has run off the end. Six pointers followed against three, still exactly double.

@4
Fast is null, so the loop stops, and slow is on 4. An even length of 6 has two middles, 3 and 4, and this loop, which runs while fast and fast's next are both non-null, picks the second. One pass, linear time, constant space. Both halves of that loop condition are load-bearing: odd lengths end with fast on the last node, even lengths end with fast null. And choose your variant on purpose: the palindrome check wants the second middle, while splitting a list for merge sort wants the first.
