@0
Sorted input, 1 through 7: the input that turned a plain search tree into a chain. This time the tree is AVL, so every node's balance factor, left height minus right height, must stay between minus one and plus one. The labels show each node's height, counted in edges so that a leaf is 0, and its balance.

@1-4
One becomes the root. Two goes right and attaches, and 1 is at height 1, balance minus one, which is allowed.

@5-8
Three goes right twice and attaches under 2. Node 2 is at minus one.

@9
Node 1 is at minus two, with the new key in its right child's right side. Right-right case: rotate left about 1.

@10
Two is now the root, with 1 and 3 beside it, height 1. In a plain tree we would already have a chain of three.

@11-15
Four goes right at 2 and at 3, and attaches. Walking up, 3 and 2 are both at minus one, and the root's height is 2. No rotation.

@16-20
Five walks right past 2, 3 and 4 and attaches. Node 4 is at minus one.

@21
Node 3 is at minus two. Rotate left about 3.

@22
Four takes 3's place as the root's right child, with 3 and 5 beneath it.

@23
Back at the root, 2 is at minus one. Fine.

@24-29
Six walks right past 2, 4 and 5 and attaches. On the way back, 5 and 4 are both at minus one.

@30
At the root, 2 reaches minus two. Rotate left about 2. Rotations do not only happen near the leaves; this one moves the root.

@31
Four is now the root, with 2 over 1 and 3 on the left, and 5 then 6 on the right. Its height is 2, the same as the tree's before 6 arrived.

@32-37
Seven goes right at 4, at 5 and at 6, and attaches. Node 6 is at minus one, but 5 is at minus two: the fourth right-right case. Rotate left about 5.

@38
Six takes 5's place as the root's right child, with 5 and 7 beneath it.

@39
Back at the root, 4 is perfectly balanced at zero.

@40
The result is the perfect tree: 4 at the root, 2 and 6 below, and 1, 3, 5 and 7 as leaves. Height 2 edges, where the plain tree built a chain of height 6. It took four rotations for seven sorted keys, and that rate holds up: for a hundred thousand sorted keys, about one rotation per insert, never more. That is what balancing buys: the input order no longer decides the height, so search, insert and delete stay logarithmic whatever order clients send the keys in.
