@0
We will insert 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 and then 25 into an AVL tree. The rule it enforces at every node: the heights of the two subtrees may differ by at most one. Each node's label shows its height and its balance factor, left height minus right height. Heights are counted in edges, as in the rest of this module, so a leaf has height 0 and an empty subtree minus one. The balance factor must stay at minus one, zero or plus one.

@1
Ten arrives in an empty tree and becomes the root, a leaf of height 0.

@2-4
Twenty is larger, so it goes right and attaches as a leaf. Then we walk back up, recomputing heights. At 10, the left side is empty, minus one, and the right has height 0, so 10 has height 1 and balance minus one. Lopsided, but allowed.

@5-8
Thirty goes right at 10, right at 20, and becomes a leaf under 20. Back at 20, the balance is minus one: fine.

@9
Back at 10, the height is 2 and the balance is minus two. The rule is broken. The new key went into the right child's right side, a straight line leaning right, so this is the right-right case, and one left rotation about 10 fixes it.

@10
The rotation lifts 20 to the root, with 10 on its left and 30 on its right. Its height is 1, the same as the subtree's height before 30 arrived, so nothing above could have changed. Three pointers moved and the in-order sequence did not, so it is still a search tree.

@11-15
Forty arrives, goes right at 20 and right at 30, and becomes a leaf. On the way back, 30 has balance minus one and 20 has balance minus one, at height 2. Both are within the rule, so nothing rotates.

@16-20
Fifty walks right three times, past 20, 30 and 40, and attaches under 40. Node 40 is fine at minus one.

@21
But 30 is now at minus two: another straight line to the right. Right-right again, so rotate left about 30.

@22
Forty is lifted into 30's old place, as 20's right child, with 30 and 50 beneath it. The rotation happened in the middle of the tree, and the rest of it, 20 and 10, did not move.

@23
Back at 20, the balance is minus one, so the walk up finds nothing else to fix.

@24-27
Now the interesting one. Twenty-five goes right at 20, then left at 40, then left at 30, and becomes 30's left child.

@28-29
Walking back up: 30 is now plus one, leaning left, and 40 is plus one as well, at height 2. Both allowed.

@30
At 20 the height is 3 and the balance hits minus two. The tree is right-heavy at 20, but its right child, 40, leans left. That sign flip is the tell for a zig-zag, the right-left case. A single left rotation at 20 would just produce the mirror-image imbalance. So first, rotate 40 right, to straighten the line.

@31
After that rotation, 30 is 20's right child, and 30, 40 and 50 form a straight line leaning right. Now the signs agree, and one left rotation about 20 finishes the job.

@32
Thirty is the new root, with 20 over 10 and 25 on the left, and 40 over 50 on the right. Its height is 2, exactly what the subtree at 20 had before 25 arrived. That is why one fix per insert is enough: the insert raised the subtree's height by one, which is the only reason 20 went out of balance, and the double rotation brought it back to the old height, so no ancestor sees a change and the walk can stop.

@33
The final height is 2 edges for six keys; a plain search tree fed the same order would have height 4. An insert is a logarithmic descent plus a constant amount of repair.
