@0
We will insert 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 and then 25 into an AVL tree. The rule it enforces at every node: the heights of the two subtrees may differ by at most one. Each node's label shows its height and its balance factor, left height minus right height. The labels count height in nodes, so a leaf shows height 1. The balance factor must stay at minus one, zero or plus one.

@1
Ten arrives in an empty tree and becomes a leaf.

@2-4
Twenty is larger, so it goes right. Then we walk back up, recomputing heights. At 10, the left side is empty and the right has height 1, so the balance is minus one. Lopsided, but allowed.

@5-8
Thirty goes right at 10, right at 20, and becomes a leaf. Back at 20, the balance is minus one: fine.

@9
Back at 10, the balance is minus two. The rule is broken. The new key went into the right child's right side, a straight line leaning right, so this is the right-right case, and one left rotation about 10 fixes it.

@11
The rotation lifted 20 to the top, with 10 on its left and 30 on its right. Three pointers changed and the in-order sequence did not, so it is still a search tree. Now 40 arrives, and it goes right at 20.

@12-15
It goes right again at 30 and becomes a leaf. On the way back, 30 has balance minus one and 20 has balance minus one. Both are within the rule, so nothing rotates.

@16-20
Fifty walks right three times, past 20, 30 and 40, and attaches under 40. Node 40 is fine at minus one.

@21
But 30 is now at minus two: another straight line to the right. Right-right again, so rotate left about 30.

@23
Forty has been lifted, with 30 and 50 beneath it. Back at 20, the balance is minus one, so the walk up finds nothing else to fix.

@24-27
Now the interesting one. Twenty-five goes right at 20, then left at 40, then left at 30, and becomes 30's left child.

@28-29
Walking back up: 30 is now plus one, leaning left, and 40 is plus one as well. Both allowed.

@30
At 20 the balance hits minus two. The tree is right-heavy at 20, but its right child, 40, leans left. That sign flip is the tell for a zig-zag, the right-left case. A single left rotation at 20 would just produce the mirror-image imbalance. So first, rotate 40 right, to straighten the line.

@31
After that rotation, the right side is a straight line: 30, then 40, then 50, all leaning right. Now the signs agree, and one left rotation about 20 finishes the job.

@33
Thirty is the root, with 20 over 10 and 25 on the left, and 40 over 50 on the right. The height is three nodes; a plain search tree fed the same order would be five. Here is why one fix per insert is enough: before 25 arrived, the subtree at 20 had a certain height. The insert raised it by one, which is the only reason 20 went out of balance. The double rotation brought it back to the old height, so no ancestor sees a change, and the walk can stop. An insert is a logarithmic descent plus a constant amount of repair.
