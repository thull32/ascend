@0
We start from an empty tree and insert nine keys in this order: 8, 3, 10, 1, 6, 14, 4, 7, 13. Keep one rule in mind, because insert exists only to protect it: every key in a node's left subtree is smaller than the node, and every key in its right subtree is larger. Not just the children; the entire subtrees.

@1
The tree is empty, so 8 becomes the root. The first key to arrive is the root forever.

@2-3
Three is less than 8 and the left slot is empty, so 3 becomes a leaf there. Ten is greater than 8, so it becomes a leaf on the right.

@4-5
Now the walks get longer. One is less than 8, go left; less than 3, and that left slot is empty, so 1 attaches there at depth 2.

@6-7
Six goes left at 8, then right at 3, because 6 is larger than 3. It lands as 3's right child.

@8-9
Fourteen goes right at 8 and right at 10, and attaches under 10.

@10-12
Four takes three comparisons: left at 8, right at 3, then left at 6. It becomes a leaf at depth 3. Notice that nothing already in the tree moved. Insert is a search that falls off the bottom, and the new key always becomes a leaf where it fell.

@13-15
Seven follows the same path, left, right, and then right at 6, so it sits beside 4 under 6.

@16-18
The last key, 13, goes right at 8, right at 10, then left at 14.

@19
Nine nodes, height 3, which is as short as nine nodes can be. Each insert cost one walk from the root, so the cost is the height: logarithmic when the tree is balanced, linear when it is a chain. And nothing here chose the shape except the order the keys arrived in. Send the same nine keys in a different order and you get a different tree.
