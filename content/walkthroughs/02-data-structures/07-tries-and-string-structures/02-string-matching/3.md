@0
The string is a a b x a a b. Entry i of the Z-array is how far the string starting at position i agrees with the string's own prefix. Entry 0 is the whole length, 7, by convention. The Z-box tracks the rightmost stretch found so far that matches the prefix, and it is the whole trick: inside the box, the answer is already known.

@1-3
Position 1 is outside any box, so we compare from scratch. Its a matches the first a, but the next b does not match the second a. Entry 1 settles at 1.

@4
That match reaches further right than anything before, so the box now covers just position 1.

@5-8
Positions 2 and 3 are outside the box as well. The b at 2 and the x at 3 each fail on their first comparison with a, so both entries are 0.

@10-13
Position 4 is outside the box, so compare from scratch again: a matches a, a matches a, and b matches b, reaching the end of the string. Entry 4 is 3, because a a b at position 4 repeats the prefix a a b.

@14
The box moves to cover positions 4 to 6. Everything in it is a copy of positions 0 to 2.

@15-16
Here is the payoff. Position 5 is inside the box, and its mirror in the prefix is position 1, whose entry is 1. So entry 5 starts at 1 without a single comparison, capped by the room left in the box, which is 2. One fresh comparison follows: a against b, a mismatch, and entry 5 settles at 1.

@17-18
Position 6 is inside the box too. Its mirror is position 2, whose entry is 0, so it starts at 0. One comparison, a against b, fails, and entry 6 is 0.

@19
The Z-array is 7, 1, 0, 0, 3, 1, 0. Every fresh comparison that succeeds pushes the box's right edge further, and that edge only moves right, so the total work is linear. To search for a pattern, compute Z on the pattern, a separator, then the text: every position whose entry equals the pattern's length is a match.
