@0
An item already inside a heap needs a smaller key: Dijkstra found a shorter path, or an operator bumped a job's priority. A plain heap cannot find the old entry without scanning, because siblings are unordered. Lazy deletion sidesteps the problem. Leave the old entry where it is, push a fresh one with the new key, and when an entry surfaces at the top, check it against your own record and discard it if it is stale.

@1-5
Three items arrive with keys 7, 3 and 9. The 3 climbs to the root; the 9 stays put. The heap is 3, 7, 9.

@6-8
Now the item keyed 7 gets a new key, 3. We do not touch the 7. We push a second entry, 3, which climbs past the 7 and stops under the root's 3, because it is not strictly smaller. Both entries for that item now sit in the heap, the stale 7 and the fresh 3, and the caller's record says 3 is current.

@9-11
Pop. A 3 comes off the top. The last element, 7, moves to the root and sifts down below the other 3.

@12-14
Pop again: the other 3. Both 3s were live entries, one for each item, so both are returned as normal. The 9 moves up and sifts down below the 7.

@15-16
A new item arrives with key 1 and climbs straight to the root.

@17-18
Pop: the 1 comes out. The 7 is back on top.

@19-20
Pop, and the heap hands back the 7. That is the stale entry for the item whose key dropped to 3, and that item was already served. The heap has no idea; it is the caller's check against its record that discards this entry and pops again. In Dijkstra, that check is simply: has this vertex already been finalised?

@21
The heap did nothing special; every push and pop was the ordinary logarithmic sift. The cost of laziness is garbage: the heap can hold one entry per update, not one per item, so for Dijkstra it grows with the number of edges rather than vertices. That is fine in most cases, and the lesson's measurements found far fewer than the worst case. When cancellations vastly outnumber pops, you need a bound, as asyncio has: rebuild the heap once more than half its entries are cancelled. Otherwise, move to an indexed heap with a position map.
