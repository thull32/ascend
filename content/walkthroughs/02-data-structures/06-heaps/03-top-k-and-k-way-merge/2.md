@0
We want the median of a stream after every value: 5, 16, 1, 4, 9, 2. Two heaps face each other. Low is a max-heap holding the smaller half; high is a min-heap holding the larger half. Two rules: everything in low is no larger than anything in high, and low has either the same number of values as high, or one more. Then the median is always at the roots.

@1-3
Every insert uses the same three steps, with no case analysis. Push 5 into low. Move low's maximum, the 5, across to high; this keeps the first rule, whatever the value was. Now high is bigger than low, so move high's minimum back.

@4
Low holds one value and high none. With an odd count, the median is low's root: 5.

@5-6
Push 16 into low, where it sifts to the top. Move low's maximum, 16, across to high. The sizes are one and one, so no rebalance.

@7
Equal sizes, so the median is the average of the two roots, 5 and 16: 10.5.

@8-10
Push 1 into low. Low's maximum, 5, moves across to high, which now holds 5 and 16. High is bigger, so its minimum, 5, comes back.

@11
Low holds 5 and 1, high holds 16. The median is low's root: 5.

@12-13
Push 4 into low. Low's maximum, 5, crosses to high. Two and two: balanced.

@14
The median is the average of 4 and 5: 4.5.

@15-17
Push 9 into low, and it rises to the top. Here is why push-then-move matters: 9 belongs in the upper half, and it gets there with no comparison at all, because the step that moves low's maximum across sends the 9. High is now bigger, so its minimum, 5, comes back to low.

@18
Low is 5, 4 and 1, high is 9 and 16. The median is 5.

@19-20
Push 2 into low. Low's maximum, 5, crosses to high. Three and three.

@21
The median is the average of 4 and 5: 4.5 again.

@22
Each value cost one push into low and one or two moves between the heaps, each logarithmic, and the median is read from the roots in constant time. Sorting after every value would cost n log n per query. The same two heaps facing each other track any boundary in a changing set, such as a percentile. The limit is deletion: a sliding-window median must remove the value leaving the window, which a plain heap cannot do. That needs lazy deletion, the next lesson.
