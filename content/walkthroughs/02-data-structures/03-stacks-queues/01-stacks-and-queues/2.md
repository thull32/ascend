@0
An empty queue. Unlike a stack, a queue works at two ends: enqueue appends at the back, dequeue removes from the front. So the first item in is the first item out.

@1-3
Enqueue 1, 2 and 3. Each joins at the back, and 1 stays at the front the whole time, because nothing has left yet.

@4-5
Two dequeues. One leaves from the front, then 2, in exactly the order they arrived. Only 3 remains, and it is now both the front and the back.

@6-7
Enqueue 4 and 5 at the back. Three is still the front: new arrivals never jump the line.

@8-10
Three more dequeues: 3, then 4, then 5, and the queue is empty. The dequeued list reads 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, the same order things went in.

@11
That is the contract. The cost is the hard part: with a linked list or a ring buffer, both ends are constant time. Built on a plain array, every dequeue from the front shifts all the remaining elements left by one, which is linear per operation, and draining a backlog of a million items that way takes seconds instead of milliseconds.
