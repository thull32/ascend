@0
Eight brackets to check: a curly brace, a square bracket and a round bracket opening, the three closers in reverse order, then a round opener and a square closer. The rule is two lines. Every opener is pushed. Every closer must match whatever is on top of the stack, because the most recent thing opened is the first thing that has to close.

@1-3
The first three characters are all openers: curly, square, round. Each is pushed, and the depth climbs to 3. The stack now records the exact order in which they must close, most recent on top.

@4
A round closer. The top is the round opener from position 2, so they match: pop it. Depth 2.

@5
A square closer, and the top is now the square opener from position 1. Match, pop, depth 1.

@6
A curly closer matches the curly opener from position 0. The stack is empty and the depth is 0. If the string ended here, it would be balanced, because balanced means every closer matched and nothing is left over.

@7
But it continues. A round opener is pushed, depth 1.

@8
Then a square closer. The top of the stack is a round opener, so they do not match, and the scan stops: not balanced. This is one of three failure modes, and every test set needs all three: a closer that does not match the top, as here; a closer when the stack is already empty; and openers left over at the end. The last one is the one people forget, which is why the function returns whether the stack is empty rather than simply true.
