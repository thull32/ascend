@0
Eight values: 3, 1, 4, 1, 5, 9, 2, 6. Below them is the prefix array, one slot longer than the input. Its first entry is already filled: P of 0 is 0, the sum of no elements at all. Every later entry P of i holds the sum of the first i values. Build it once, in one pass, and any range sum after that is a single subtraction.

@1-2
P of 1 is the previous entry plus the first value: 0 plus 3, which is 3. P of 2 is 3 plus 1, which is 4. That is the whole rule, one addition per element.

@3-8
The rest follows the same way. 4 plus 4 is 8. Plus 1 is 9. Plus 5 is 14. Plus 9 is 23. Plus 2 is 25. Plus 6 is 31. The last entry, P of 8, is the sum of the whole array.

@9
Now a query: the sum of positions 3 through 5, which hold 1, 5 and 9. Take P of 6, the total of everything up to and including position 5, which is 23, and subtract P of 3, the total of everything before position 3, which is 8. Everything before the range cancels, and 23 minus 8 is 15. One subtraction, constant time, however long the range. For q queries the total cost is n plus q instead of n times q. The leading zero is what makes every query the same formula: a range that starts at position 0 just subtracts P of 0, which is 0, with no special case and no off-by-one.
