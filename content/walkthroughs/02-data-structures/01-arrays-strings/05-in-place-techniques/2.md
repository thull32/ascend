@0
A sorted array, 1, 2, 4, 7, 11, 15, and a target of 15: find two values that add up to it. One pointer starts at each end. The idea to hold onto is this: if the pair exists, both of its positions are between the two pointers. The sum of the two ends tells you which pointer has become useless.

@1
1 plus 15 is 16, too big. The right value, 15, is too large to pair with anything, because 1 is already the smallest value available. So the right pointer moves left.

@2
1 plus 11 is 12, too small. Now the left value is the useless one: even the largest remaining partner, 11, cannot lift it to 15. The left pointer moves right.

@3
2 plus 11 is 13, still too small. The left pointer moves right again.

@4
4 plus 11 is 15. Found, at positions 2 and 4, after four comparisons. Every step throws away one element for good, so the whole search is linear. This only works because the array is sorted: the comparison has to tell you which pointer is useless to keep.
