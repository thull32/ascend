@0
Rotate 1 through 7 to the right by 3, in place. The answer should be 5, 6, 7, 1, 2, 3, 4. The trick is three reversals: reverse the whole array, then reverse the first three, then reverse the rest.

@1-3
First, reverse everything with two pointers moving inward. Swap the ends, 1 and 7. Then 2 and 6. Then 3 and 5. The 4 in the middle stays. The array reads 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1. The last three elements are now at the front, but backwards.

@4
Reverse the first three. One swap, the 7 with the 5, and the front reads 5, 6, 7, in the right order.

@5-6
Reverse the last four: swap 4 with 1, then 3 with 2. The back reads 1, 2, 3, 4.

@7
The array is 5, 6, 7, 1, 2, 3, 4: rotated right by 3, in linear time and constant space. Reversing everything moves the last k elements to the front, backwards, and the two smaller reversals restore each block's internal order. The swaps stream through memory in order, with the prefetcher's help. One guard before you start: take k modulo n, or a k larger than the array indexes off the end.
