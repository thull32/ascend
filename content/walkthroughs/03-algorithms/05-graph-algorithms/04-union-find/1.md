@0
Seven nodes, A to G, and each label shows a node's parent pointer. Right now every node points to itself, so every node is a root, the representative of its own set, with rank 0. Two nodes are in the same set exactly when they have the same root. The edges arrive one at a time, and each one is a union.

@1-2
The first edge is A to B. Find returns two different roots, A and B, so the sets merge: one root is hung under the other. Both have rank 0, a tie, so either could win; here B now points to A, and A's rank goes up to 1.

@3-4
The edge C to D does the same: roots C and D tie at rank 0, D now points to C, and C's rank becomes 1. There are two trees of two nodes each, rooted at A and at C.

@5-6
Now B to C. Find walks up from B to its root, A, and from C to its root, C itself. Different roots, so the two trees merge. Their ranks tie again, at 1, so root C is hung under root A, and A's rank rises to 2. A single pointer change joined four nodes into one set, because union only ever touches roots. Notice D's label: it still points to C. D is now two hops from the root, and nothing has needed to change that yet.

@7-8
E to F: roots E and F, both rank 0, so F now points to E, and E's rank becomes 1. A second set starts to form at the bottom.

@9-10
Here is the edge to watch. A to D: find from A gives A, and find from D climbs through C to A. On the way, path compression rewrites D's pointer to aim straight at A, so the next find from D takes one hop instead of two. Same root, so the union does nothing and returns false. That edge would close a cycle, and that returned false is the entire cycle-detection logic of problems like Redundant Connection and Graph Valid Tree.

@11-12
Last, F to G. F's root is E, with rank 1, and G is its own root, with rank 0. The ranks differ, so the lower one goes underneath: G is hung under E, and no rank changes. Two sets remain: A, B, C and D, and E, F and G.

@13
Each union is two finds and at most one pointer change, so the cost is the depth of the trees. Union by rank hangs the lower-rank root under the higher one, and only a tie raises a rank, which bounds the height at log of n. Path compression points every node a find passes through straight at the root. With both, each operation costs amortised inverse Ackermann of n, which is at most 4 for any input that will ever exist: effectively constant.
