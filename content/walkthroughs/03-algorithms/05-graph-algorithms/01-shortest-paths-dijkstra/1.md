@0
Six nodes, A to F, joined by directed, weighted edges. A starts at distance 0 and every other node at infinity, because no path to it has been found yet. Hold onto one rule, because it is the whole algorithm: always settle the closest unsettled node. With no negative weights, a settled node's distance can never improve.

@1-3
The heap pops A with distance 0, and A is settled. Now relax its outgoing edges. A to B has weight 4, so B drops from infinity to 4. A to C has weight 2, so C drops to 2. The heap holds two entries: B at 4 and C at 2.

@4
The smallest entry is C at 2, so C is settled next. Nothing can ever reach C more cheaply, because any other route would have to leave through B at 4 or more.

@5
Here is the moment worth staring at. C to B has weight 1, and 2 plus 1 is 3, better than the 4 that A gave B. B's distance becomes 3, but the old entry is not updated in place: a second entry, B at 3, is pushed, and B at 4 is still sitting in the heap.

@6-7
C also reaches D, at 2 plus 8, which is 10, and E, at 2 plus 10, which is 12. The heap now holds four entries, one of them stale.

@8-9
The smallest is B at 3, so B is settled at 3. Its edge to D has weight 5, and 3 plus 5 is 8, better than 10. D improves to 8, and another entry is pushed.

@10
D at 8 is settled next. Look at the heap: the old B at 4 entry has gone. When it surfaced, 4 was worse than B's settled distance of 3, so it was skipped, costing one pop and one comparison and nothing more.

@11-12
From D, the edge to E gives 8 plus 2, which is 10, better than 12. The edge to F gives 8 plus 6, which is 14, the first path to F.

@13-14
E is settled at 10. The stale D at 10 entry has been skipped the same way. E's edge to F gives 10 plus 3, which is 13, better than 14.

@15
F is settled at 13. Every node now has its final distance.

@16
The highlighted edges are the shortest-path tree. The cheapest path to F is A, C, B, D, E, F, five edges for a cost of 13, while the three-edge path A, B, D, F costs 15. Fewest edges and cheapest are different questions. With a binary heap the cost is order of V plus E, times log V. And notice the discipline that made it right: a node is settled when it is popped, never when it is pushed. Had B been marked visited when A first pushed it, C's improvement to 3 would have been thrown away.
