@0
Six nodes and seven directed edges. A, B and C form a cycle, D and E point at each other, and E leads on to F. Each label will show two numbers: the discovery time, and the low-link, the smallest discovery time this node can reach while that node is still on the stack. Hold onto one rule: when a node finishes and its low-link still equals its own discovery time, it is the root of a strongly connected component.

@1-3
The depth-first search starts at A, discovery time 0, then follows A to B, time 1, and B to C, time 2. Each node starts with its low-link equal to its discovery time, and each is pushed on the stack as it is discovered.

@4
C's edge leads back to A. A is still on the stack, so C's low-link drops to A's discovery time: the smaller of 2 and 0, which is 0. C now finishes with low 0, not equal to its discovery time of 2, so it is not a root and stays on the stack.

@5
Back in B, the search takes the smaller of B's own low-link and its child's: C's subtree reaches time 0, so B's low-link drops from 1 to 0. That propagation is the moment to remember: it keeps B and C on the stack until A closes their component.

@6-7
B's other edge leads to D, time 3, and from D the search goes to E, time 4.

@8
E's edge back to D is the second back edge. D is on the stack, so E's low-link drops from 4 to 3.

@9
E's other edge leads to F, discovered at time 5.

@10
F has no outgoing edges, so it finishes, and its low-link of 5 equals its discovery time. F is a root: pop the stack down to F, and F alone is the first component.

@11
Back in E, low 3 does not equal discovery 4, so E is not a root. Back in D, D's low-link is still 3, because E's subtree reached no further than D itself, and 3 equals D's discovery time, so D is a root. Pop down to D: E and D form the second component.

@12
Back in B, low 0, not a root. Back in A, low 0 equals discovery 0. Pop the rest of the stack, C, B and A, as the third component.

@13
Three components from a single depth-first search, in order of V plus E. They came out as F, then D and E, then A, B and C, which is the reverse topological order of the condensation, the graph you get by collapsing each component to one node. That order is free, and it is always a DAG you can run a dynamic program over. One detail to get right in code: only an edge to a node still on the stack may lower the low-link. A node already popped belongs to a finished component, and following it would wrongly merge two components.
