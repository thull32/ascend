@0
The same five meetings, 1 to 4, 2 to 5, 3 to 6, 4 to 7 and 5 to 8, now as a difference array indexed by time, from 0 to 8. Each slot holds plus 1 for every meeting that starts at that time and minus 1 for every meeting that ends there. So times 1, 2 and 3 hold plus 1. Times 4 and 5 each have one meeting ending and one starting, which cancel to 0. Times 6, 7 and 8 hold minus 1. The idea: a running total of these changes is the number of rooms in use.

@1-4
Build the running total. At time 0, nothing: 0. Then 1, 2, 3, as three meetings start one after another.

@5-6
At times 4 and 5, one meeting ends and another starts in the same instant, so the total holds at 3. That is the half-open convention in action: a room freed at 4 is reused at 4.

@7-9
Then the meetings end without replacements, and the total steps down 2, 1, 0.

@10
The last frame shows the other use of a prefix array: any range sum is one subtraction, here the sum from time 1 to time 7, which is 1. For rooms we only need the running total itself, and its peak, 3, is the answer, the same as the heap found. This version skips the sort entirely when times are small integers. The trap is real timestamps: one day in milliseconds is about 86 million slots, so on real data you sort the start and end events instead, putting ends before starts at equal times.
