@0
Six numbers: 4, 10, 3, 5, 1 and 8, drawn as a tree. The array is the tree: the children of position i sit at 2i plus 1 and 2i plus 2. Heap sort runs in two phases. First, turn the array into a max-heap, where every parent is at least as large as its children. Then repeatedly swap the maximum, at the root, to the end, and shrink the heap by one.

@1-2
Heapify starts at the last node with children, position 2, and works backwards. Position 2 holds 3, and its only child is 8. 8 is larger, so they swap, and 3 is now a leaf.

@3
Position 1 holds 10, with children 5 and 1. It is already larger than both, so nothing moves.

@4-6
Now the root, 4. Its larger child is 10, so they swap. 4 keeps sinking: its new children are 5 and 1, and 5 is larger, so they swap again. 4 is a leaf, and the array reads 10, 5, 8, 4, 1, 3.

@7
That is a max-heap, built with three sifts for six elements. The maximum, 10, is at the root. Now the extraction phase.

@8-10
Swap the root 10 with the last heap element, 3, and shrink the heap to five. 10 is now in its final sorted place at the end. The new root, 3, is too small, so it sifts down: its larger child is 8, they swap, and 3 is a leaf.

@11-14
Again. Root 8 swaps with 1 at position 4, and the sorted suffix is 8, 10. 1 sifts down: swap with 5, then with its only child 4. The heap reads 5, 4, 3, 1.

@15-17
Root 5 swaps to position 3, and the heap shrinks to three. 1 sinks below 4, and stops.

@18-21
Root 4 goes to position 2. 3 is already larger than its one child, so nothing sifts. Then 3 swaps to position 1, leaving 1 alone at the root.

@22
Sorted ascending, in place: 1, 3, 4, 5, 8, 10. The build is linear, and each of the n extractions sifts through up to log n levels, so heap sort is n log n in every case with constant extra space. It is not stable, because sifting moves elements long distances past their equals. And on large arrays it is usually the slowest of the three, because each sift jumps to an address twice as far along, which misses the cache.
