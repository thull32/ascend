@0
Eight numbers: 3, 8, 2, 5, 1, 4, 7 and 6. Quicksort does its work in the partition. Pick a pivot, move everything smaller to its left and everything larger to its right, and the pivot is then exactly where it will be in the sorted output. It never moves again. This is the Lomuto scheme, which takes the last element as the pivot.

@1
The first range is the whole array, and the pivot is 6. Pointer j sweeps left to right; pointer i marks the end of the region of elements smaller than the pivot.

@2-3
3 is smaller than 6, so it joins the small region; here it swaps with itself. 8 is not smaller, so it is left alone, and it now sits at the front of the larger-than region.

@4-7
Now 2, 5, 1 and 4 are each smaller than 6. Each time, i advances and the small element swaps with the first element of the larger zone, which is 8. Watch the 8 get pushed one slot right with every swap, until the array reads 3, 2, 5, 1, 4, 8, 7, 6.

@8
7 is larger than 6, so it stays.

@9
The sweep is done. One final swap puts the pivot 6 at position 5, just past the small region. Everything to its left is smaller, everything to its right is larger, and 6 is finished.

@10-15
Now the left side, positions 0 to 4, with pivot 4. 3 and 2 are smaller and stay in place, 5 is larger and is skipped, and 1 swaps in past it. The pivot 4 lands at position 3, also final.

@16-19
Next, positions 0 to 2 with pivot 1. Neither 3 nor 2 is smaller than 1, so the small region stays empty and the pivot goes all the way to position 0. That is a lopsided split: nothing on the left, two on the right. Keep that in mind.

@20-22
Positions 1 to 2, pivot 3. 2 is smaller, and 3 stays at position 2.

@23-25
Back on the right of the first pivot, positions 6 and 7 with pivot 8. 7 is smaller, so 8 stays at position 7. The single 5 at position 4 needed no work at all.

@26
Sorted. On average every partition splits the range reasonably, so quicksort is n log n, in place, and in practice the fastest of the comparison sorts because its scans run through memory in order. But a pivot that is always the smallest or largest element, like that pivot of 1 a moment ago, peels off one element per pass. With a last-element pivot, already sorted input does that every time, and the cost becomes n squared. That is why real libraries use a better pivot rule, or a random pivot.
