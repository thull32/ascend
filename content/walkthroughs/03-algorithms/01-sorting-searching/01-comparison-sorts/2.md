@0
Seven numbers: 38, 27, 43, 3, 9, 82 and 10. Merge sort never looks at the values while it splits. It cuts the range in half, sorts each half the same way, and then merges two sorted halves by repeatedly taking the smaller of their two front elements.

@1-3
The first cut splits positions 0 to 6 the way the code does, with the shorter half on the left: 0 to 2, and 3 to 6. The left part is cut again, into the lone 38 and positions 1 to 2, and then 1 to 2 into two single elements. A single element is already sorted, so the recursion stops there.

@4-5
The first merge: 27 against 43. 27 is smaller, so it goes to position 1, then 43 follows into position 2.

@6-8
Now the lone 38 merges with that pair. The fronts are 38 and 27, so 27 goes to position 0. Then 38 beats 43, and 43 is copied in last. Cells that still show an old value are slots waiting to be written; the values themselves sit safely in a scratch copy. Positions 0 to 2 read 27, 38, 43.

@9-15
The right half gets the same treatment. Positions 3 to 6 split into two pairs. 3 and 9 merge as 3, 9. Then 82 and 10 merge, and this time the right element is smaller, so 10 goes first and 82 follows.

@16-19
Those two pairs merge: 3 from the left, 9 from the left, then 10 from the right, and 82 last. Positions 3 to 6 read 3, 9, 10, 82.

@20-26
The final merge brings together 27, 38, 43 and 3, 9, 10, 82, which is exactly the merge traced in the table. 3 is the smaller front, then 9 and 10 from the right half, then 27, 38 and 43 from the left, and once the left half is used up, 82 is copied in without a comparison. Six comparisons to merge seven elements; a merge never needs more than one fewer comparison than the number of elements it outputs.

@27
Sorted. There are about log n levels of splitting, and each level does linear merge work, so merge sort is n log n, and it is n log n on every input, sorted, reversed or random, because the split never depends on the values. The price is a scratch buffer of linear size for the merge. And because a tie takes from the left half first, merge sort is stable.
