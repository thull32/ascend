@0
Factorial of 5 is 5 times factorial of 4. Hold onto the shape of that sentence, because it is the whole picture: every call pushes a frame and waits for the smaller answer, so nothing at all is multiplied until the base case returns. The base case here is the lesson's: factorial of 0 is 1.

@1-5
Factorial of 5 needs factorial of 4 first, so it pushes a frame and recurses. Factorial of 4 needs factorial of 3, which needs factorial of 2, which needs factorial of 1, which needs factorial of 0. Each new call goes one level deeper, and every caller above it is parked, holding a multiplication it cannot finish yet. The depth counter climbs to 5.

@6
Factorial of 0 is the base case. It returns 1 directly, with no recursive call. This is the deepest point: six frames on the stack, one per call, from 5 down to 0.

@7-8
Now the answers come back up. Factorial of 0 is popped and hands 1 to factorial of 1, which does its pending multiply: 1 times 1 is 1. That is the first of five multiplications, one for every frame above the base case.

@9-11
Factorial of 2 computes 2 times 1, which is 2. Factorial of 3 computes 3 times 2, which is 6. Factorial of 4 computes 4 times 6, which is 24. Each frame finishes exactly when the one below it hands back its answer, and is popped.

@12
Factorial of 5 computes 5 times 24, which is 120, and the stack is empty again.

@13
120, after 6 calls and a maximum stack depth of 6. Every multiplication happened on the way up, which is why every frame had to stay alive until the bottom was reached. That is linear time and linear stack space, and it is why a loop is preferred when n is large: the loop does the same multiplications without keeping a frame for each one.
