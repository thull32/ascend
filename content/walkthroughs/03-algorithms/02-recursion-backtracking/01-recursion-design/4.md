@0
Three discs on peg A, to be moved to peg C, never putting a larger disc on a smaller one. The rule makes two recursive calls per frame. To move n discs, move the n minus 1 smaller ones out of the way onto the spare peg, move the biggest disc, then move the n minus 1 back on top of it. Keep two numbers apart while it runs: how deep the stack gets, and how many calls happen in total.

@1-2
Moving 3 discs to C starts by moving 2 discs to B. That call starts by moving the smaller disc, disc 1, out of the way onto C. The stack is now 2 frames deep, each waiting.

@3-5
A single disc just moves. Disc 1 goes from A to C, move 1. Disc 2 is now free and goes from A to B, move 2. Then disc 1 is brought from C onto it, move 3.

@6
The two-disc call is complete, in 3 moves, and it returns to the top call.

@7
Disc 3, the biggest, is now free, and it goes straight from A to C. That is move 4, the only time it moves. What is left is the two-disc job again, from B to C.

@8-11
The same pattern with the pegs relabelled: disc 1 steps aside from B to A, move 5. Disc 2 goes from B to C, move 6. Disc 1 follows it from A to C, move 7.

@12-13
The second two-disc call completes in 3 moves, and then the top call completes: 3, plus 1, plus 3, which is 7.

@14
All three discs are on C after 7 moves, which is 2 to the 3, minus 1. The stack never held more than 3 frames, one per disc, so the depth is n and the stack is fine. But the call tree is a full binary tree, doubling in width at every level, so the time is exponential. Depth is the stack cost; the total number of calls is the time cost.
