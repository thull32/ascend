@0
The longest common subsequence of two strings: A, B, C, D, E down the rows, and A, C, E across the columns. Each cell holds the LCS length of a prefix of one against a prefix of the other. The recurrence looks at the last character of each prefix. If they match, that character can end the subsequence: take the diagonal cell and add 1. If they differ, at least one of them is not used, so drop one or the other and keep the better: the larger of the cell above and the cell to the left.

@1
Base cases: an empty prefix shares nothing with anything, so row 0 and column 0 are all zeros.

@2
The first cell compares A with A. A match, so it takes the diagonal, 0, plus 1: the cell holds 1.

@3-4
The rest of row A. A against C, then against E: both mismatches, so each takes the larger of up, 0, and left, 1. The 1 carries along the row.

@5-7
Row B matches nothing in A, C, E. Every cell takes the larger of its neighbours above and to the left, and the row stays at 1.

@8
Row C starts with C against A: a mismatch, and the 1 from above carries down.

@9
Then C against C: a match. The diagonal holds 1, so this cell is 2. That is the second shared character, A then C, in order.

@10
Row C ends with C against E, a mismatch: the larger of up, 1, and left, 2, gives 2.

@11-13
Row D matches nothing, so it copies its best neighbours: 1, then 2, then 2.

@14-15
Row E begins with two mismatches, E against A and E against C, giving 1 and 2.

@16
The answer cell: E against E, a match. The diagonal holds 2, so this cell is 3.

@17
The LCS has length 3, and the walk back is highlighted. Start at the answer cell. A match emits its character and steps diagonally; a mismatch steps toward the larger neighbour. From E, diagonal to the D row, which mismatches and steps up; then C, a match, diagonal; then the B row, which steps up; then A, a match. Read backwards, the emitted characters spell A, C, E. The table has one cell per pair of prefixes, so time and space are the product of the two lengths, and two rolling rows are enough if you only need the length.
