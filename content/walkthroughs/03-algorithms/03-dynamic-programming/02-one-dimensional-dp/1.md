@0
Five houses holding 2, 7, 9, 3 and 1, and you may never rob two neighbours. Cell i holds the best loot from houses 0 through i. The recurrence is one decision about the last house: skip it and keep the best from the houses before, cell i minus 1, or rob it and add its value to the best from two houses back, cell i minus 2. Take the larger.

@1
Base case: with only house 0, rob it. Cell 0 is 2.

@2
Base case: with houses 0 and 1, which are adjacent, take the better one. Cell 1 is the larger of 2 and 7, which is 7.

@3
House 2 holds 9. Robbing it gives 9 plus the 2 from two houses back, which is 11. Skipping it keeps 7. Rob wins: cell 2 is 11.

@4
House 3 holds 3. Robbing it gives 3 plus cell 1, which is 7, for 10. Skipping keeps 11. Skip wins, so cell 3 is 11, unchanged. The 3 is never worth losing the 9.

@5
House 4 holds 1. Robbing it gives 1 plus cell 2, which is 11, for 12. Skipping keeps 11. Rob wins: cell 4 is 12.

@6
The answer is the last cell, 12: houses 0, 2 and 4, for 2 plus 9 plus 1. To read the choice back, walk from the end: a cell that equals the one before it means that house was skipped, and a cell that changed means it was robbed. Each cell reads only the two before it, so two variables replace the whole row: linear time, constant space. Every plausible greedy rule for this problem has a counterexample; this two-way comparison at every house is what replaces them.
