@0
The longest increasing subsequence of 10, 9, 2, 5, 3, 7, 101 and 18. The state is the important part: cell i holds the length of the longest increasing subsequence that ends exactly at position i. Ending there fixes the last value, which is all a later element needs to know. The recurrence: look at every earlier position with a smaller value, take the best length found there, and add 1. If there is none, the cell is 1.

@1
Cell 0 starts at 1: 10 on its own is an increasing subsequence of length 1.

@2-3
Cell 1, value 9, also starts at 1. It scans to its left and finds 10, which is not smaller, so it cannot come before 9. The cell stays 1.

@4-6
Cell 2, value 2, starts at 1 and scans 10 and 9. Neither is smaller, so it stays 1.

@7-10
Cell 3, value 5, scans 10 and 9, which are too big, then reaches 2. Two is smaller, and the cell there holds 1, so 5 extends it: cell 3 becomes 2.

@11-15
Cell 4, value 3, does the same. It skips 10 and 9, extends the subsequence ending at 2 to length 2, and then skips 5, which is bigger than 3. Cell 4 is 2.

@16-21
Cell 5, value 7. The scan skips 10 and 9, extends from 2 to reach length 2, then from 5 to reach length 3. The 3 also comes before 7, but it offers only 3 again, no better. Cell 5 is 3: 2, 5, 7 for example.

@22-28
Cell 6, value 101, is larger than everything before it, so every earlier cell is a candidate. The length climbs as the scan finds better predecessors: 2 through 10, 3 through 5, and finally 4 through 7. Cell 6 is 4.

@29-36
Cell 7, value 18. The scan climbs the same way, 2 through 10, 3 through 5, 4 through 7, and then reaches 101, which is bigger than 18 and cannot come before it. Cell 7 is 4.

@37
The answer is the largest cell, 4. Following the recorded predecessors back from cell 6 gives 2, 5, 7, 101, one of several increasing subsequences of length 4. Here the last cell ties at 4, so reading it would happen to work, but the rule is the maximum over all cells: the longest subsequence can end anywhere, and returning the last cell is the classic LIS bug. Each cell scans everything to its left, so this is quadratic. Patience sorting with a binary search over pile tops brings it to n log n.
