@0
This is the naive recursive ways function for climbing stairs, computing ways of 6. Every call that is not a base case makes two more calls: ways of k needs ways of k minus 1 and ways of k minus 2. The base cases are ways of 1 and ways of 0, and both are 1. Keep one question in mind as the tree grows: how many different arguments are there, and how many calls?

@1-5
Ways of 6 calls ways of 5 first and leaves its own frame waiting. Ways of 5 calls ways of 4, which calls ways of 3, which calls ways of 2. Each call parks on the stack until its children answer. Five frames are now waiting, and nothing has returned yet.

@6-9
Ways of 2 calls ways of 1, a base case that returns 1, then ways of 0, which also returns 1. Ways of 2 adds them, gets 2, and returns.

@10-12
Back in ways of 3, which now calls ways of 1 and gets 1. Ways of 3 is 2 plus 1, which is 3.

@13
Back in ways of 4 with ways of 3 equal to 3. It still needs ways of 2.

@14-18
This is the second call to ways of 2. Its value, 2, was computed a moment ago, but naive recursion has no memory, so the whole subtree runs again: ways of 1, ways of 0, add, return 2.

@19
Ways of 4 is 3 plus 2, which is 5.

@20
Back in ways of 5, which now needs ways of 3.

@21-29
The second call to ways of 3, and inside it the third call to ways of 2. The same three-level subtree that was built at the start is rebuilt from scratch, call by call, and returns the same 3.

@30
Ways of 5 is 5 plus 3, which is 8. The left half of the tree is finished.

@31
Back at the root, ways of 6 now needs ways of 4, a value the left half already computed.

@32-48
So the entire ways of 4 subtree is recomputed. That is the second call to ways of 4, the third to ways of 3, and the fourth and fifth calls to ways of 2, each one descending all the way to the base cases again. It comes back with 5, the same answer as before.

@49
Ways of 6 is 8 plus 5, which is 13: thirteen ways to climb six stairs.

@50
Twenty-five calls, but only 7 distinct arguments, 0 through 6. The live stack never went deeper than 6 frames, so depth was never the problem; repetition was. The call count grows by about 1.6 times for every extra unit of n: the same tree for n equal to 40 has about 331 million calls. Store each of the 7 results the first time it is computed and every repeat becomes a lookup, which makes the whole thing linear. That gap, exponential calls against a handful of distinct subproblems, is the signal that a problem wants dynamic programming.
