@0
This is the naive recursive Fibonacci, computing fib of 6. Every call that is not a base case makes two more calls: fib of k needs fib of k minus 1 and fib of k minus 2. The base cases are fib of 1, which is 1, and fib of 0, which is 0. Keep one question in mind as the tree grows: how many different arguments are there, and how many calls?

@1-5
Fib of 6 calls fib of 5 first and leaves its own frame waiting. Fib of 5 calls fib of 4, which calls fib of 3, which calls fib of 2. Each call parks on the stack until its children answer. Five frames are now waiting, and nothing has returned yet.

@6-9
Fib of 2 calls fib of 1, a base case that returns 1, then fib of 0, which returns 0. Fib of 2 adds them, gets 1, and returns.

@10-12
Back in fib of 3, which now calls fib of 1 and gets 1. Fib of 3 is 1 plus 1, which is 2.

@13
Back in fib of 4 with fib of 3 equal to 2. It still needs fib of 2.

@14-18
This is the second call to fib of 2. Its value, 1, was computed a moment ago, but naive recursion has no memory, so the whole subtree runs again: fib of 1, fib of 0, add, return 1.

@19
Fib of 4 is 2 plus 1, which is 3.

@20
Back in fib of 5, which now needs fib of 3.

@21-29
The second call to fib of 3, and inside it the third call to fib of 2. The same three-level subtree that was built at the start is rebuilt from scratch, call by call, and returns the same 2.

@30
Fib of 5 is 3 plus 2, which is 5. The left half of the tree is finished.

@31
Back at the root, fib of 6 now needs fib of 4, a value the left half already computed.

@32-48
So the entire fib of 4 subtree is recomputed. That is the second call to fib of 4, the third to fib of 3, and the fourth and fifth calls to fib of 2, each one descending all the way to the base cases again. It comes back with 3, the same answer as before.

@49
Fib of 6 is 5 plus 3, which is 8.

@50
Twenty-five calls, but only 7 distinct arguments, 0 through 6. The live stack never went deeper than 6 frames, so depth was never the problem; repetition was. The call count grows by about 1.6 times for every extra unit of n: the same tree for n equal to 40 has about 331 million calls. Store each of the 7 results the first time it is computed and every repeat becomes a lookup, which makes the whole thing linear. That gap, exponential calls against a handful of distinct subproblems, is the signal that a problem wants dynamic programming.
