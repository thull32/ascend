@0
Climbing 7 stairs, one or two steps at a time. Cell i of this row holds the number of ways to reach stair i. The recurrence comes from asking about the last move: it came either from stair i minus 1 or from stair i minus 2, so cell i is the sum of the two cells to its left. Bottom-up means filling the row from the left, so both of those cells always exist before they are read.

@1
Base case: cell 0 is 1. There is exactly one way to stand at the bottom, which is to do nothing.

@2
Base case: cell 1 is 1, a single one-step.

@3
Cell 2 reads cells 1 and 0: 1 plus 1 is 2. Two one-steps, or one two-step.

@4
Cell 3 reads cells 2 and 1: 2 plus 1 is 3.

@5-8
The same move repeats along the row, each cell the sum of the two before it: 5 for stair 4, 8 for stair 5, 13 for stair 6, and 21 for stair 7. Every read lands on a cell filled in an earlier step, which is the whole point of the left-to-right order.

@9
The answer is the last cell: 21 ways to climb 7 stairs. That is linear time, and since each cell only reads the two before it, two variables are enough for constant space. The row is the Fibonacci sequence shifted by one. Why it is correct comes down to two words: the two last-move cases are disjoint, because a sequence has one last move, and exhaustive, because there are no other move sizes. So nothing is counted twice and nothing is missed.
