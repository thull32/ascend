@0
Eight values: 1, 3, minus 1, minus 3, 5, 3, 6, 7, and we want the maximum of every window of three. A sum can subtract the element that leaves; a maximum cannot, because when the current maximum leaves, a single number cannot tell you the next one. So keep a deque of indices whose values decrease from front to back. The front is always the window's maximum.

@1
Index 0, value 1, goes in at the back.

@2-3
Then value 3 arrives. It is at least as large as the 1 at the back, so the 1 is evicted: it can never be a window maximum while 3 is in the same window, and 3 will stay in the window longer. Then 3 is pushed.

@4-5
Minus 1 is smaller than 3, so it simply joins the back. The first window, positions 0 to 2, is complete, and its maximum is the front of the deque: 3.

@6-7
Minus 3 joins the back too, and the deque reads 3, minus 1, minus 3, still decreasing. The window from position 1 to 3 has maximum 3.

@8-11
Now 5 arrives, and it dominates everything. It evicts minus 3, then minus 1, then 3, all from the back, one at a time, and then 5 is pushed alone.

@12
The window from position 2 to 4 has maximum 5.

@13-14
Next, 3 is smaller than 5, so it joins the back. The window from 3 to 5 has maximum 5.

@15-18
Then 6 arrives, evicts 3 and then 5 from the back, and is pushed. The window from 4 to 6 has maximum 6.

@19-21
Finally 7 evicts 6 and is pushed. The last window, positions 5 to 7, has maximum 7.

@22
The maxima are 3, 3, 5, 5, 6 and 7. Some steps evicted three indices at once, but each index is pushed once and popped at most once, so the whole pass is order of n, not n times k. In this run every old maximum was evicted from the back before it could expire, so nothing ever left from the front. In general, when the left end of the window passes the front index, that index is popped from the front. For a constraint like maximum minus minimum, keep a second deque, increasing, for the minimum.
