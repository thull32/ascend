@0
Eight values: 10, 5, 7, 3, 4, 1, 6, 2, and we want the maximum of every window of three. A sum can subtract the element that leaves; a maximum cannot, because when the current maximum leaves, a single number cannot tell you the next one. So keep a deque of indices whose values decrease from front to back. The front is always the window's maximum.

@1-2
Index 0, value 10, goes in at the back. Then 5, which is smaller, joins behind it.

@3-4
Value 7 arrives. It is larger than the 5 at the back, so the 5 is evicted: it can never be a window maximum while 7 is in the same window, and 7 will stay in the window longer. Then 7 is pushed. The deque reads 10, 7.

@5
The first window, positions 0 to 2, is complete, and its maximum is the front: 10.

@6-8
Value 3 joins the back: 10, 7, 3. But the window is now positions 1 to 3, and the front, index 0, has slid out of it. So 10 is popped from the front. Nothing evicted it; it simply aged out. The window's maximum is the new front, 7.

@9-11
Value 4 evicts the 3 from the back and is pushed. The deque reads 7, 4, and the window from 2 to 4 has maximum 7.

@12-14
Value 1 joins the back: 7, 4, 1. Now index 2, the 7, leaves the window and is popped from the front. The window from 3 to 5 has maximum 4.

@15-18
Value 6 evicts the 1, then the 4, from the back, and is pushed alone. The window from 4 to 6 has maximum 6.

@19-20
Finally 2 joins behind 6, and the last window, positions 5 to 7, has maximum 6.

@21
The maxima are 10, 7, 7, 4, 6 and 6. Some steps evicted two indices at once, but each index is pushed once and popped at most once, from the back or the front, so the whole pass is order of n, not n times k. Twice an old maximum left from the front because the left end of the window passed it. For a constraint like maximum minus minimum, keep a second deque, increasing, for the minimum.
