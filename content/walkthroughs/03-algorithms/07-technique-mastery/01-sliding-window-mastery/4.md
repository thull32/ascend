@0
For aggregates like gcd or bitwise OR there is no subtraction and no monotone shortcut. The general fix is a queue built from two stacks: an in stack and an out stack. Enqueue pushes onto in. Dequeue pops from out, and only when out is empty does it refill, by draining in, which reverses the order back to first in, first out.

@1-3
Enqueue 12, then 18, then 8. Each is a single push onto in, constant time, and out is not touched. The newest item, 8, is on top of in.

@4-6
Now a dequeue, but out is empty. So transfer: pop 8 from in and push it onto out, then 18, then 12. Three moves, and the oldest item, 12, ends up on top of out.

@7
The dequeue pops 12 from the top of out. Two items remain there, so the next two dequeues are free.

@8
Enqueue 6. It goes onto in, as always; out is not touched.

@9-10
Dequeue returns 18, then 8, each a single pop from out. Neither touched in.

@11-12
The next dequeue finds out empty again, so 6 is moved across, one move, and popped.

@13
Count the work: four moves over eight operations. One dequeue cost three moves, but each element crosses from in to out at most once, so the cost is amortised constant per operation. For window aggregates, store beside each value the aggregate of itself and everything beneath it in its stack. Then the window's aggregate is the two stack tops combined, and the same moves maintain the gcd, the OR or the maximum of the whole window.
