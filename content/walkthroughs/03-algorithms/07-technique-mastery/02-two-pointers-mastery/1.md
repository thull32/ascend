@0
A sorted array, 1, 3, 4, 6, 8, 11, and we want two values that sum to 10. Picture the table of every pair: values grow along each row and down each column, because the array is sorted. The pointers start at the two ends. Hold onto one sentence: every comparison rules out a whole row or a whole column of that table.

@1
1 plus 11 is 12, too big. Even paired with the smallest value still in play, 11 overshoots, so 11 cannot be part of any remaining pair. Its whole column is gone, and the right pointer moves left.

@2
1 plus 8 is 9, too small. Even paired with the largest value still in play, 1 falls short, so its whole row is gone, and the left pointer moves right.

@3
3 plus 8 is 11, too big. The column for 8 goes, and the right pointer moves left.

@4
3 plus 6 is 9, too small. The row for 3 goes, and the left pointer moves right.

@5
4 plus 6 is 10. Found, at positions 2 and 3. Neither kind of move could ever discard the answer, and each one shrinks the remaining triangle of candidates by a row or a column, so the loop finishes within n minus 1 steps. That is the full answer to "how do you know you didn't skip the pair". The same walk on any matrix whose rows and columns are both sorted is called saddleback search.
