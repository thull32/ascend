@0
Eight coefficients, a0 to a7, of a polynomial A. The transform rests on one identity: A of x equals E of x squared, plus x times O of x squared, where E holds the even-indexed coefficients and O the odd-indexed ones. So each call splits its coefficients by position, evaluates both halves recursively, and combines them on the way back up.

@1
The root splits by parity, not by halves of the array: a0, a2, a4 and a6 go to E, and a1, a3, a5 and a7 go to O. E is evaluated first.

@2-3
The same rule applies inside E. Its even positions, a0 and a4, form the next E, and a2 and a6 form the next O. Then a0 and a4 split once more, into single coefficients. That is three levels of splitting for eight coefficients.

@4-5
A single coefficient is a constant polynomial, so its value is the coefficient itself. a0 and a4 return at once.

@6
The first combine. One butterfly takes E's value and O's value times a root of unity, omega, and emits their sum and their difference: E plus omega O, and E minus omega O. Two outputs from two inputs, the values of this small polynomial at two roots of unity.

@7-10
a2 and a6 split, return as constants, and combine with one butterfly the same way. Then their parent combines both pairs with two butterflies, giving four values. Each combine touches every value of its node exactly once.

@11
That completes E for the root: the even-indexed coefficients, evaluated at four roots of unity.

@12-21
The odd half does the same, level by level. a1, a3, a5 and a7 split into a1 and a5, and a3 and a7. Each pair splits into constants and combines with one butterfly, and then two butterflies combine the pairs into four values for O.

@22
The root's combine: four butterflies turn E's four values and O's four values into eight outputs, the values of A at the eight roots of unity. The sign flip is why each butterfly yields two outputs: A of minus x needs the same two half-size values as A of x.

@23
15 calls, three levels of splitting, and four butterflies at every level, 12 in all. Two calls on half the size plus linear combining is the recurrence of merge sort, which solves to n log n. The roots of unity are what let the halving continue all the way down, because their squares pair up as plus and minus again.
