@0
Eight values: 38, 27, 43, 3, 9, 82, 10 and 5. Merge sort splits the range in halves until every piece is a single element, then merges sorted halves on the way back up. Watch the tree rather than the values, and keep a tally of how much merging each level of the tree does.

@1-3
The root splits positions 0 to 7 into 0 to 3 and 4 to 7, and always sorts the left half first. Positions 0 to 3 split into 0 to 1 and 2 to 3, and positions 0 to 1 split into two single elements. That is three levels of splitting.

@4-6
A single element is already sorted, so 38 returns at once, and so does 27. These leaves are the base case, and they do no merge work at all.

@7-9
The first merge. 27 is smaller than 38, so 27 is written at position 0. The right side is now empty, so 38 follows at position 1. The node returns 27, 38, after 2 writes.

@10-17
The same for positions 2 and 3: split into 43 and 3, and merge them. 3 comes first, then 43. Another 2 writes.

@18-22
Now a merge one level up: 27, 38 against 3, 43. The fronts are 27 and 3, so 3 goes first. Then 27, then 38, and once the left side is used up, 43. Positions 0 to 3 read 3, 27, 38, 43, after 4 writes.

@23-31
The right half of the array gets the same treatment. Positions 4 to 7 split into 4 to 5 and 6 to 7, and 4 to 5 into single elements. 9 and 82 are already in order, so the merge writes 9, then 82.

@32-39
Positions 6 and 7 hold 10 and 5. The merge writes 5 first, then 10.

@40-44
Then the two pairs merge: 9, 82 against 5, 10. The writes go 5, 9, 10, and finally 82. Four writes, and positions 4 to 7 read 5, 9, 10, 82.

@45-53
The root's merge. 3 is the smaller front, then 5, 9 and 10 come from the right side, then 27, 38 and 43 from the left, and 82 is copied last. Eight writes, and the array is sorted.

@54
15 calls in all, and the tree has 4 levels counting the leaves. Now the tally: the bottom merging level did four merges of 2 writes, 8 in total. The next did two merges of 4, also 8. The root did one merge of 8. Every merging level moves all n elements, and there are log of n such levels, three here, so the total is n log n. Double n and you add exactly one level of n work. That is the habit to keep from this picture: when the work is the same on every level, the answer is that work times the number of levels.
