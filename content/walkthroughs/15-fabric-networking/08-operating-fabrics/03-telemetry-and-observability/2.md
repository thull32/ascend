@0
The same picture as before: leaf2.ams streaming its counters every 10 seconds to the collector, 10.250.1.10, at DSCP zero, the link to spine1 at 38 percent, and the out-of-band monitor on its separate management network. This time the silence has a different cause. Keep one idea in mind: the absence alert only says that something stopped, and it is the independent witnesses that say what.

@1
At five seconds past ten, leaf2 loses power. Every one of its links goes dark at once: both uplinks, the link to Globex's servers, and its management port. The gNMI stream stops mid-sample. On the chart, leaf2's line breaks off, and spine1 now reports zero on that link.

@2
At thirty seconds past ten, the collector has missed three samples in a row and raises the absence of data alert, exactly as in the congested case. Its table is identical too: last sample at ten o'clock, no data for 30 seconds. From the collector's own data, the two cases cannot yet be told apart.

@3
The same triage table, the same first row. The gNMI keepalive, an HTTP 2 ping, gets no answer, as before, and for the same reason it proves nothing: it shares the data path.

@4
The out-of-band monitor pings 172.16.1.12, and three echoes in a row time out, after a second each. Every other device on the same management switch still answers, so the problem is leaf2 itself, not the management network. That check matters, because a broken management network would look the same from here.

@5
The neighbours saw it go. spine1 and spine2 lost signal on their ports to leaf2, swp2 on each, at five seconds past ten. Their IS-IS adjacencies to leaf2 are down, LLDP no longer lists it, and the receive load from it is zero. Every observed cell in the table now matches the column for dead.

@6
Verdict, at thirty-five seconds past ten: dead. The platform pages the on-call engineer and ties the alert to the servers behind leaf2, which are cut off unless they are dual-homed to another leaf. The spines had already removed leaf2 from their routing within milliseconds of losing signal; the platform's job is to say why.

@7
Put the two scenarios side by side and the design is clear. The absence alert was the same in both; the out-of-band reply and the neighbours' view gave opposite answers. The two signals together also cover the harder case in between: a management port that answers while the neighbours see the adjacency down means the device is up but cut off from the fabric. A platform that reports streaming, blind but alive, and dead as separate states is one an on-call engineer can trust. It breaks when the out-of-band network quietly shares fate with the fabric, or when the neighbour check reads the silent device's own data.
