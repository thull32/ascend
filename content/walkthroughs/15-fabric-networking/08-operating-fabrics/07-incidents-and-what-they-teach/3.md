@0
On screen, leaf1.ams in Meridian's Amsterdam fabric, with four 400-gigabit uplinks, one per spine. It sends about 1,000 gigabits per second towards the other leaves, and ECMP gives each uplink a quarter of the flows, about 250 gigabits per second each. The idea to hold: a gray failure is a link that is neither working nor down, and ECMP keeps feeding it its full share for as long as routing says it is up.

@1
Errors are counted where a frame is received, so the counters that matter are on spine3.ams's port facing leaf1. Week zero is the baseline: minus 1.8 dBm of light per lane, and a bit error rate before correction of about one in a hundred million, normal for these lanes, which always need some correction. Forward error correction repairs those few errors, about 4,250 a second, so no frame fails its FCS check.

@2
Over two weeks the receive power falls to minus 4.6 dBm, as it does when a connector is contaminated, and the error rate before correction rises 300-fold, to 3 in a million. FEC still repairs every codeword: zero FCS errors, zero drops. Remember this frame. A 2.8 decibel drop and a 300-fold rise in errors, against this link's own baseline, is where an alert should have fired.

@3
Week four: minus 5.9 dBm, and an error rate of 9 in a hundred thousand, within a factor of three of the most FEC is specified to repair, about 2.4 in ten thousand. 38 million bit errors are being repaired every second, and the link still shows zero FCS errors and looks perfectly healthy.

@4
Week five: minus 6.8 dBm and an error rate of 1.5 in a thousand, six times the limit. FEC fails abruptly, not gradually. About one codeword in a hundred now has more errors than it can repair, and spine3 drops the 2 percent of leaf1's frames that fail their FCS check, about 625,000 a second. leaf1's own counters look perfect, because a sender never learns that the receiver threw its frames away.

@5
Nothing routes around it. The optic still delivers light, so the port stays up, and the routing hellos and BFD packets get through at least 98 times in a hundred. BFD declares a link down only after three misses in a row, which at 2 percent loss is about 8 chances in a million. So spine3 stays in the ECMP group and keeps receiving its quarter.

@6
The impact: one in four of leaf1's flows crosses this link and loses 2 percent of its packets. TCP throughput falls roughly with one over the square root of the loss rate, so those flows slow to a fraction of their normal speed, while the other three quarters are untouched. Averaged over the uplinks the loss is only half a percent, small enough to vanish on a dashboard of totals. One slow connection in four is exactly what Acme reported, and a single ping, one 5-tuple on one uplink, comes back clean three times in four.

@7
Now the signals. The module's own low-power warning, set by its vendor at minus 8.9 dBm to suit every cable plant, never fired at all. The alerts that would have fired from week two watch receive power and the error rate before correction against this link's own baseline. Here in week five, FCS errors on spine3's receive side, plus probes spread over every uplink in which only those through spine3 lose packets, pin the fault to this one link.

@8
With headroom checked, the operator costs the link out, raising its IS-IS metric at both ends far above any real path, so leaf1 drops spine3 from its ECMP group. Three uplinks now carry the 1,000 gigabits per second at 83 percent utilisation, tight but loss-free.

@9
A technician cleans the connector, receive power returns to minus 1.9 dBm and the error rate to its baseline, and after a soak with zero FCS errors the metric is restored. Four uplinks again, a quarter of the flows each, at 62.5 percent utilisation.

@10
The general lesson: a gray failure passes its own health checks while its users see it failing, and averages hide it, because the damage is concentrated on one member of a group. So show errors on the link with both ends, compare each optic with its own history rather than a vendor threshold, and group probe results by the path they took. Some operators automate the response: cost out any link whose error rate crosses a threshold, provided the remaining uplinks have the headroom to take its traffic.
