@0
The same slice of Amsterdam: the collector behind leaf4, subscribed to leaf2 over the data network with a sample every 10 seconds, and the out-of-band monitor reaching leaf2's management port, 172.16.1.12, through the management switch. The same question is coming, with the same two witnesses. The one idea: the alert cannot tell you what happened, only the witnesses can, and this time they say something different.

@1
At 10:00:05 leaf2 loses power. Its gNMI stream stops mid-sample, and so does everything else it was sending.

@2-3
From the collector's side, this looks exactly like the congested case. At 10:00:30, after three missed samples, the absence-of-data alert fires, and the gNMI keepalive goes unanswered, as it would either way. Up to this point nothing distinguishes dead from blind.

@4
The out-of-band ping does. Three echoes to 172.16.1.12 time out, a second each. Every other device on the same management switch answers, so the problem is leaf2 itself, not the management network. That comparison matters: on its own, a timed-out ping could just mean the out-of-band network broke.

@5
The neighbours agree. spine1 and spine2 lost signal on their ports to leaf2 at 10:00:05, their IS-IS adjacencies to it are down, the load arriving from it is zero, and LLDP no longer lists it.

@6
Verdict at 10:00:35: dead. The platform pages the on-call engineer and ties the alert to the servers behind leaf2, which are cut off unless they are dual-homed to another leaf. Routing did not wait for the verdict: the spines removed leaf2 within milliseconds of losing signal.

@7
Put the two witnesses side by side and they cover the harder cases too. A management port that answers while the neighbours see the adjacency down means the device is up but cut off from the fabric, the gray state in between. A management port that times out while the links stay up means it is still forwarding and only management is lost. A platform that reports streaming, blind but alive, and dead as separate states is one an on-call engineer can trust.
