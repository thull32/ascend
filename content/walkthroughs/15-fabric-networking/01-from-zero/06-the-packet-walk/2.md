@0
No router on a path knows the whole path, yet traceroute prints it. The trick is to expire probes on purpose. Send a probe with a TTL of 1, and the first router takes it to zero, drops it, and sends back an ICMP time exceeded message from its own address. Raise the TTL by one each round, and each round dies one router further on. On screen: you, three hops, and the target.

@1
The first probe goes out with a TTL of 1.

@2
Hop 1 cannot forward it, so it drops the probe and reports back with time exceeded. The reply quotes the start of the original probe, which is how traceroute matches it to the probe it sent. The round trip is about 1 millisecond, and hop 1 is on the map.

@3
The next probe has a TTL of 2, so it gets past hop 1.

@4
Hop 2 takes it to zero and reveals itself, at about 8 milliseconds.

@5
The third probe goes out with a TTL of 3.

@6
And nothing comes back before the timeout. Hop 3 rate-limits or filters its ICMP replies, so traceroute prints three stars on that line. This is not a broken path. The probe went through, and only the reply is missing. Routers forward transit packets in hardware but answer expired ones from their CPU, under a strict rate limit, so silence or a slow answer at one hop is about the control plane, not the link.

@7
A probe with a TTL of 4 passes straight through the silent hop 3 and reaches the target.

@8
The target answers differently. Classic traceroute sends UDP probes to high ports where nothing listens, so the destination replies port unreachable; ICMP probes get an echo reply instead. That different answer is how traceroute knows it has arrived.

@9
Read the output with care. A spike at one hop that does not persist to the hops after it is a slow responder, not a slow link: only latency or loss that lasts to the destination is real. The return path can differ from the forward path. In a leaf-spine fabric, each probe is a new flow that equal-cost routing may hash onto a different spine, so one hop line can show several addresses, which is why Paris traceroute holds the flow fixed. Tunnels can hide hops entirely. And for continuous per-hop loss and latency, use mtr.
