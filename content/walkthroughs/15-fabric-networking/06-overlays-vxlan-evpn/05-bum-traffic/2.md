@0
On screen, Meridian's VNI 10100, Acme's VLAN 100, which spans five VTEPs: leaf1, leaf3 and leaf4 in Amsterdam, and leaf1 and leaf2 in Singapore. leaf2 Amsterdam carries only Globex and has no VNI 10100. Two Amsterdam spines and spine1 Singapore are drawn, joined by the 10,500 kilometre circuit. Hold onto one idea: every receiving VTEP gets exactly one copy of a flooded frame whichever design you pick. The only question is where the copies are made, at the ingress leaf or inside the underlay.

@1
First, ingress replication. Every VTEP with VNI 10100 advertised one EVPN type-3 route, the inclusive multicast Ethernet tag route, which says: send me this VNI's broadcast, unknown unicast and multicast traffic. Its PMSI tunnel attribute, type 6, asks for ingress replication. leaf1 Amsterdam turned the four it imported into its flood list: 10.255.1.13 for leaf3, 10.255.1.14 for leaf4, and 10.255.2.11 and 10.255.2.12 for the two Singapore leaves. leaf2 Amsterdam sent none, so it is not on the list.

@2
acme-web1 ARPs for 10.1.1.50, an address no leaf has learned, because the host is silent or not up yet. ARP suppression has no answer, so this broadcast must reach every VTEP in the segment.

@3
leaf1 makes one copy per entry on its flood list, four in all. Each is an ordinary unicast VXLAN packet of 110 bytes, the padded 60 byte ARP frame plus 50 bytes of outer headers, with its own outer destination. The copy shown goes to leaf3, 10.255.1.13.

@4
All four copies leave on leaf1's own uplinks, 440 bytes for one 60 byte frame: the ingress leaf did all of the replication. The outer destinations differ, so the underlay hash spreads them. Here the copy for leaf4 takes spine2, and the other three take spine1.

@5
The spines forward each copy as plain unicast on its outer address. One goes down to leaf3, one down to leaf4, and two cross the circuit to Singapore, because each Singapore VTEP gets its own copy even though both share the same long link.

@6
Each receiving VTEP strips the outer headers and floods the ARP to its own VLAN 100 ports, never back into the fabric. leaf2 Amsterdam never sees a copy. Where a server is dual-homed, as acme-web2 is, only the designated forwarder passes it down.

@7
Now scale it. A VNI on n VTEPs costs n minus 1 copies of every BUM frame at the ingress leaf. At 16 VTEPs that is 15 copies, 1,650 bytes on the uplinks for one frame. At 256 VTEPs, 255 copies and 28,050 bytes. In return, the underlay stays unicast only and holds no multicast state at all.

@8
The alternative is underlay multicast. VNI 10100 is mapped to the group 239.1.1.100, and PIM sparse mode runs on every underlay router, with the rendezvous point on spine1 Amsterdam. Each VTEP in the VNI joins the group, and the joins build one tree from the rendezvous point down to exactly those VTEPs. spine1 Amsterdam holds a shared tree entry for the group, sending to the three Amsterdam leaves and to spine1 Singapore, which sends to both Singapore leaves. spine2 holds nothing: no receiver joined through it. Where type-3 routes are still sent, their tunnel type becomes 4, a sparse mode tree, carrying the group address.

@9
leaf1 sends the same broadcast once: a single VXLAN packet to 239.1.1.100, with the multicast MAC ending in 01 01 64, built from the group's low 23 bits. Strictly, the very first packets reach the rendezvous point wrapped in unicast Register messages, until spine1 joins a source tree back towards leaf1. Because the rendezvous point sits on leaf1's own uplink, that tree is this same link.

@10
spine1 Amsterdam copies the packet only where the tree branches: one down to leaf3, one down to leaf4, and a single copy across the circuit. It does not send one back to leaf1, where it came from. The replication has moved from the ingress leaf into the underlay routers, which now have to hold state for every group.

@11
spine1 Singapore makes the last two copies, for leaf1 and leaf2 there. Every VTEP still received exactly one copy. But leaf1's uplinks carried one packet instead of four, and the circuit one instead of two.

@12
The comparison. Ingress replication puts 4 copies on leaf1's uplinks and 2 on the circuit, and needs only type-3 routes. Multicast puts 1 on each, but needs PIM, a rendezvous point and a VNI to group mapping, holds a shared tree entry per group plus a source tree entry per sending VTEP, and fails as rendezvous point faults and partial trees. Most large EVPN fabrics choose ingress replication: after ARP suppression BUM traffic is a trickle, chips replicate at line rate, and there is one protocol fewer to run. Multicast pays off only where segments span hundreds of VTEPs and carry heavy flooding or real tenant multicast. The deciding variable is the BUM rate, not the size of the fabric.
