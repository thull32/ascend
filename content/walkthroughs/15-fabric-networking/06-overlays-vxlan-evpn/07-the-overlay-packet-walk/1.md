@0
acme-web1 in Amsterdam is about to send to acme-web2 in Singapore. Both are in Acme's subnet, 10.1.1.0 slash 24, on VLAN 100, which the fabric carries as VNI 10100. Between them: leaf 1 in Amsterdam, a spine in each site, the 10,500 kilometre circuit, and leaf 2 in Singapore, in a small example with two spines per site. The link labels are IS-IS metrics, 10 inside a site and 1,000 on each long-haul circuit. Hold onto one idea, because it is the whole walk: tenant state is read only at the edges, and every device in the middle reads one field, the outer destination address.

@1
Lookup 1 happens on acme-web1 itself, in its routing table. 10.1.1.11 falls inside its connected subnet, so no router is involved, and the frame must be addressed to acme-web2's own MAC, not the gateway's. Its ARP cache holds only the gateway, so it has to ask.

@2
acme-web1 broadcasts an ARP request, who has 10.1.1.11, into VLAN 100. Leaf 1 receives it on access port 1. Flooded, it would be copied to every VTEP in the segment, across the circuit too.

@3
Instead comes lookup 2. Leaf 1's ARP suppression table already pairs 10.1.1.11 with the MAC ending in 53 0B, taken from leaf 2's type 2 route. So leaf 1 answers on acme-web2's behalf, and nothing is flooded to Singapore.

@4
acme-web1 caches the answer and sends its data: an Ethernet frame to the MAC ending in 53 0B, carrying a 1,500 byte IPv4 packet with a time to live of 64, and a TCP segment for port 443.

@5
Lookups 3 and 4 happen inside leaf 1. The port map says access port 1, in VLAN 100, belongs to VNI 10100: from here on this frame is Acme, segment 10100. Then the MAC table for that segment: the destination sits behind remote VTEP 10.255.2.12, leaf 2 in Singapore, learned from its type 2 route with route distinguisher 10.255.2.12 colon 10100. The source MAC is already known, so nothing new is advertised.

@6
Lookup 5. That VTEP is a BGP next hop, so leaf 1 resolves it in its underlay table, where IS-IS offers two equal-cost paths of metric 1,020, 10 plus 1,000 plus 10, one through each spine. Leaf 1 wraps the frame: VXLAN with VNI 10100, UDP to port 4789 from source port 58479, and an outer IPv4 header from its own VTEP, 10.255.1.11, to 10.255.2.12. The hash of those outer headers picks spine 1 in Amsterdam.

@7
The tunnel packet leaves leaf 1 at 1,550 bytes, with an outer time to live of 64. From here on, only the outer headers are read. Every underlay link must carry 1,550 byte IP packets, which is why fabrics run a jumbo MTU such as 9,216 bytes. At the default 1,500, this packet would be dropped.

@8
Lookup 6, on spine 1 in Amsterdam. It matches the outer destination against IS-IS. Straight over its own circuit to spine 1 in Singapore costs 1,010. The detour down to a leaf and over spine 2's circuit costs 1,030, so it is not used. The spine decrements the outer time to live to 63, rewrites the outer MACs, and touches nothing else.

@9
Then the circuit: 10,500 kilometres of fibre at about 5 microseconds per kilometre, so about 52.5 milliseconds one way. That is more than every lookup on the path put together.

@10
Lookup 7, on spine 1 in Singapore. 10.255.2.12 slash 32 is one hop away, through leaf 2's end of the 10.254.17.2 slash 31 link, at metric 10. Outer time to live 62, outer MACs rewritten again. The VXLAN header and the inner frame are still exactly the bytes leaf 1 wrote.

@11
Down the last underlay link to leaf 2, which owns the outer destination address.

@12
Lookup 8, on leaf 2 in Singapore. The outer destination is its own VTEP, the UDP port is 4789, which says VXLAN, and VNI 10100 maps to its local VLAN 100. Some platforms also check that the source, 10.255.1.11, is a VTEP they know, here from leaf 1's type 3 route. Then it strips all 50 bytes of outer headers.

@13
Lookup 9: the MAC ending in 53 0B is on a local port. Leaf 2 does not learn acme-web1's MAC from this tunnelled frame, because EVPN already told it that MAC lives behind 10.255.1.11. With EVPN, MACs behind remote VTEPs come from BGP, not from the data plane.

@14
Leaf 2 bridges the frame out of acme-web2's port, still with acme-web1's source MAC and a time to live of 64, byte for byte what acme-web1 sent. Nothing routed it, so nothing decremented it.

@15
Nine lookups on five devices. The host and the two leaves read tenant state: a routing table, ARP, the VNI map and MAC tables that EVPN filled. The two spines read only the outer destination address in a table IS-IS filled. That is the pattern to carry into an interview: EVPN state at the edges, underlay state at every hop. When this path breaks, each row is a place to look, and the platform should be able to show every one of them.
