@0
This is Meridian's overlay control plane in a small example. Two route reflectors, rr1 in Amsterdam and rr1 in Singapore, peer with each other. Leaf 1 and leaf 2 in Amsterdam are clients of the Amsterdam reflector, and leaf 2 in Singapore is a client of the Singapore one. The dashed lines are iBGP sessions in AS 65000, on TCP port 179, riding through the spines. Leaf 2 in Amsterdam carries only Globex. Hold onto one idea: in EVPN a leaf learns a host on its own port once, from one frame, and tells every other leaf in a BGP route, so nobody has to flood to find it.

@1
acme-web1, at 10.1.1.10, boots and ARPs for its gateway, 10.1.1.1. The broadcast's source MAC ends in 53 0A, and the ARP sender fields pair that MAC with 10.1.1.10.

@2
Leaf 1 in Amsterdam learns two facts from that single frame: the MAC is on access port 1 in VLAN 100, which is VNI 10100, and 10.1.1.10 belongs to that MAC. Its MAC table and its ARP table for VRF ACME each gain a row. The gateway address is the anycast gateway that every Acme leaf owns, so leaf 1 answers the ARP itself, with the gateway MAC ending in 01 01.

@3
Now leaf 1 turns what it learned into one BGP EVPN route of type 2, a MAC and IP advertisement, carried in address family 25 with subsequent address family 70, which together mean EVPN. Its route distinguisher is the leaf's loopback and the VNI, 10.255.1.11 colon 10100, which makes it unique across the fabric. It carries the MAC, the IP, label 1 holding the bridging VNI 10100, label 2 holding Acme's routed VNI 50001, and a next hop of leaf 1's own VTEP, 10.255.1.11. Two route targets, 65000 colon 10100 and 65000 colon 50001, say who should import it.

@4
The update goes to rr1 in Amsterdam, and an identical copy to rr2. Without reflectors, leaf 1 would need a session to every other leaf, because an iBGP speaker never passes on a route it learned from another iBGP peer.

@5
The reflector adds two attributes: an originator ID of 10.255.1.11, who first sent the route, and a cluster list holding its own cluster ID, 10.255.1.101, so a copy that loops back can be recognised and dropped. It leaves the next hop alone and installs nothing. A reflector has no VNIs, keeps routes for every route target, and sits outside the data path.

@6
A route from a client is reflected to clients and non-clients alike, so it goes to leaf 2 in Amsterdam and across to the Singapore reflector. Each receiver now decides for itself whether the route concerns it.

@7
Leaf 2 in Amsterdam imports only Globex's route targets, 65000 colon 10200 and 65000 colon 50002. Neither matches, so it discards the route. Globex's segment never learns an Acme MAC, even though both tenants use 10.1.1.0 slash 24. That is how route targets keep tenants apart.

@8
The Singapore reflector learned the route from a non-client, so it reflects it only to its own clients, adding its cluster ID in front: the cluster list is now 10.255.2.101, then 10.255.1.101. The next hop is still leaf 1's 10.255.1.11. The session between the reflectors crosses the Amsterdam to Singapore circuit, so that one step costs about 52 and a half milliseconds.

@9
Leaf 2 in Singapore imports it: 65000 colon 10100 matches its MAC table for VNI 10100, and the next hop resolves through the IS-IS underlay. It installs the MAC ending in 53 0A behind VTEP 10.255.1.11, and through label 2 and the other route target, the host route 10.1.1.10 slash 32 in VRF ACME. Every other leaf with VNI 10100 does the same from its own copy.

@10
Later, acme-web2 wants to talk to 10.1.1.10 and broadcasts an ARP request for it. Leaf 2 in Singapore intercepts it, because its ARP suppression table, filled from the same type 2 route, already pairs 10.1.1.10 with the MAC ending in 53 0A.

@11
So the leaf answers on acme-web1's behalf. The request is never replicated to other VTEPs: no copy crosses the fabric or the long-haul circuit, and acme-web1 never sees it. The count of ARP requests flooded into VNI 10100 stays at zero.

@12
acme-web2's first frame hits the installed entry and is tunnelled straight to VTEP 10.255.1.11. One MAC learned on one port became one BGP route, and every leaf that needed it knew where the host lives before anyone sent it a frame. Notice where this can fail with every session still green: a route sent but not imported because of a route target, or imported but not installed because its next hop does not resolve. The signal is per VNI route counts on each leaf compared with what the reflector holds.
