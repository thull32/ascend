@0
On screen, Acme's web server acme-web2, at 10.1.1.11, bonded with LACP to two leaves in Singapore: leaf 1 at 10.255.2.11 and leaf 2 at 10.255.2.12. Leaf 1 in Amsterdam, with acme-web1 behind it, is a remote VTEP in VNI 10100, and the dashed lines are iBGP sessions. Both Singapore leaves give their bond port the same ethernet segment identifier, a type 3 ESI built from a shared system MAC ending in 53 7E and local discriminator 1, and send the same LACP system ID, so the server sees one partner. Hold onto one idea: two independent leaves become one attachment because they say so in BGP, with no cable between them.

@1
First the members find each other. Each leaf advertises a type 4 route, the ethernet segment route. Its ES-Import route target is taken from the ESI itself, so only leaves with a port on this segment import it. The Singapore leaves discover each other, and leaf 1 in Amsterdam ignores both routes, which is normal.

@2
Now the election. Each leaf waits 3 seconds for every peer's type 4 route, then computes on its own: sort the members by address, 10.255.2.11 at position 0 and 10.255.2.12 at position 1, and take the VLAN, 100, or the VNI, 10100, modulo 2, the number of members. Both are even, so position 0 wins. Leaf 1 is the designated forwarder, the DF: the only member that sends broadcast, unknown unicast and multicast traffic from the fabric down to the server. Both leaves agree without negotiating, because they computed from the same list.

@3
Each leaf also sends two type 1 routes, Ethernet auto-discovery. The per ES route carries the all-active flag, and remote leaves use it for mass withdrawal. The per EVI route, carrying VNI 10100, is the one they use for aliasing. Both reach leaf 1 in Amsterdam.

@4
acme-web2 sends its first frame, and the server's LACP hash puts it on the link to leaf 2. Leaf 2 learns the MAC ending in 53 0B and advertises a type 2 route for it, with next hop 10.255.2.12 and the segment's ESI in place of zero. Leaf 1 has never seen a frame from that MAC.

@5
It does not need to. The reflector passes the type 2 route to leaf 1 as well, which recognises its own ESI and installs the MAC on its local bond port, without waiting to see a frame.

@6
Now the remote side. Leaf 1 in Amsterdam heard the MAC from leaf 2 only. But per EVI type 1 routes from both Singapore leaves say they reach that segment in VNI 10100, so it installs the MAC behind both VTEPs, 10.255.2.11 and 10.255.2.12. That is aliasing.

@7
acme-web1's flows now hash over both VTEPs: flow 1 to leaf 1, flow 2 to leaf 2, and both bond links carry traffic. Without aliasing, every flow would go to leaf 2, the leaf that happened to learn the MAC, and leaf 1's link would sit idle.

@8
Flooded traffic is the DF's job. acme-web1 sends a broadcast, and ingress replication sends a copy to every VTEP in VNI 10100, so both Singapore leaves get one. Leaf 1, the DF, sends it down the bond, and leaf 2 drops its copy. acme-web2 receives it exactly once.

@9
The other direction needs a different rule. A broadcast from acme-web2 lands on leaf 2, which floods it to its local ports and to every VTEP, leaf 1 included. Leaf 1 is the DF, but the outer source address is 10.255.2.12, a leaf on the same segment, so it does not send the copy back down to acme-web2. This split horizon rule for VXLAN is called local bias.

@10
Now the failure. The link between acme-web2 and leaf 2 goes down. The server's LACP drops that member and sends everything to leaf 1, and leaf 2 has lost its only port on the segment. But leaf 1 in Amsterdam still lists both VTEPs, so flows hashed to 10.255.2.12 reach a leaf that cannot deliver them.

@11
Leaf 2 withdraws its per ES type 1 route, a single message, and every remote leaf removes 10.255.2.12 from every MAC on that segment at once. Leaf 1 in Amsterdam now shows only 10.255.2.11. If 500 MACs sat behind the bond, say a hypervisor's virtual machines, this one withdrawal does the work of 500 type 2 withdrawals. That is mass withdrawal. Leaf 2's type 4 route goes too, so leaf 1 in Singapore is the only member and DF for every VLAN.

@12
Both flows now hash to 10.255.2.11 and reach acme-web2 through leaf 1, after one withdrawal instead of one per MAC. The bookkeeping follows: leaf 2 withdraws its type 2 route, and leaf 1, which now receives all of acme-web2's frames, advertises the MAC itself. Forwarding did not wait for that.

@13
Step back. EVPN multihoming does with standard BGP routes what MLAG does with a vendor protocol over a peer link: no peer link, two or more leaves per segment instead of exactly two, and remote leaves that see two VTEPs and alias across them, not one shared anycast VTEP. Because every piece is in the control plane, it can be measured: the DF per segment, which exactly one member must claim; both members' type 1 routes at every remote leaf, since when they go missing every session stays up and one link quietly carries everything; and bond members that are down while the leaf is still up.
