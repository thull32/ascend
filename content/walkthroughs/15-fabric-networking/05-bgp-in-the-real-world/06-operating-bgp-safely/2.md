@0
The same job, gw1 out for an upgrade, done in the right order. The same picture: users in AS 64511, ISP1 and ISP2, gw1 and gw2, two spines, and leaf1 with the servers at 203.0.113.0 slash 26. Today everything enters and leaves through ISP1 and gw1. The one idea is make before break: get every other router to prefer another path while the old one still works, check that nothing is left, and only then take the path away.

@1
gw1's two dependencies again. Its slash 24 announcement to ISP1 rides on a static discard route, and its route to the servers needs IS-IS to resolve leaf1's loopback, 10.255.1.11. ISP1 prefers its customer gw1, at a local preference of 200, over the prepended path through its peer ISP2, at 100.

@2
Step one is BGP. gw1 re-announces the slash 24 to ISP1 tagged with the graceful shutdown community, the well-known value 65535 colon 0 from RFC 8326. The update on screen carries the prefix, an AS path of just 64500, and that community, highlighted. It asks the receiver to lower the route's local preference to 0, and ISP1 must be set up to honour it, as many transit providers are. Inside Meridian, gw1 gives its internet routes a local preference of 0 as well, and its own table now shows them at 0. Notice what does not happen: nothing is withdrawn, so the old path keeps carrying traffic.

@3
Inbound moves first, and loses nothing. ISP1's table now holds its customer path at 0 and the path through ISP2 at 100, and 100 wins. AS 64511 switches to its own path through ISP2. Packets for 203.0.113.10 now enter through ISP2 and gw2, and go down spine2 to leaf1. The switch happened while gw1's path still worked, which is why no packet fell in between.

@4
Outbound follows. On screen is gw2's view: the iBGP route from gw1 through ISP1, at 0, and its own eBGP route through ISP2, at 100, now best. The route reflectors pass gw2's routes to every leaf, and leaf1 sends its outbound traffic up through spine2 to gw2. A plain withdrawal could not have done this cleanly. Before the drain, gw2's best path was gw1's, so gw2 advertised nothing, and the alternative would have had to be discovered after the old path was already gone.

@5
Now the gate, which is a measurement, not a timer. The engineer checks gw1's interface counters, sees them fall to almost nothing, 0 bits per second on screen, and only then closes the eBGP session to ISP1. ISP1 deletes a path it was no longer using, and its table is left with just the path through ISP2. Nothing moves.

@6
Step two is the IGP. gw1's IS-IS adjacencies to spine1 and spine2 are shut, and its loopback, 10.255.1.201, leaves IS-IS. This is exactly the action that caused the outage when it came first. Now no BGP route points at gw1 and no traffic crosses it, so not a single packet is lost. The checklist reads: eBGP closed, internal routes at local preference 0 and unused, IS-IS shut, traffic 0. The gateway is ready for the upgrade.

@7
Coming back runs in reverse. IS-IS first, so the iBGP sessions between loopbacks can come up. Then BGP, with the graceful shutdown community still attached, so routes load while nobody prefers them. Then a wait until gw1 has learned the full table again, and only then is the community removed, so traffic returns while gw2's paths still work. Attracting traffic before the routes are in place would recreate the black hole; the IS-IS overload bit, held after a reboot until BGP has converged, exists for exactly that. The pattern holds for every router that speaks BGP: remove what attracts traffic, prove the device is empty, then remove what delivers it, and restore in the opposite order.
