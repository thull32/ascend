@0
This is drill three, the server that cannot reach its default gateway. acme-web1, at 10.1.1.10 slash 24 with gateway 10.1.1.1, has just booted with an empty ARP cache, and an application opens a connection to acme-db1 at 10.1.2.20 on port 5432. Keep one idea in mind as it plays out: the gateway is one hop away, so no IS-IS, no BGP and no spine is involved, and the first test that fails names the layer.

@1
First the host decides: is 10.1.2.20 on my subnet? ANDed with the mask, it gives 10.1.2.0, not acme-web1's own 10.1.1.0. So the connected route does not match, the default route does, and the packet goes to the gateway, 10.1.1.1.

@2
To build the frame, acme-web1 needs the gateway's MAC, and its cache has no entry. The kernel creates one in state INCOMPLETE and holds the TCP SYN in a small queue while it asks.

@3-4
Layer 2. The ARP request goes to the broadcast address, all ones: who has 10.1.1.1, tell 10.1.1.10. The target MAC is all zeros, because that is the question. leaf1 floods it through VLAN 100, and would hand a copy to its gateway interface for the subnet, if it had one.

@5
acme-app1 ignores the broadcast, since 10.1.1.1 is not its address. And in this run the VLAN 100 gateway interface on leaf1 is missing, a change left half done. Nobody owns 10.1.1.1, so no reply comes back.

@6-7
Linux sends up to three broadcast probes, one second apart. The second goes out at one second, the third and last at two seconds, and neither is answered while the SYN waits in the queue.

@8
At three seconds the entry becomes FAILED, the queued SYN is thrown away, and the connect call returns No route to host. A ping would print Destination Host Unreachable, from 10.1.1.10, the server's own address. That is the giveaway: the error comes from acme-web1, not from the network, and no packet to acme-db1 was ever sent.

@9
This is the pivot of the model answer. INCOMPLETE then FAILED means requests left and nothing came back, so the fault is at layer 2 or the gateway, and the frame lists the order: is the link up, is the server's port in the right VLAN, does the gateway interface exist and is it up, and does a capture on the server show requests leaving and nothing returning. The order works because each step can only pass once the step below it does: ARP cannot succeed on a dead link, and a ping cannot succeed without ARP.

The two slips this picture prevents: opening with the IS-IS adjacencies or the BGP summary, which tells the interviewer you do not know where the gateway is; and the opposite case, where ARP does resolve to the anycast gateway MAC and the ping still fails. Then layer 2 works, the leaf may be rate-limiting pings to its own CPU, and the next test is a ping through the gateway, not to it.
