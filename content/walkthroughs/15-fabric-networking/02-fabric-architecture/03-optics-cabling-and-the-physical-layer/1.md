@0
On screen is Meridian's Amsterdam fabric: leaf1 has four uplinks, one to each spine, each a 400 gig D R 4 optic with four lanes of 100 gigabits per second over single-mode fibre. leaf1 sends about 1,000 gigabits per second towards the other leaves, and ECMP gives each uplink a quarter of the flows, about 250 gigabits per second. Hold onto one idea, because it is the whole failure: routing only asks whether a link is up or down, and this link is about to be neither.

@1
Week zero. Errors are counted where a frame is received, so the counters that matter for traffic from leaf1 are on spine3's port facing it. Light power is measured in d B m, decibels relative to one milliwatt. Each lane receives minus 1.8 d B m, and the bit error rate before correction is about one in a hundred million. That is normal, because 100 gig PAM4 lanes always make some errors. Forward error correction, a Reed-Solomon code called K P 4, repairs those 4,250 bit errors a second, so no frame fails its checksum.

@2
Over two weeks the received light falls to minus 4.6 d B m: a contaminated connector, or a fibre bent too tightly in a tray. The error rate before correction rises 300 times, to three in a million: over a million bit errors a second. Error correction still repairs every codeword. Zero checksum errors, zero drops, and nothing at the IP layer has noticed.

@3
Week four, minus 5.9 d B m. The error rate is nine in a hundred thousand, within a factor of three of 2.4 in ten thousand, the highest rate at which K P 4 is specified to deliver a practically error-free link. It is repairing 38 million bit errors a second. This is the earliest strong warning, and the link still shows zero checksum errors and looks perfectly healthy.

@4
Week five, minus 6.8 d B m, and an error rate of 1.5 in a thousand, six times the limit. Error correction fails abruptly, not gradually: about one codeword in a hundred now has more than 15 bad symbols, roughly 680,000 a second, and every frame touching one fails its checksum. spine3 drops 2 percent of the frames from leaf1, about 625,000 a second. leaf1's own counters look perfect: a sender never learns that its frames were thrown away.

@5
Nothing routes around it: there is still light, so the port stays up. The IS-IS hellos, L L D P and BFD packets get through at least 98 percent of the time, and the short ones more often still, since a short packet has fewer bits to hit. BFD declares a link down only after three misses in a row, and at 2 percent loss that happens with a probability of about 8 in a million per window. So spine3 stays in the ECMP group and keeps receiving its 25 percent.

@6
The damage lands unevenly. One in four of leaf1's flows crosses this link and loses 2 percent of its packets, and TCP throughput falls roughly with one over the square root of the loss rate, so those flows slow to a fraction of their normal speed. Averaged over leaf1's uplinks the loss is only half a percent, small enough to vanish on a dashboard of totals, yet one slow request in four is exactly what users report.

@7
The alerts that would have fired weeks earlier watch the error rate before correction and the received light against this link's own baseline, both moving the wrong way since week two. The module's own low-power warning, set by its vendor at minus 8.9 d B m, never fired at all. Now the checksum errors on spine3's receive side, plus probes spread over every uplink, where only those through spine3 lose packets, pin the fault to this one link.

@8
The operator costs the link out: its IS-IS metric is raised at both ends, far above any real path. leaf1 drops spine3 from its ECMP group, and three uplinks carry the 1,000 gigabits per second at 83 percent, tight but loss-free.

@9
A technician cleans the connector, or swaps the optic. Received light is back to minus 1.9 d B m and the error rate to its baseline. After a soak period with zero checksum errors, the metric is restored, and leaf1's group is back to four members at 25 percent each, 62.5 percent utilisation.

@10
That is a gray failure: a component that is neither working nor down, and ECMP makes it worse by feeding the sick link its full share for as long as routing says it is up. Error correction turns a slow decline into a cliff, so zero checksum errors does not mean healthy; received light and the error rate before correction, against the link's own baseline, are the margin gauges. Some operators automate the response, costing out any link whose error rate crosses a threshold, provided the remaining uplinks have the headroom to take its traffic.
