@0
Back to four spines and four leaves, sixteen links, every leaf wired to every spine. The run that follows is the same failure as with two spines, and the idea is the same: losing one spine costs every leaf the share of its uplink that went through it. With four spines that share is a quarter, and that is what makes it survivable.

@1-2
leaf1 has four equal two-hop paths to leaf3, one per spine, each costing 20. It installs all four next hops for 10.1.2.0 slash 24 as one ECMP group.

@3-4
Flow A hashes modulo 4 to 0, goes up to spine1 and down to leaf3: two hops.

@5-9
The other flows spread out: flow B to spine2, C to spine3, D to spine4, then E joins B on spine2, and F joins C on spine3.

@10
Six flows, split one, two, two and one over spine1 to spine4. Lumpy, because there are only six of them.

@11
Before any failure, leaf1 sends 1,000 gigabits per second towards the spines over 1,600 gigabits per second of uplink: 62.5 percent, the same utilisation as the two-spine run, spread over all four spines.

@12
spine2 loses power, and every leaf's link to it goes dark at once. Each leaf sees loss of signal on its own port within milliseconds, and removes that next hop locally, before any routing message arrives.

@13
The ECMP group shrinks from four next hops to three. Every leaf still reaches every other leaf in two hops, through the three spines that remain, so there is still redundancy.

@14
Flows are now rehashed modulo 3, over spine1, spine3 and spine4. Flows B and E had to move, because they were on spine2: B goes to spine4 and E to spine3. But plain modulo hashing also moved flow F, which was on a healthy spine, from spine3 to spine1. Resilient hashing, from the lesson on ECMP and hashing, avoids that. Packets in flight on spine2 are lost and TCP retransmits them, and a flow moved between healthy spines may see a few packets out of order. No connection breaks, and the six flows now sit two on each surviving spine.

@15
leaf1 now has three uplinks of 400 gigabits per second, 1,200 in all: losing one spine cost a quarter of the capacity, not the fabric. The chart shows the capacity line stepping down from 1,600 to 1,200 while the offered load stays at 1,000. That is 83.3 percent utilisation, close enough to full that bursts will queue, but no longer more than the links can carry.

@16
When spine2 is repaired, it rejoins every leaf's ECMP group and flows rehash modulo 4; planned work drains a spine first instead of letting it fail like this. Put the two runs side by side: losing one of N spines multiplies utilisation by N over N minus 1. From 62.5 percent, two spines go to 125 percent and drop packets, four go to 83 percent, and eight would go to about 71 percent. That is why fabrics are built with four, eight or sixteen spines, and why the signal to watch after any failure is ECMP width per leaf and uplink utilisation against what the survivors can carry.
