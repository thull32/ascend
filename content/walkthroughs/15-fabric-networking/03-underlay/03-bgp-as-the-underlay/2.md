@0
The same small Amsterdam fabric, two spines and four leaves, eight external BGP sessions and no IS-IS, but with a shortcut some operators take: the spines share AS 65100, and every leaf uses the same AS, 65101. The one idea to hold: BGP's loop check only sees AS numbers, not routers. A route that passed through another router with my number looks exactly like a route that passed through me.

@1
Leaf 1 announces its loopback, 10.255.1.11 slash 32, to spine 1 in an UPDATE over their TCP session on port 179. The AS path holds only 65101, and the next hop is leaf 1's address on the link, 10.254.1.1, sent with a T T L of 1.

@2
The same UPDATE goes to spine 2 with next hop 10.254.2.1. Each spine looks for 65100 in the path, does not find it, and installs the route: one path, straight to leaf 1.

@3-4
Spine 1 prepends its AS and passes the route to leaf 2, leaf 3 and leaf 4: the AS path reads 65100, 65101, and the copy to leaf 3 has next hop 10.254.1.4. Spine 2 does the same, with next hop 10.254.2.4. So far, nothing differs from the per-leaf plan.

@5
Here is the difference. Leaf 3 is also AS 65101, so it finds its own number in 65100, 65101 and rejects both copies as loops. Its table shows both paths, each marked rejected, own AS 65101. Leaf 3 cannot reach leaf 1's loopback at all, although nothing is looping, and every session is still Established.

@6
The usual fix is allow A S in, with a count of 1: leaf 3 may accept a path that contains its own AS once. Both copies are accepted, and multipath installs an E C M P route through both spines. It works, but it weakens loop protection on every leaf that has it, which is why the RFC 7938 plan gives each leaf its own AS inside a cluster.

@7
Leaf 3 advertises its best path on to spine 2, prepending its AS: the path is now 65101, 65100, 65101, with next hop 10.254.2.5. To spine 2, that is a path to leaf 1 that goes down to leaf 3, up to spine 1 and down again.

@8
Spine 2 finds 65100 in the path and discards it. This is why allow A S in 1 is safe here: the spines' shared AS still rejects every path through two spines, so the only loop the leaves now admit is a leaf's own route coming back to it, which loses to the route it originated itself.

@9
Then the link between leaf 1 and spine 2 fails, and its session drops on loss of light or a B F D timeout. Spine 2 has no other usable path, because it never accepted the looped one, so it withdraws 10.255.1.11 slash 32 from leaf 2, leaf 3 and leaf 4.

@10
Leaf 3 drops the path via spine 2 and keeps spine 1 as its only path. As in the per-leaf plan, spine 2 itself can no longer reach leaf 1, where an IS-IS spine would detour through a leaf.

@11
The general lesson: reusing an AS number makes the loop check reject routes that never looped, and the failure is quiet. A leaf that someone forgot to give allow A S in has Established sessions and normal received counts, yet near-zero accepted routes, with the A S path loop denial counters rising. Allow A S in is safe only while something else bounds the path, here the spine tier; with a large count, or nothing above to reject the loop, a withdrawn route can circulate until the count runs out. Unique four-byte private AS numbers need none of it.
