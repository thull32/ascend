@0
On screen is Meridian's Amsterdam underlay, the small example: two spines and four leaves in IS-IS area 49.0001, every router reaching the other five loopbacks. Since a recent change, the switches take their time from an NTP server reached through the fabric itself, behind leaf 1, instead of over the out-of-band management network. Hold onto one rule, because the whole incident follows from it: any IS-IS router can delete any other router's LSP from every database in the domain, by purging it.

@1
Leaf 4 reboots after a software upgrade. Its hardware clock has lost its setting and starts at midnight on the first of January, 2000. Nothing checks the clock before routing starts.

@2
41 seconds after boot, leaf 4's adjacencies to both spines come up and the spines send it the other five LSPs. Here is the shortcut. This network operating system does not count each LSP's remaining lifetime down; it stores an expiry time on the wall clock, receipt time plus remaining lifetime. Spine 1's LSP arrives with 1,043 seconds left, so it expires at 18 minutes and 4 seconds past midnight, in the year 2000.

@3
With the underlay up, leaf 4 can finally reach its NTP server, through spine 1 and leaf 1, and sends a query on UDP port 123. Time now depends on the network, and, as the next frames show, the network depends on time.

@4
The reply says it is 9 in the morning on the 31st of October, 2026. Leaf 4 steps its clock forward 846,752,400 seconds, about 26 years and 10 months, in one jump. Every expiry it stored is now 26 years in the past, and the table marks all five LSPs expired. Its own LSP it simply re-originates.

@5
So the aging check purges the five it did not originate. Each purge keeps the LSP's sequence number, sets its remaining lifetime to zero and removes its topology: the header shows spine 1's, sequence 5C in hex, lifetime zero. Each also carries a purge originator TLV, from RFC 6232, with leaf 4's system ID, the only clue to where the purges came from.

@6
The purges flood to both spines. A purge beats a stored copy with the same sequence number, which is why it keeps the number. So each spine replaces the other routers' LSPs with the purges, drops them from SPF and floods the purges on, as if those routers had vanished. The one purge a spine refuses is the purge of its own LSP.

@7
Within milliseconds every router holds purges for every other router's LSP except leaf 4's. SPF uses a link only when the LSPs at both ends describe it, so it finds almost nothing: each spine reaches one loopback, its neighbour leaf 4, and the leaves reach none. The topology has vanished from every database at once, while every link and adjacency is still up.

@8
Everything above the underlay falls with it. BGP's next-hop tracking marks every EVPN route unusable, since no next-hop loopback is reachable, the VXLAN tunnels are removed, and tenant traffic stops across the site. The iBGP sessions to the route reflectors start counting down their hold timers.

@9
Now the originators fight back. Each router that received a purge of its own LSP, one it knows is valid, re-issues it at once with a higher sequence number and a fresh 1,200 second lifetime: spine 1 from 5C to 5D, spine 2 from 51 to 52, leaf 3 from 2A to 2B, and so on. A higher number beats a purge, so the new copies flood everywhere and replace the purges.

@10
Leaf 4 stores the new copies with expiries from its now correct clock, so it has no reason to purge again, and SPF rebuilds the full topology on every router. In six routers the gap lasts well under a second; in a real area of thousands of LSPs, flooding paced to tens of LSPs per second per interface makes each wave take tens of seconds. And tenant traffic is still stopped.

@11
IS-IS heals first; the overlay takes longer. Once the gap outlasts the BGP hold timers, 9 seconds at Meridian, the sessions drop, reconnect and re-exchange every EVPN route, and MAC and ARP state is relearned, so tenants see minutes. And the blast radius of a purge is the whole flooding domain: Meridian's single level-2 domain spans all three sites.

@12
The fixes: take time over the out-of-band network, so the clock is right before routing starts, and age LSPs with a countdown, as the IS-IS standard intends. The signals that name leaf 4 within seconds: its clock-step log, purges whose originator TLV carries its system ID, and the LSP count collapsing everywhere at once. To generalise: measure durations with a monotonic clock, never wall time, and never let the management plane depend on the network it manages. Adjacency dashboards stayed green throughout, so put the database itself on the dashboard.
