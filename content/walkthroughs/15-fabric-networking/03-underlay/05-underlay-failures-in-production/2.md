@0
On screen is Meridian's Amsterdam fabric, a small example with four spines and four leaves on 400 gigabit links; spines 1 and 2 also carry the two 100 gigabit circuits to Singapore. Tonight spine 2 gets a new operating system, which means a reboot, and the goal is to lose not one packet. The idea is make before break: move every flow off spine 2 while it is still forwarding, then work on an empty box.

@1
The pre-check does the arithmetic first. With one spine of four drained, each leaf's traffic spreads over three uplinks, four thirds of today's load on each. At 2 in the afternoon, leaf 3's 270 gigabits per second per uplink would become 360, 90 percent, and all 90 gigabits per second for Singapore would land on spine 1's circuit. Both exceed Meridian's 80 percent limit, so the tool refuses to start.

@2
At 2 in the morning the same check passes: leaf 3 would reach 220 gigabits per second, 55 percent, and the surviving circuit 70 percent. The state checks pass too: all four spines are in service, since draining one while another is out would halve the fabric; 18 adjacencies are up; every leaf has four equal-cost paths, and probes are clean.

@3
The drain. Spine 2 raises the metric on all five links, 10 on the leaf links and 52,500 on the circuit, to 16,777,214, the largest value SPF still uses, and floods a new LSP. A metric is the cost of leaving a router, so every path through spine 2 now pays it: leaf 3's table shows 16,777,224 through spine 2 against 20 through the others, and spine 2 drops out of every ECMP group. The overload bit would forbid transit even if spine 2 were the only path left; a maximum metric keeps it as a last resort.

@4
Traffic moves as each leaf updates its forwarding table: leaf 3 goes from four uplinks at 165 gigabits per second to three at 220, and Singapore traffic shifts onto spine 1's circuit, 70 of its 100. Nothing is lost, because spine 2 keeps forwarding whatever still reaches it until every leaf has moved; a flow that changes spine may see a few packets reordered, once.

@5
Before touching spine 2, verify it is empty. Each port carries about 20 kilobits per second, just the hellos, BFD and LLDP that keep its adjacencies up. The busiest remaining uplink is at 55 percent, the circuit at the predicted 70, with no discards. The drain did what the pre-check said.

@6
Now the work. Spine 2 reboots, its links go dark for minutes, and the leaves see five adjacencies drop, which the change-aware monitoring expects and does not page for. Packets lost, zero; probes failed, zero. It is an ordinary spine failure, with the traffic moved out of the way first.

@7
Spine 2 comes back on the new release still costed out, because the high metric is in its saved configuration: adjacencies form, but it attracts no traffic. That is when to check it: five adjacencies up, 52 routes, the same as spine 1, optics in range and zero errors.

@8
Restore. Spine 2 sets its metrics back to 10 and 52,500 and floods a new LSP; every leaf returns it to its ECMP groups at cost 20, leaf 3's uplinks go back to four at 165, and each circuit to 35.

@9
The post-check matches the pre-check snapshot: equal shares, 18 adjacencies, zero discards, zero packets lost, with both snapshots and every timestamp in the change record. To generalise: pre-check, drain, verify, work, restore, verify, on any device. And costing out breaks by being forgotten: a spine left at the maximum metric runs the site on three spines of four until the next failure, so diff metrics and overload bits against the source of truth.
