@0
Amsterdam to Singapore, in the small example. Leaf 1 in Amsterdam sends 80 gigabits per second to leaf 2 in Singapore, and ECMP splits it over the two circuits, about 40 each. Circuit 1 runs through a carrier's optical network, so the light spine 1 in Amsterdam receives comes from the carrier's equipment, not from its partner in Singapore. Hold onto one idea, because it decides the whole timeline: convergence is four phases, detection, propagation, computation and forwarding table programming, and when the port cannot see the failure, detection is nearly all of the time.

@1
The two spine 1s run a BFD session across the circuit. Each sends a small UDP control packet every 300 milliseconds, with a detect multiplier of 3, so the session goes down after 900 milliseconds of silence. The packet carries IP T T L 255, which the peer insists on as proof the sender is directly attached. IS-IS is registered as a client of this session, because its own hellos, every 10 seconds with a 30 second hold time, are far too slow to rely on.

@2
At time zero a fibre breaks inside the carrier's network. The carrier does not pass the failure on, so its equipment keeps sending light to both spines and both ports stay up. Neither spine notices anything, and every packet hashed onto circuit 1, half of leaf 1's Singapore traffic, is now lost.

@3
Spine 1 in Amsterdam expects a BFD packet every 300 milliseconds, and none arrives, so its count of missed intervals climbs towards 3. IS-IS on its own would still call the adjacency up.

@4
At 900 milliseconds, three intervals after the last packet, which here arrived just before the cut, the worst case, spine 1 declares the session down and tells IS-IS at once, which drops the adjacency. Spine 1 in Singapore reaches the same verdict on its own, within one interval. That is BFD's whole job: detection, and nothing else.

@5
Now propagation. Spine 1 waits its initial LSP generation delay of 50 milliseconds, in case more changes arrive to batch, then floods a new LSP without circuit 1. It reaches leaf 1 and the other Amsterdam leaves about 2 milliseconds later. The timeline reads 952 milliseconds.

@6
Computation. Leaf 1 waits its initial SPF delay, another 50 milliseconds, then runs SPF in about 1 millisecond. Its route to leaf 2's loopback, 10.255.2.12, now has one next hop, spine 2 in Amsterdam, and the timeline reads 1,003 milliseconds. Spine 1, which saw the failure directly, already routes Singapore traffic down through the leaves, so until every leaf has updated, a few packets bounce between spine 1 and a leaf still pointing at it: a microloop.

@7
The new route is written into the ASIC in about 15 milliseconds, because every affected prefix shares one ECMP group and only that group is rewritten: prefix independent convergence. All 80 gigabits per second now leave through spine 2 and circuit 2, and traffic flows again.

@8
From cut to recovered traffic, 1,018 milliseconds, and 900 of them, 88 percent, were detection. At 40 gigabits per second, about 5.1 gigabytes were lost on circuit 1. TCP retransmits them and connections survive, but the affected flows stall at least that long, and longer where the retransmission timer has backed off.

@9
Now the same cut without BFD. The only detector is IS-IS itself: with 10 second hellos and a multiplier of 3, the adjacency dies when the 30 second hold timer runs out. The remaining steps add the same 118 milliseconds, so recovery takes about 30.1 seconds, with about 150.6 gigabytes lost. Even hellos tuned to 1 second, with a 3 second hold, recover at 3,118 milliseconds, detecting 3.3 times more slowly than BFD at 300 milliseconds times 3.

@10
BFD exists for failures the port cannot see. Had the fibre run straight between the two spines, loss of light would have taken the port down within a few milliseconds without it. The pattern to remember: once a failure is detected, a well tuned fabric spends about a tenth of a second on everything else, and the deliberate waits dwarf the real work. So the detector sets the outage. And the trap: timers much below 100 milliseconds times 3 usually need BFD to run in the line card or ASIC rather than on the CPU, or a busy control plane misses its own packets and declares a healthy link dead.
