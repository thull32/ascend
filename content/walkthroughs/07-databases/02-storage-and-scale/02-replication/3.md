@0
A lease store at the top, node A and node B, and the clients. A leader lease is a time-bounded promise: while a node holds a valid lease, it knows no one else is leader. Here a lease lasts 5 seconds, it is renewed every 2, and clocks are trusted to drift by no more than half a second.

@1-2
A asks the lease store, a consensus group such as etcd, for the lease. Only one node can hold it. A is now leader until t plus 5 seconds, and it measures that expiry on its own clock from when it sent the request, the conservative end.

@3-4
A client reads x. A answers 7 straight from memory, with no round trip. Without the lease it would have to confirm with a majority that it is still leader on every read.

@5
At t plus 2 seconds, A renews, long before expiry, and the lease now runs to t plus 7. A renewal is a heartbeat with teeth.

@6
Then A is cut off from the store. Its renewal at t plus 4 seconds never arrives. Clients on A's side keep sending reads.

@7
At t plus 7 seconds by its own clock, A stops serving, even though nothing told it to. This is the discipline that makes leases safe: the holder enforces the expiry on itself.

@8
The store sees the lease lapse, but waits the extra drift bound, half a second, before granting a new one, in case A's clock runs slow and A is still serving for a moment.

@9
B acquires the lease, which runs to t plus 12.5 seconds. Between A stepping down and now, no reads were served. The window of unavailability is roughly one lease length plus the grace.

@10
Clients redirect to B, which reads x equals 7 from its own state, under its own lease.

@11
The second failure mode: if A's clock ran slower than the drift bound, or A paused in the middle of a request, A could still answer after B became leader. Stale reads: the read-side form of split brain. Spanner buys certainty with TrueTime; others add fencing tokens or accept the risk.

@12
The trade: reads become one local lookup instead of a quorum round trip, at the cost of an outage window after a leader dies, and a safety argument that rests on clocks behaving. A shorter lease means a shorter outage but more renewal traffic. This is how Patroni fences a Postgres primary: the leader must renew a key in etcd every 10 seconds with a time to live of 30, and a leader that cannot renew demotes itself. So failover takes roughly that time to live plus promotion, around 30 to 60 seconds.
