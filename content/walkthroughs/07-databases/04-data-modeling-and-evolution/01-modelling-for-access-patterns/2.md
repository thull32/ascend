@0
Every denormalised copy needs a named mechanism that keeps it in sync. Inside one database, that is the same transaction. Once the copy lives where the transaction cannot reach, a Redis leaderboard, a cache, a warehouse, it is change data capture. The app writes only to Postgres. A connector tails the write-ahead log and turns every committed change into an event, in commit order. The sink on the right is labelled search index; read it as any read model.

@1
Setup: the connector creates a replication slot, so Postgres keeps the log from here on, and streams a consistent snapshot of the existing rows. After that, it needs only the log.

@2-3
The app inserts user 1, Ada, and commits at log position 1. The app knows nothing about the read model. The connector decodes the change, with the row's before and after images, and the consumer applies it as an upsert. The gap between commit and apply is the staleness a reader can see.

@4-7
An update renames Ada to Ada L., at position 2, and an order is inserted for user 1, at position 3. Each is decoded and applied in the same order the database committed it.

@8-9
User 1 is deleted at position 4, and the consumer deletes it by primary key.

@10
The connector crashes after sending position 4 but before confirming it, so on restart it sends 4 again. Delivery is at least once, so applying an event must be idempotent: upsert by key, or skip anything at or below the last applied position.

@11
An abandoned slot pins the log. Down for 3 days, and the primary's disk is 95 percent full. Monitor slot lag.

@12
Why not write the copy from the request handler? A crash between the two writes loses an update, and two racing writers can apply their updates to the copy in the opposite order. One log makes the database the single arbiter of order.

@13
The rule from the lesson: one transaction for copies inside the database, change data capture for copies outside it, and triggers only with a written reason. The commit-to-apply lag, tens of milliseconds to seconds, is the copy's staleness window, so monitor it. And every read model must be droppable and rebuildable from the write model, or its first inconsistency becomes permanent.
