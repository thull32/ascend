@0
This is the post created topic, the event the post service produces once a post is committed. Three partitions, each an ordered, append-only log, and two fan-out workers in one consumer group. The producer hashes each event's key, here the author, to choose a partition, and order is guaranteed only within a partition. So every event from one author stays in order.

@1-3
Author 42 posts, and the hash sends the event to partition 1. Author 7 goes to partition 2. Author 42 posts again: partition 1, behind the first event. That is the property the design wants: an edit can never overtake its create.

@4-6
Author 913 lands in partition 2, then author 7 again, behind its earlier post, and author 42 a third time, in partition 1. With these keys, partition 0 got nothing at all. Hashing spreads keys, not load.

@7
The group coordinator assigns each partition to exactly one worker. Worker 1 gets partitions 0 and 2, worker 2 gets partition 1. Partitions, not messages, are the unit of fan-out parallelism.

@8-9
Worker 1 polls partition 2, receives its three events in offset order, and after processing commits offset 3. Committing after processing gives at-least-once delivery, so a crash between the two replays work.

@10-11
Worker 2 polls partition 1 and works through author 42's three events. Having finished offsets 0 and 1, it commits offset 2, and starts on offset 2.

@12
Worker 2 dies after processing offset 2, before committing it. The committed offset for partition 1 is still 2.

@13
After its session timeout the coordinator rebalances, and worker 1 takes over partition 1, resuming from the committed offset, 2. That event is fanned out a second time. In the feed, a replayed page pushes a post ID into a timeline twice, which is why the read path deduplicates. The group pauses during the rebalance.

@14
Two limits. A group can use at most as many workers as there are partitions. And a hot key sends all its traffic to one partition, which one worker must handle alone. A burst from one prolific author lands on one partition, which is one more reason celebrity accounts skip this path entirely and are pulled at read time.

@15
The trade-off: per-key ordering and horizontal scale, at the price of no global order, parallelism capped by the partition count, and pauses during rebalances. For the feed, the author is the right key: ordering is needed per author, and large authors are split into page tasks on a second topic.
