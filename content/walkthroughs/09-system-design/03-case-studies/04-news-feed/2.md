@0
Two sorted lists: list A holds 3, 8, 12 and 20, list B holds 5, 9 and 21. Think of them as post IDs, which sort by time, from a viewer's timeline and from one celebrity's recent posts. The rule: the smallest remaining value is always one of the two heads. Compare the heads, take the smaller, advance that pointer.

@1-2
3 against 5: take 3 from list A. Then 8 against 5: take 5 from list B.

@3-4
8 against 9: take 8. 12 against 9: take 9. Each comparison places exactly one value, and neither list is ever scanned twice.

@5-6
12 against 21: take 12. 20 against 21: take 20. List A is now empty.

@7
When one list runs out, the rest of the other is already sorted, so the tail is linked on in one step, with no more comparisons: 21 joins the end.

@8
Seven values merged with 6 comparisons, linear in the total length. The feed runs the same merge newest-first, over several streams at once, with a heap: the viewer's timeline, the viewer's own recent posts, and a list for each celebrity they follow. It stops after 300 IDs, and extra streams only deepen the heap. Measured, that merge took about 51 microseconds with 14 pulled streams and 65 with 61, which is why the cost of the hybrid design is not the merge but where the celebrity lists live.
