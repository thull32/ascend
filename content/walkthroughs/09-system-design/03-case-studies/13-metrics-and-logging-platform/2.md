@0
A producer, a buffer with room for 8 messages, and a consumer that can process 2 per tick. Backpressure is the consumer's ability to slow the producer down. Without it, a fast producer and a slow consumer are separated only by a buffer, and every buffer is finite.

@1
The producer pushes a burst of 10. The buffer keeps 8, and the last 2 have nowhere to go: they are dropped. An unbounded buffer would only move the problem, growing until the process is killed for running out of memory.

@2
The consumer drains 2, its real capacity, leaving 6. The 2 dropped messages are gone, and nobody upstream was told: the producer believes it delivered 10.

@3
The fix flips the direction of control. The consumer tells the producer how many messages it may send, as credits or a window, and the producer may not exceed that number. Sending becomes pull-driven.

@4-5
The consumer sees 2 free slots and grants exactly 2 credits. The producer sends messages 9 and 10, filling the buffer, and then stops: with zero credits it must wait. The buffer cannot overflow, by construction.

@6
Each tick the consumer processes 2 and hands back 2 credits. In steady state the producer runs at exactly the consumer's rate, and the buffer only absorbs jitter.

@7
The pressure travels upstream. A blocked producer stops reading from its own source, whose handlers slow down, whose clients see latency or a 429. That is the point: the slowest stage sets the pace, and overload shows up at the edge instead of as silent loss in the middle.

@8
But a hung request is not always acceptable. For telemetry, dropping is the right policy, oldest first or sampled; for orders it is not. And beware cycles: two stages each blocked waiting on the other is a deadlock.

@9
That is how the platform uses it. When the gateway or a tenant limit refuses a batch with a 429, the agent keeps it in a bounded disk spool and retries later. The application being monitored never waits, and if the spool fills, the oldest telemetry is dropped first. So a 429 is the platform saying slow down, not something broken.
