@0
A producer on the left, a Kafka topic split into three partitions, and a consumer group of two. Each partition is an ordered, append-only log. The producer hashes each message's key to pick a partition, and order is guaranteed within a partition only. Here the keys are metric series, so every sample of one series stays in order.

@1-3
Series 91 hashes to partition 0 and lands at offset 0. Series 12 goes to partition 1, and series 40 to partition 2, each at offset 0. The broker never reorders or rewrites a log.

@4
Series 91 arrives again. Same key, same hash, same partition: it is appended at offset 1, right behind the earlier sample. A consumer will always see this series' samples in the order they were written.

@5-6
Series 133 also hashes to partition 0 and takes offset 2; different keys can share a partition. Then the second series 12 sample follows the first in partition 1. The logs now hold three, two and one messages.

@7
The group coordinator assigns each partition to exactly one consumer: consumer 1 takes partitions 0 and 2, consumer 2 takes partition 1. Partitions, not messages, are the unit of parallelism.

@8-9
Consumer 1 polls partition 0 and receives offsets 0 to 2, in order. The broker deletes nothing on read, so each consumer tracks its own position. After processing, it commits offset 3. Committing after processing gives at-least-once: a crash between the two replays those messages.

@10-11
Consumer 2 polls partition 1 and its two series 12 samples. After the first it commits offset 1, and moves on to offset 1.

@12
Consumer 2 dies after processing offset 1, before committing it. The committed offset for partition 1 is still 1.

@13
After its session timeout the coordinator rebalances, and consumer 1 takes over partition 1, resuming from the committed offset, 1. The second series 12 sample is delivered again, so writing it must be idempotent. The whole group pauses during the rebalance.

@14
Two limits follow. Three partitions means at most three useful consumers; a fourth would sit idle, so the partition count you choose caps parallelism. And a hot key sends all its traffic to one partition, which one consumer must handle alone.

@15
In the platform, metrics are keyed by series hash across 128 partitions, so every sample of one series reaches one ingester, which keeps its chunks append-only and in order, and a dead ingester's partitions resume from the last commit. The trade is per-key order and horizontal scale, with no global order at all.
