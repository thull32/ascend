@0
A task queue, two workers, and a dead-letter queue. Each task is one encode: one chunk of the video at one rung of the ladder. The producer enqueues and moves on, and workers pull when they have capacity.

@1
Five tasks arrive. The broker persists each one and acknowledges at once, so the orchestrator never waits for encoding.

@2-3
Worker 1 takes t1 under a 30 second lease, and renews it every 10 seconds while it encodes. Nobody else can take a leased task. Worker 2 takes t2. Each task goes to exactly one worker.

@4
Worker 1 finishes and acknowledges, and only now is t1 marked done. In the pipeline, the orchestrator marks a task done only after its output exists.

@5-6
Worker 2 crashes halfway through t2. Encode workers are preemptible, so this is routine. No acknowledgement was sent, and now nobody renews the lease. When it expires, t2 is handed out again, and worker 1 takes it: attempt 2.

@7
If worker 2 had already uploaded its encoded chunk, that has now happened twice. For an encode, a second run is harmless, by design. Each task is a pure function of the source's hash, the chunk index, the rendition and the encoder version, and its output key is derived from exactly those values. A retry overwrites the same object with the same bytes.

@8
Meanwhile more tasks keep coming: 5 more arrive in the time the workers finish 2, and the depth grows from 3 to 6. Depth divided by the drain rate is the wait every new task will see, which is why the pipeline watches it.

@9-11
t5 is a poison task: a file that crashes the decoder every time. The worker fails it, and the broker makes it available again and counts the attempt. After the third failed attempt it goes to the dead-letter queue instead of looping forever. The lesson adds what happens next: the video is marked failed with a reason the creator can act on, such as an unsupported codec, and an engineer can replay it after a fix.

@12
The trade-off: decoupling and elastic workers, at the price of at-least-once delivery, no end-to-end ordering, and a backlog that can grow silently. So the pipeline autoscales each queue on the age of its oldest task, not on CPU, which is always near 100 percent on an encoding fleet. And priority is separate queues, not a field: a priority field does not help when 500,000 backfill tasks are already ahead.
