@0
A sender retrying against a provider that is overloaded. The rule: after each failure, wait twice as long as last time, 100, then 200, then 400 milliseconds, and randomise each wait so that senders that failed together do not retry together. For notifications there is one more limit, and it comes at the end.

@1-2
Attempt 1 fails with a 503. A 503 or a timeout is worth retrying; a 400 or a 404 is not. In the notification design, a 410 Unregistered from APNs means the device token is dead: invalidate it and never retry. The first wait is up to 100 milliseconds, and full jitter picks 24.

@3-4
Attempt 2 fails. The ceiling is now 200 milliseconds, and jitter picks 183.

@5-8
Attempt 3 fails, and the sender waits 245 of a possible 400. Attempt 4 fails, and it waits 742 of a possible 800. Each wait gives the provider more room to recover.

@9
Attempt 5 succeeds, after about 1.2 seconds of total waiting. Had every retry fired at once, all five would have landed inside the same overload and failed.

@10
Without jitter, a thousand senders that failed at the same instant all sleep exactly 100 milliseconds and retry in lockstep, hitting the recovering provider with the same wave. That is the lesson's thundering herd when a provider recovers: a burst of retries trips its limit again.

@11
With full jitter the same retries spread across the whole interval, and the provider sees a ramp it can serve. The notification design adds one more spreader: retries go through the same token bucket as first attempts.

@12
Two more rules. Cap retries with a budget, so a full outage cannot multiply load. And only retry what is safe to repeat. A push is safe to resend with the same collapse ID, because the phone replaces the first copy. Marketing is deliberately at-most-once: on an ambiguous send, do not retry, and record it as sent.

@13
And here is the notification-specific limit: the retry budget ends at the message's expiry. A late your driver is here is worse than none, and a login code that expired at 13:15 should never be sent at 13:16. Backoff bounds the load, jitter spreads it, the expiry decides when to give up, and a circuit breaker stops it when the failure is not transient.
