@0
Users, an API gateway with one shared pool of 12 threads, and three dependencies: payments, search and recommendations. A bulkhead partitions resources per dependency, like the watertight compartments in a ship's hull. First, the version without one.

@1
Search becomes slow. Calls that took 50 milliseconds now hang for 8 seconds before timing out. Nothing else has changed.

@2-3
Every search call now holds a thread for 8 seconds instead of 50 milliseconds. Six are in flight and half the pool is gone. Traffic keeps arriving at the normal rate, but calls now leave 160 times slower than they arrive, and soon search alone holds all 12 threads.

@4-5
A checkout request arrives. It needs payments, which is perfectly healthy. It is rejected anyway, with a 503, because there is no thread to run it. A slow search box has taken down payments: one dependency's latency became every feature's outage.

@6
The fix: split the 12 threads into 3 pools of 4, one per dependency. A search call may only use a search thread, and when that pool is full, the call is rejected at once instead of waiting.

@7-8
Search degrades exactly as before and fills its 4 threads, but it cannot borrow anyone else's. The next search call fails in microseconds, and the page renders without the search box instead of hanging.

@9
Checkout arrives, takes a payments thread from a pool with 3 to spare, and succeeds. The compartment held.

@10
The fix has its own failure mode: sizing. Too small, and a normal spike rejects legitimate calls; too large, and the isolation is only on paper. Size it from measured concurrency, rate times latency, with headroom. In the lesson's home page model, ratings normally has 35 calls in flight, 49 at its slowest normal latency, so the bulkhead is 60. Hystrix's default of 10 failed 72 percent of home pages while ratings was perfectly healthy.

@11
Bulkheads cap the blast radius of one dependency at its compartment's size, at the price of idle threads that cannot help another pool. In the simulation, the bulkhead of 60 kept playback at 100 percent, but home pages still failed, because nothing yet substitutes for the missing row. That takes a breaker and a fallback.
