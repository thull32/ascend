@0
Checkout calls a payments API through a circuit breaker, a proxy with three states. Closed passes calls and counts failures. After 3 failures in a row it opens, and fails every call instantly. After 5 seconds it goes half-open, and lets one trial call decide.

@1
Normal operation: the breaker is closed, call 1 succeeds in 40 milliseconds, and the failure count stays at zero.

@2-3
The payments API starts timing out. Calls 2 and 3 each hold a checkout thread for the full 2 second timeout before failing. Failure 1 of 3, then 2 of 3. Under load, this is how one slow dependency drains the whole thread pool.

@4
Call 4 times out too: the third failure. The breaker trips open.

@5-6
Call 5 is rejected in under a millisecond with a 503, without touching payments. A slow failure has become a fast one, and the thread is free again. Over the next 5 seconds, 15 more calls are shed the same way. Payments gets quiet time to recover instead of a queue of retries, and checkout can serve a fallback, like payment pending.

@7-8
The 5 seconds pass, and the breaker goes half-open. It lets one trial call through. The trial times out: payments is still sick, so the breaker opens again for another 5 seconds. Many implementations back this off exponentially.

@9-10
Payments recovers. After another 5 seconds the breaker goes half-open again, and this trial succeeds in 45 milliseconds. The breaker closes and resets its count, and traffic flows normally.

@11
Three traps. A raw count of failures trips on a few flaky calls, so real breakers use a failure rate over a rolling window with a minimum volume; the lesson's breaker opens once half the calls in its 10 second window fail. One breaker shared by unrelated endpoints lets a broken refund take down charges. And a breaker without a fallback still fails the user, just faster.

@12
The breaker sacrifices the few calls that might have succeeded while it was open, and frees the caller to stay up. In the lesson's simulation it cut calls to the broken ratings service from 700 a second to one trial every 5 seconds. The cost showed on the way out: after ratings healed, full pages took 4 seconds to return, because nothing called it until the next trial.
