@0
One client, 999 others behind it, and an overloaded service. The client retries with exponential backoff: the wait doubles after each failure, 100 milliseconds, then 200, then 400, capped at 2 seconds. And jitter: each wait is a random value between zero and that delay, so clients that failed together do not retry together.

@1-2
Attempt 1 fails with a 503, overloaded. That is retryable; a 400 or a 404 is not, and retrying it only adds load. The schedule says wait up to 100 milliseconds, and jitter picks 24.

@3-6
Attempt 2 fails. The ceiling doubles to 200 milliseconds, and jitter picks 183. Attempt 3 fails at 207 milliseconds; the ceiling is 400 and the wait is 245.

@7-10
Attempt 4 fails at 452 milliseconds and waits 742 of a possible 800. Attempt 5 fails; the ceiling is now 1,600, and this time jitter happens to pick only 79. The waits are random, but their ceiling grows exponentially.

@11
Attempt 6 succeeds, after about 1.3 seconds of total waiting. Had every retry fired immediately, all six would have landed inside the same overload and failed.

@12
Now the crowd. Without jitter, 1,000 clients that failed at the same instant all sleep exactly 100 milliseconds and retry in lockstep. The service, just starting to recover, is hit by the same wave again, now synchronised. Retries turned an outage into a longer one.

@13
With full jitter the same 1,000 retries spread across the whole interval, and the service sees a gentle ramp it can actually serve.

@14
Two more rules. Cap retries with a budget, say 10 percent of requests, so an outage cannot multiply load by the attempt count. And only retry idempotent operations, or a charge that timed out but succeeded is charged again.

@15
Backoff spreads retries in time; it does not reduce how many there are. In the lesson, three layers each making up to three attempts send 27 attempts to the bottom for every home page when ratings is fully down: 700 calls a second become 18,900, exactly when it can least take them. Retrying at one layer with a 10 percent budget caps it at 770.
