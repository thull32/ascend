@0
An event source, a window operator, and a sink that receives results. The clicks are counted in one-minute windows. Tumbling windows have a fixed size and never overlap, so every event belongs to exactly one window, chosen by its event time, and each window emits one result when it closes.

@1-4
Clicks at 6, 18, 36 and 54 seconds all fall in the first minute, and its count climbs to 4. It stays open, because a later click might still belong to it.

@5
A click at 1 minute 12 belongs to the next window. So the first window is complete: the operator emits a count of 4 to the sink and discards that window's state. A window's result exists only once the next window has started.

@6-8
Clicks at 1 minute 24 and 1 minute 42 bring the second window to 3, and a click at 2 minutes 12 closes it: 3 is emitted.

@9-11
2 minutes 30 and 2 minutes 42 join the window from 2 to 3 minutes, and the click at 3 minutes 6 closes it with a count of 3.

@12
The click at 3 minutes 18 is the second in the window from 3 to 4 minutes, which is still open.

@13
For comparison, sliding windows: one minute long, starting every 30 seconds. Windows overlap, so each click is counted in 2 of them, and a result comes out every 30 seconds: smoother output, twice the state.

@14
And session windows have no fixed size. Clicks at most 24 seconds apart merge into one session, which closes only after a longer silence. The same 12 clicks form 2 sessions, from 6 seconds to 1 minute 42, and from 2 minutes 12 to 3 minutes 18. Size follows the data, so a key that is never quiet holds state forever.

@15
Tumbling is the cheapest, one bucket per key, which is why the click counts use it. But all three assume you know when a window is complete, and that raises the catch: which clock? The lesson assigns each click by event time, the moment of the tap, not when it arrives, and fires partial counts every 10 seconds while the window is open, so the dashboard is fresh long before the final count.
