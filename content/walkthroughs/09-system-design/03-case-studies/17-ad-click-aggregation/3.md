@0
A source, one window covering event times 0 up to 10 seconds, and a sink. There are two clocks: event time, when the click happened, stamped by the device, and processing time, when it reaches us. Events arrive out of order, so the wall clock cannot say when a window is complete. A watermark is the operator's estimate that nothing older will arrive. Here it trails the largest event time seen by 2 seconds.

@1-3
The first event happened at 1 second; the watermark is minus 1. The second happened at 4, and the watermark moves to 2. The third happened at 9, arriving at processing time 3, and the watermark jumps to 7. The window holds 3 clicks.

@4
The fourth event happened at 3 seconds but arrives after the one from 9: out of order. The watermark stays at 7, below the window's end at 10, so the window has not fired, and the event is counted normally. The count is 4.

@5
The fifth event happened at 12 seconds. It belongs to the next window, and it pushes the watermark to 10. The operator now believes the first window is complete and emits a count of 4. Notice it fired at processing time 5, not at 10: completeness is decided by event time.

@6
The sixth event happened at 7 seconds, but its window has already fired. It is late. By default it is dropped or sent to a side output; with allowed lateness the window would be kept and a corrected result emitted.

@7-8
An event from 15 seconds goes to the next window and moves the watermark to 13. Then one from 2 seconds arrives: late again, dropped.

@9
So the emitted count was 4 and the true count was 6. The 2 late events are the price of deciding early. A bound of 6 seconds would have caught them, but delayed every result by 6 seconds.

@10
Watermarks fail in three ways: a wrong guess drops late data silently, so measure it; one stalled partition holds the watermark back for the whole job, because the operator takes the minimum across its inputs; and a device with a skewed clock stamps events in the future.

@11
The bound is a dial between latency and completeness. In the lesson it is 30 seconds, with partial counts fired every 10 seconds, a final count at the watermark, corrections for 5 minutes of allowed lateness, and anything later diverted to a topic for the daily billing batch, which counts every click.
