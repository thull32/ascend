@0
A lease store and two nodes, A and B. In the chat design, the node holding the lease is a conversation's owner, the single writer that assigns each message's sequence number. A lease is a time-bounded promise: while a node holds a valid one, it knows nobody else is in charge, so it can act on its own state without asking anyone.

@1-2
A asks the lease store for a 5 second lease, and gets it. It notes the expiry on its own clock, measured from when it sent the request, the conservative end.

@3-4
A client reads, and A answers from memory, with no round trip. For a conversation owner, that is the point: one writer per conversation, so no coordination on the hot path.

@5
A renews long before expiry, at 2 seconds, and the lease now runs to 7. Renewal is a heartbeat with teeth.

@6-7
Now A is cut off from the store, and its renewals stop arriving. At 7 seconds by its own clock, A must stop, even though nothing told it to. The holder enforces the expiry on itself.

@8-9
The store sees the lease lapse, but waits an extra half second, a bound on clock drift, in case A's clock runs slow. Then B acquires the lease. Between A stepping down and now, nobody was in charge: the gap is roughly one lease length plus that grace.

@10
Clients go to B, which works under its own lease.

@11
Here is the failure that matters for chat. If A's clock runs slower than the bound, or A pauses mid-request, A can still act after B has taken over. That is the lesson's trace: owner X stalls in a garbage collection pause, Y takes over with epoch 8 and assigns 1044 to Bob's message, then X wakes and assigns 1044 to Ann's. The fix is a fencing token. Every write carries the lease epoch, and the store's conditional write accepts it only if the epoch is at least the latest. X's write with epoch 7 is rejected, Ann's device retries through Y, and her message becomes 1045.

@12
The trade-off: a single owner acting locally, at the cost of a short gap after an owner crashes, and a safety argument that rests on clocks behaving. The fencing token is what removes that last dependency on clocks: without it, 1044 would exist twice, with different bodies on different devices.
