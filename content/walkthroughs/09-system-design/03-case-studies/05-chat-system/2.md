@0
A lease store, two owner nodes, X and Y, and the message store. In the chat design, the node holding a conversation's lease is its owner, the single writer that assigns each message's sequence number. The lease comes with an epoch, a number that only ever goes up. The owner stamps its epoch on every write, and the message store remembers the highest epoch it has accepted. Hold onto that last part: the store is where safety lives.

@1
Owner X acquires the lease for the conversation, with epoch 7.

@2
X assigns sequence 1043 and writes it, stamped with epoch 7. The store accepts, and records 7 as the highest epoch it has seen.

@3
Then X stops: a long garbage collection pause. It is not partitioned and not dead, only frozen, so it cannot renew its lease, and it cannot notice that time is passing.

@4
The lease expires. The lease store cannot tell a paused owner from a dead one, so it grants the lease to Y, with the next epoch, 8.

@5
Y assigns sequence 1044 to Bob's message and writes it with epoch 8. Accepted, and the store's highest epoch is now 8.

@6
X wakes up. From its own point of view nothing happened: it still owns the conversation, so it assigns 1044 to Ann's message and writes it, stamped with epoch 7.

@7
The store's conditional write sees epoch 7, older than 8, and rejects it. X learns that it was deposed, and steps down. In the lesson's trace, Ann's device times out and retries through Y, and her message becomes 1045. For a moment there were two owners, but only one of them could write.

@8
A lease alone cannot stop an owner that is paused rather than cut off: no timeout runs while it is frozen. The epoch check at the store is what makes the lease safe, because the store is the one party that sees both writers. Without it, sequence 1044 would exist twice, with different bodies on different devices.
