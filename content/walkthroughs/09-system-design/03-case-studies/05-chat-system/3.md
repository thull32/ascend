@0
The presence service on the left, one user's channel in the middle, for user u 12, and three gateways on the right. Hold onto one rule: a status change is published once, to that user's channel, and only gateways whose clients have the user on screen are subscribed. The fan-out follows attention, not the contact list.

@1
Clients on gateways A and B have u 12 on screen, so those two gateways subscribe to the channel. Gateway C holds clients who have u 12 in their contacts but are not looking at them, and it does not subscribe.

@2
u 12 comes online. The presence service publishes one event to the channel and moves on. It does not know who is watching.

@3
Only the two subscribed gateways receive it, and each pushes it to its viewers. Two deliveries, not one per contact. Gateway C, with no viewer, gets nothing.

@4
The client on gateway B scrolls u 12 off screen. Gateway B keeps the subscription for a short grace period in case the user scrolls back, then stops renewing it. Subscriptions are leases, so a crashed gateway cannot leak them either.

@5
Now u 12's phone switches from Wi-Fi to cellular, and the connection drops. The presence service waits about 30 seconds before publishing offline. u 12 reconnects within that window, so nothing is published, and nobody sees a flash of offline.

@6
Later u 12 closes the app. After the 30 second debounce the service publishes offline once, and only gateway A, the one subscriber left, receives it. Last seen is written lazily, on this transition, not on every heartbeat.

@7
A client on gateway C scrolls u 12 into view. Gateway C subscribes, and fetches the current state in the same batched call that returns everyone on that screen. From now on it receives u 12's changes too.

@8
The arithmetic is why this matters. A hundred million online users with 200 contacts each, changing state about every 10 minutes, would mean about 33 million notifications a second, almost all to people not looking. Subscribe on view sends only to screens that show the user.

@9
The trade-off: subscribe on view moves the cost into subscription churn as people scroll, which stays inside the datacentre and is batched, in exchange for dropping the fan-out to contacts who are not looking. Debouncing offline trades a few seconds of accuracy for no flicker.
