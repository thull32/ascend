@0
Six nodes, all holding version 1 of the cluster's view, and no coordinator. The rule here is push-pull gossip with a fanout of one: every round, every node exchanges its view with one random peer. If the node knows the news, it pushes it; if its peer knows, it pulls it. News reaches everyone in a number of rounds that grows with the logarithm of the cluster size.

@1
N1 learns something new: a membership change, or a peer's heartbeat counter. It has no list of who needs to hear it.

@2
Round 1: six exchanges. N1 pushes the news to N5, and N4 and N6, which happened to pick N1 as their peer, pull it. Three new nodes in a single round: 4 of 6 know.

@3
Round 2: N6 pushes to N3, the only exchange that teaches anyone. 5 of 6 know.

@4
Round 3: N2 and N5 exchange, and N2 learns. All 6 know.

@5
Everyone has the news after 3 rounds and 18 exchanges, and no node made more than one exchange a round. Doubling the cluster adds about one round. In the lesson's 48 node cluster, each node gossips with a random peer every second, so news reaches everyone in seconds.

@6
Two flavours. Rumour mongering pushes new updates for a few rounds; it is cheap but can miss a node. Anti-entropy periodically compares full state with a peer, using Merkle trees; it is heavier but guarantees convergence. This store runs both: gossip for membership, Merkle trees for repairing data.

@7
The failure modes. During a partition, each side converges to its own view. And stale news lingers, so a removed node can be re-added by a peer that never heard it left. That is why, in this design, a silent node is only temporarily unavailable: hints accumulate and nothing moves. Removal is an explicit operator action, because a gossip-driven removal would turn a 30 second garbage collection pause into a terabyte of data movement.

@8
The trade-off: no coordinator, no single point of failure, and load spread evenly, at the price of eventual delivery, redundant exchanges, and membership state on every node.
