@0
A stream of events, a window operator, and a sink. Windowing turns an endless stream into finite groups. Tumbling windows are fixed-size and never overlap, and here they are one minute long, the size used for stream starts per second, Netflix's primary health signal, counted per region, device and ISP. Every event belongs to exactly one window, and the window emits one result when it closes.

@1-4
Events at 6, 18, 36 and 54 seconds all fall in the first minute. The count climbs to 4, and the window stays open, because a later event might still belong to it.

@5
An event at 1 minute 12 falls in the next window. So the first minute is complete: the operator emits a count of 4 and discards that window's state. A window's result is available only once the next has started.

@6-8
Events at 1 minute 24 and 1 minute 42 join the second minute. An event at 2 minutes 12 closes it with 3.

@9-12
Events at 2 minutes 30 and 2 minutes 42 join the third minute, which closes at 3 minutes 6 with 3. Then 3 minutes 18 joins the fourth. One small counter per open window, per key. That is why tumbling windows scale to a firehose of start events across thousands of region, device and ISP combinations.

@13
Sliding windows overlap. With one-minute windows sliding every 30 seconds, each event counts in two windows, and a result comes out every 30 seconds. Smoother output, twice the state.

@14
Session windows have no fixed size. Events at most 24 seconds apart merge into one session, which closes only after a longer silence. The same 12 events form 2 sessions: 7 events from 6 seconds to 1 minute 42, and 5 from 2 minutes 12 to 3 minutes 18.

@15
All three assume you know when a window is complete, and that is the job of watermarks: an event that arrives after its window fired is either dropped or forces a correction. For stream starts, each minute's count is then compared with the expected count for that minute, because viewing follows strong daily and weekly cycles. A shortfall in one ISP's window points at that ISP's appliances or peering, not at the whole service.
