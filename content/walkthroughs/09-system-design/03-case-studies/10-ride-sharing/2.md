@0
In this design each city has exactly one matcher, and it proves it owns the city by holding a lease. On screen: a lease store, two matchers, A and B, and the trip store where assignments are written. Each lease comes with an epoch, a number that only goes up. The matcher stamps its epoch on every write, and the trip store remembers the highest epoch it has accepted. That remembered number is what will save us.

@1
Matcher A acquires the city's lease, with epoch 3.

@2
A assigns driver d17 to rider r1 and writes it, stamped with epoch 3. The trip store accepts, and records 3 as the highest epoch it has seen.

@3
Then A stops: a long garbage collection pause. It is not partitioned and not dead, only frozen, so it cannot renew its lease and cannot notice time passing.

@4
The lease expires. The lease store cannot tell a paused matcher from a dead one, so it grants the city to matcher B, with the next epoch, 4.

@5
B assigns driver d22 to rider r2, with epoch 4. Accepted, and the trip store's highest epoch is now 4.

@6
A wakes up, still believing it owns the city. Working from its stale view, it assigns the same driver, d22, to a different rider, r3, stamped with epoch 3. Without a check, d22 would now hold two trips.

@7
The trip store sees epoch 3, older than 4, and rejects the write. A learns it was deposed and steps down. For a moment there were two matchers, but only one of them could write, so no driver is assigned twice.

@8
A lease alone cannot stop a matcher that is paused rather than cut off: no timeout runs while it is frozen. The epoch carried in every write is what makes the lease safe, because the store is the one party that sees both writers. Together with the compare-and-set on each driver, that is how the design guarantees one trip per driver.
