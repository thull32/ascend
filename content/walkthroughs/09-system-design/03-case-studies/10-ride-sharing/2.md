@0
In this design each city has exactly one matcher, and it proves it owns the city by holding a lease. The picture shows the mechanism in its general form: a lease store at the top, node A as a candidate, node B on standby, and clients below. The lease lasts 5 seconds, it is renewed every 2, and clocks are trusted to drift by no more than 500 milliseconds. A lease is a time-bounded promise: while you hold a valid one, nobody else is leader.

@1-2
A asks the lease store, a consensus group such as etcd, for a 5 second lease, and gets it: A is leader until t plus 5 seconds. A measures that expiry on its own clock from the moment it sent the request, which is the conservative end.

@3-4
A client reads x, and A answers 7 straight from memory, with no round trip. Without the lease, A would have to confirm with a majority on every read that it is still leader.

@5
At t plus 2 seconds, long before expiry, A renews, and the lease now runs to t plus 7. Renewal is a heartbeat with teeth.

@6
Now the failure. A is cut off from the store, and its renewal at t plus 4 never arrives. Clients on A's side keep sending reads.

@7
At t plus 7 by its own clock, A stops serving, even though nothing told it to. This is the discipline that makes leases safe: the holder enforces the expiry on itself.

@8
The store sees the lease lapse, but waits another 500 milliseconds, the drift bound, in case A's clock runs slow and A is still serving for a moment.

@9-10
Then B acquires the lease, which runs to t plus 12 and a half seconds, and clients redirect to B, which serves reads under its own lease. Between A stepping down and B taking over nothing was served: roughly one lease length plus the grace period.

@11
The second failure is subtler. If A's clock ran slower than the bound, or A paused in the middle of a request, it could still answer after B became leader. That is a stale read, the read side of split brain. Spanner buys certainty with its TrueTime clocks; other systems add fencing tokens or accept the risk.

@12
The trade-off: one local lookup instead of a quorum round trip, paid for with an outage window after a crash and a safety argument that rests on clocks. A shorter lease means a shorter outage but more renewal traffic. For the matcher, the lesson closes the gap with fencing: an epoch travels in every write, and the store rejects writes from an older epoch, so a matcher that wakes from a pause still believing it owns the city cannot assign a driver twice.
