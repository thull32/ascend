@0
Three servers, A, B and C, all followers in term 1, each with a random election timeout between 150 and 300 milliseconds. This is a Raft election, and the point of showing it in a Paxos lesson is one sentence: a Raft election is Multi-Paxos's phase 1, run once for every future slot.

@1
A's timeout fires first. It moves to term 2, becomes a candidate and votes for itself.

@2
A asks B and C for their votes. A voter grants only if the candidate's term is at least its own and the candidate's log is at least as up to date. That second condition is Raft's addition: the winner must already hold everything a majority has, so it never has to reconcile slot by slot the way a new Multi-Paxos leader does.

@3
Both grant their vote. Each server votes once per term and writes the vote to disk before replying. That is the same discipline as a Paxos acceptor, which must write its promise to disk before replying, because an acceptor that forgets a promise after a restart can accept an older proposal and let two values be chosen.

@4
A holds all three votes, more than the two a majority needs, and leads term 2. It now has what a Multi-Paxos leader has after phase 1, and can skip straight to accepts, one round trip per entry, which is what AppendEntries is.

@5
The leader sends heartbeats, which reset the followers' timers so no other server starts a competing round. A single stable proposer is how production systems avoid duelling proposers.

@6
Then a partition cuts A off, and B and C stop hearing heartbeats.

@7-8
C times out first and asks for votes in term 3. B grants, so C has two of three, a majority, even without A.

@9
C leads term 3. When A reconnects, the higher term in any message makes it step down, and its uncommitted entries are overwritten. In Paxos language: a higher-numbered prepare won a majority, so the old proposer's accepts can no longer succeed.
