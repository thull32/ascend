@0
This picture shows plain quorum replication, three replicas holding v1, but watch it for the one fact Paxos is built on. A write must reach 2 of the 3, and a read must consult 2 of the 3. Two plus two is four, more than three, so any two majorities of three share at least one member. In Paxos terms, the write is a majority accepting a value, and the read is a later proposer collecting promises from a majority.

@1-2
A client sends a write of v2 to a coordinator, which sends it to all three replicas in parallel, stamped with timestamp 12.

@3-4
Replicas 1 and 2 take it and acknowledge. Replica 3 is slow and still holds v1. Two acknowledgements are a majority, so the write succeeds without it. Nobody needs every node, which is how a majority protocol escapes waiting forever on a node that might have crashed.

@5-6
Now a read arrives, and the coordinator asks all three replicas, waiting for the first two answers.

@7
Replica 3 answers first, with the stale v1.

@8
Then replica 2 answers with v2. This is the overlap at work. Whichever two replicas answer, at least one of them was in the majority that took the write, so the newest value is guaranteed to be among the replies. That shared member is exactly how a later Paxos proposer learns what an earlier majority already accepted, and why it must adopt that value instead of its own.

@9-10
The coordinator returns the value with the highest timestamp, v2, and writes it back to the stale replica, which is read repair.

@11
Break the arithmetic and the guarantee goes. With a write quorum of 1 and a read quorum of 1, a read can consult only a replica that missed the write, and the client sees v1 after v2 was acknowledged. Flexible Paxos uses the same arithmetic the other way round: the two quorums need not both be majorities, as long as together they exceed N.

@12-13
The rest is the fine print of leaderless stores: sloppy quorums break the overlap, equal timestamps need a tiebreak, and a quorum alone is not linearizable. That last point is the reason consensus adds promises, numbered proposals and a leader on top of plain majorities.
