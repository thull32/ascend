@0
Three replicas, N1, N2 and N3, counting likes. Each holds a vector with one slot per replica, all zeros. Two rules make this a grow-only counter, a G-Counter. A replica increments only its own slot. And any two states merge by taking the larger value slot by slot. The count is the sum of the slots. Because that merge does not care about order, grouping or repetition, every order of delivery ends in the same place.

@1
N1 counts one like. Its own slot becomes 1. No message, no lock, and it is instant.

@2
N2 counts two likes, so its slot becomes 2. The replicas disagree for now, N1 says 1 and N2 says 2, and that is allowed.

@3
N3 counts one. And now suppose N1 is partitioned from the other two.

@4
N2 sends its state to N3, which merges slot by slot: 0, 2, 1, a total of 3. Nothing was lost, and nothing was counted twice.

@5
N1, still cut off, keeps counting, and its slot goes to 2. This is the whole point: a CRDT never has to refuse a write, partition or not.

@6-7
The partition heals, and N1 sends its state to the others. Each merges it. Their own slots are untouched, since the maximum of their value and N1's zero is their value, while N1's slot jumps to 2. N2 now reads 2, 2, 0, which is 4. N3 reads 2, 2, 1, which is 5.

@8-9
The others reply with their states, and N1 merges them, reaching 2, 2, 1: 5. N1 and N3 agree on 5. N2 still reads 4, because nobody has yet sent it N3's slot. One more exchange brings it to 5 as well. That is what strong eventual consistency promises: replicas that have received the same updates hold the same state, in any delivery order, with duplicates, or with messages dropped and resent later.

@10
Why a vector, and not one integer merged by max? Because if two replicas each counted 2, the maximum of 2 and 2 is 2, and an increment vanishes. Giving each replica its own slot is what makes concurrent increments add instead of collide.

@11
Decrements need a PN-Counter: two G-Counters, one for increments and one for decrements, subtracted. And there is a cost: a slot for every replica that ever existed, so churn bloats the state until retired ids are garbage collected. Nor can a counter enforce a rule like never exceed 100. Invariants that need agreement need consensus, not CRDTs.

@12
The trade-off. Always-available, coordination-free writes with guaranteed convergence, but only for operations that commute. Anything with a global invariant is out of scope. Counters, sets, and collaborative text in Yjs, Automerge and Riak's data types all work this way.
