@0
No leader here. A client, a coordinator, and three replicas, each holding x equals v1 with timestamp 9. Three numbers set the behaviour: N, the replica count, is 3. A write must be acknowledged by W of them, here 2. A read must consult R of them, also 2. The one rule to hold onto: when W plus R is greater than N, here 4 against 3, every read set overlaps every write set, so a read always reaches at least one copy of the latest write.

@1
The client sends a write of v2 to a coordinator. Any node can coordinate.

@2
The coordinator sends it to all three replicas in parallel, stamped with timestamp 12.

@3
Replicas 1 and 2 apply it and acknowledge. Replica 3 is slow, stuck in a garbage collection pause or a disk stall, and still holds v1. Two acknowledgements meet W, so the coordinator does not wait.

@4
The write succeeds even though replica 3 never took it. A quorum of two out of three tolerates one slow or dead replica on the write path.

@5-6
Now a read arrives. The coordinator asks all three replicas and waits for the first two replies.

@7
Replica 3 answers first, with the stale v1 and timestamp 9.

@8
Then replica 2 answers with v2. This is the overlap rule paying off. Two readers and two writers out of three must share at least one replica, so the newest version is guaranteed to be among the replies.

@9
The coordinator returns the value with the highest timestamp, v2.

@10
And because it noticed replica 3 was stale, it writes v2 back to it. That is read repair. Keys that nobody reads are fixed instead by background anti-entropy sweeps.

@11
The failure mode is choosing W plus R no greater than N, say W of 1 and R of 1 for speed. A read may then consult only a replica that missed the write, and the client sees v1 after v2 was acknowledged. So tune per workload: write to all and read from one for read-heavy data, or write to one for write-heavy data you can afford to lose.

@12
Three subtleties. Sloppy quorums with hinted handoff, where non-home nodes accept writes during a partition, break the overlap guarantee. Two concurrent writes with the same timestamp need a tiebreak. And even a strict quorum is not linearizable without extra care.

@13
The trade-off: no leader and no election, availability through partial failures, and consistency you can tune per request. But every operation costs N messages, and the guarantees are weaker than they look. This is the model of Dynamo, Cassandra, Riak and Voldemort.
