@0
A producer, a topic split into three partitions, and a consumer group with two consumers. Each partition is an ordered, append-only log. The producer hashes each message's key to pick a partition, so all events for one key land in one log, in order. Ordering holds within a partition, and only there.

@1-2
User 1 hashes to partition 1 and is appended at offset 0. User 7 also hashes to partition 1, at offset 1. The broker never reorders or rewrites what it has appended.

@3
Order 9 hashes to partition 2, and lands at offset 0 there.

@4
Now the consumer group. The group coordinator gives each partition to exactly one consumer: Consumer 1 gets partitions 0 and 2, Consumer 2 gets partition 1. Partitions, not messages, are the unit of parallelism.

@5
Consumer 1 polls partition 0. Each consumer tracks its own position, an offset, per partition, and the broker deletes nothing on read, so another group could read the same log independently.

@6
After processing, Consumer 1 commits its offset. This ordering is the choice that decides your guarantee. Commit after processing, and a crash in between replays the records: at least once. Commit before processing, and a crash in between loses them: at most once. Exactly once needs more, transactions or an idempotent sink.

@7
Now Consumer 2 dies. After its session timeout, the coordinator rebalances, and Consumer 1 takes over partition 1, resuming from Consumer 2's last committed offset. The whole group pauses during the rebalance, and any records Consumer 2 processed but had not committed are delivered again. Those are exactly the duplicates the rest of this lesson is about absorbing.

@8
Two design limits. A group can use at most three consumers here, one per partition, and a fourth would sit idle, so the partition count chosen at creation caps parallelism. And a hot key sends all its traffic to one partition, which one consumer must handle alone.

@9
The trade-off. Partitioning by key gives per-key ordering and horizontal scale with a simple, durable broker. The price is no global order, parallelism fixed by the partition count, and rebalances that pause consumption and replay uncommitted work.
