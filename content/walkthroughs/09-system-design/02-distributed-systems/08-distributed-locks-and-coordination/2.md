@0
Three servers, A, B and C, each a follower in term 1 with a random election timeout between 150 and 300 milliseconds. This time no separate lock service is involved. The service embeds Raft, and whoever leads the log is the leader. Watch how that gives you fencing without any extra machinery.

@1
A's timeout fires first. It moves to term 2, becomes a candidate and votes for itself.

@2
A asks B and C for their votes. A voter grants only if the candidate's term is at least its own and its log is at least as up to date.

@3
Both grant. Each server votes at most once per term, and writes that vote to disk before replying.

@4
With all three votes, more than the two a majority needs, A leads term 2. A majority means at most one leader per term.

@5
A sends heartbeats, which keep the followers from starting elections of their own.

@6
Now a partition cuts A off from B and C, and the heartbeats stop arriving.

@7-8
C times out first, starts term 3, and asks B, which grants. Two of three is a majority, so C wins without A.

@9
C leads term 3. Here is the fencing. Anything A sends now carries the old term 2, so it is rejected, and its entries can never commit. When A reconnects and sees term 3, it steps down, and its uncommitted entries are overwritten. The term plays the role a fencing token plays for a lock. Whichever way you elect a leader, it is the single writer for something, and that something must check its token.
