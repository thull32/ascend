@0
A Raft cluster: a leader, A, in term 2, two followers, B and C, and some clients. There is no lease store anywhere in this picture. The lease comes from the followers themselves, and here is the one idea: a follower that has just heard from the leader will not start an election until its own election timeout runs out. That promise is the lease.

@1
At time t, the leader sends an ordinary heartbeat round to both followers.

@2
A majority acknowledges. Each acknowledgement carries a promise: I heard a leader at t, and I will not vote anyone else in before my election timeout passes. The election timeouts here start at 150 milliseconds, so both followers are bound until at least t plus 150 milliseconds.

@3
So no other leader can be elected before t plus the minimum election timeout. A measures its lease from the moment it sent the round, not from when the acknowledgements arrived, and subtracts a margin for clock drift. For that long, A knows it is still the leader.

@4
A client reads, and A answers from its own state. No heartbeat round per read, which is the round trip ReadIndex would have paid.

@5
Now the failure: A is cut off. Its next heartbeat round gets no acknowledgements, so its lease is not extended.

@6
When the lease runs out by A's own clock, A stops answering reads locally. It may still believe it is the leader, but without a fresh majority it can no longer prove it.

@7
Only after their election timeouts expire, as they promised, do B and C hold an election, and C wins term 3. By then A has already stopped serving, so the old leader and the new one never answer reads at the same time.

@8
Clients move to C.

@9
The catch is that all of this rests on clocks. If the followers' clocks run fast, or the leader's runs slow, by more than the margin, an election can finish while the old leader still believes its lease, and it serves a stale read. ReadIndex costs a round trip per read but needs no clock assumption at all. The lease trades that round trip for a bet on bounded clock drift.
