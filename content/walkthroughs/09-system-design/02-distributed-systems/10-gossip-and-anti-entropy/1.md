@0
Eight nodes, N1 to N8, all holding version 1, and no coordinator. This is push-pull gossip with a fanout of 3: every round, every node, informed or not, exchanges state with three peers chosen at random. An informed node pushes the news; an uninformed node that happens to contact an informed one pulls it. Watch how few rounds it takes.

@1
N1 learns something new, version 2. It might be a configuration change, a membership update, or a peer's heartbeat counter. It has no list of who needs it.

@2
Round 1: eight nodes, three exchanges each, 24 exchanges in all. Five new nodes learn version 2, three of them by push from N1 and two by pulling from N1 when they happened to pick it. The rest of the exchanges met nodes in the same state. Now 6 of 8 know.

@3
Round 2: another 24 exchanges, and the last two nodes, N5 and N6, learn it. All 8 know.

@4
Everyone has version 2 after just 2 rounds and 48 exchanges, and no node made more than three exchanges a round. The informed count multiplies each round, so doubling the cluster adds about one round. Pull is what makes the finish fast: late in the spread, a straggler that asks almost anyone gets the news, instead of waiting for a push to find it. That is why push-pull, both halves in one exchange, is the standard choice.

@5
Two flavours. Rumour mongering spreads a specific new update and retires it after a few rounds: cheap, but a rare node can miss it. Anti-entropy periodically compares full state, or a digest of it, with a peer: heavier, but it never stops, and it repairs any difference however old. It is the safety net under rumour mongering, and Cassandra and Riak run both.

@6
The failure modes. During a partition, each side converges to its own view. And stale news lingers: a removed node can be re-added by a peer that never heard it left, unless removals are gossiped as tombstones.

@7
The trade-off. No coordinator, no single point of failure, and load spread evenly. In exchange, delivery is only eventual, most exchanges are redundant once the news is out, and each node keeps membership state that grows with the cluster. Cassandra, Consul and Redis Cluster all gossip for membership and failure detection.
