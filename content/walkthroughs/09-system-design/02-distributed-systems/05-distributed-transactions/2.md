@0
An orchestrator and four services, each with its own database: Order, Payment, Inventory and Shipping. A saga runs one business transaction as a sequence of local transactions, each committed straight away. If a later step fails, the earlier steps are not rolled back. They are undone, by compensating transactions.

@1-3
Step 1, the order service creates a pending order and commits. Step 2, the payment service charges 40 dollars and commits. Step 3, inventory reserves the stock and commits. Each change is visible to everyone the moment it commits: there is no isolation across a saga.

@4
Step 4 fails. Shipping is down, or rejects the request. The first three steps are already committed, and no database rollback can reach them.

@5
So the orchestrator switches to compensation. It runs the undo action for every completed step, in reverse order. Each compensation is itself a local transaction, retried until it succeeds.

@6
First, release the reserved stock, undoing step 3.

@7
Then refund the 40 dollars, undoing step 2. Notice what kind of undo this is. A refund is a new transaction that reverses the effect; the original charge still happened, and is still in the ledger.

@8
Finally, cancel the order, undoing step 1.

@9
The saga ends in the aborted state. Every transition was written to the saga log before the next call, so if the orchestrator itself crashes halfway, its replacement reads the log and resumes from the last recorded step, instead of leaving a half-done order behind.

@10
Three failure modes. Another request can see the intermediate state, an order that was pending and is then cancelled. A compensation might arrive twice, so it must be idempotent. And some actions have no undo, like an email already sent, so put the irreversible step last.

@11
The trade-off. Sagas keep services independent and available, with no locks held across services, but you give up atomicity and isolation, and you design every compensation by hand. Choreography, with events and no orchestrator, removes the central coordinator, at the cost of a flow nobody can read in one place.
