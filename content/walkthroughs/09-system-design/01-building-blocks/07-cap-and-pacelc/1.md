@0
Three replicas of one key, all at v1. A write needs two acknowledgements and a read consults two replicas. Two plus two is more than three, so every read set overlaps every write set. Keep asking one question as this plays out: which side can still make progress when one replica cannot be heard from?

@1-2
The client sends a write to a coordinator, and the coordinator sends v2 with a timestamp to all three replicas.

@3
R1 and R2 acknowledge. R3 does not. Here it is slow, a garbage collection pause or a disk stall, but from the coordinator's seat that looks exactly like a partition: a node that cannot be heard from. Two acknowledgements meet the quorum, so the coordinator does not wait for the third.

@4
The write succeeds while R3 still holds v1. This is the majority side of a partition at work: two of three can still commit. One silent replica costs nothing on the write path.

@5-7
A read arrives. The coordinator asks all three and takes the first two replies. R3 answers first, with the stale v1.

@8-9
Then R2 answers with v2. Because the two readers must include at least one of the two writers, the newest version is guaranteed to be there, and the coordinator returns the highest timestamp: v2.

@10
The coordinator noticed that R3 is behind, so it writes v2 back to it. That is read repair; background anti-entropy fixes replicas nobody reads.

@11
Now the dial PACELC cares about. Set both quorums to one for speed and a read may hit only the replica that missed the write, so a client sees v1 after v2 was acknowledged. That is choosing latency over consistency every day, not just during a partition.

@12
The caveats. Sloppy quorums, where nodes outside the key's home set accept writes during a partition and hand them over later, break the overlap guarantee. Equal timestamps need a tiebreak. And even a strict quorum is not linearizable without extra work, so it is not CAP's C either.

@13
The trade-off: no leader to elect, availability through partial failures, consistency tunable per request, at the cost of a message to every replica and guarantees weaker than they look. And the side you did not see: a client that can reach only the lone replica cannot gather two acknowledgements. It must reject the write, which is the consistent choice, or accept it locally and reconcile later, which is the available one.
