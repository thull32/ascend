@0
An app server, a cache and a database, and both hold price 7 equals 10. This is write-through: every write goes to the cache, and the cache writes it to the database synchronously before it acknowledges. Caching writes is a dial between latency and durability, and this is one end of it. Watch where it pays.

@1
The app writes price 7 equals 12, and the write goes to the cache. The cache is the front door of the write path.

@2
The cache forwards the write to the database and waits. That synchronous hop is what puts the through in write-through.

@3
The database commits the row.

@4
Only now is the app acknowledged. Here is the bill: the write costs a cache write plus a database write, about 6 milliseconds instead of about 1.

@5
And here is what it bought. A read hits the cache and gets 12. There is no invalidation step to forget, as long as every write goes through the cache, one at a time per key.

@6-8
That last condition matters. Two writers, with nothing ordering their writes to one key. Writer A sets 13 in the cache, and writer B sets 14 just after. Then B's database write lands first.

@9
And A's lands last. The cache says 14, the database says 13, and they stay that way until the next write or an eviction: the two stores applied the same writes in different orders. The fix is to serialise writes per key, with a version check or a per-key lock, so both stores see one order.

@10-11
With writes serialised and both stores back at 14, the database goes down just as a write of 15 arrives. The cache tries its synchronous write, and cannot complete it.

@12
So the write fails, and the cache keeps 14. Writes are exactly as available as the database, never more.

@13
Say it the way the lesson's template does. I choose write-through because reads after a write must not be stale. It costs about 5 milliseconds on every write, per-key ordering for concurrent writers, and cache space spent on keys nobody reads, and that is acceptable when a fresh read matters more than a fast write. If write latency became the constraint, I would move the dial toward write-behind.
