@0
An app server, a cache and a database, and both hold price 7 equals 10. This is write-through: every write goes to the cache, and the cache writes it to the database synchronously before it acknowledges. Caching writes is a dial between latency and durability, and this is one end of it. Watch where it pays.

@1
The app writes price 7 equals 12, and the write goes to the cache. The cache is the front door of the write path.

@2
The cache forwards the write to the database and waits. That synchronous hop is what puts the through in write-through.

@3
The database commits the row.

@4
Only now is the app acknowledged. Here is the bill: the write costs a cache write plus a database write, about 6 milliseconds instead of about 1.

@5
And here is what it bought. A read hits the cache and is guaranteed fresh. There is no invalidation step to forget and no stale window.

@6-7
Now the database goes down, and a write of 13 arrives. The cache tries its synchronous write, and cannot complete it.

@8
So the write fails, and the cache keeps 12. Writes are exactly as available as the database, never more. Consistency is preserved, and availability pays for it.

@9
Say it the way the lesson's template does. I choose write-through because reads must never be stale after a write. It costs about 5 milliseconds on every write, plus cache space spent on keys nobody reads, and that is acceptable when a fresh read matters more than a fast write. If write latency became the constraint, I would move the dial toward write-behind.
