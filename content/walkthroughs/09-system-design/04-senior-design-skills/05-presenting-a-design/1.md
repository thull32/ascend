@0
A producer, a Kafka topic with three partitions, and a consumer group with two consumers. This is the deep dive from the lesson's mock interview: viewing-progress heartbeats, keyed by profile. Each partition is an ordered, append-only log, and the producer hashes the key to choose one. Ordering is guaranteed within a partition only, and that is exactly enough, because every event for one profile lands in the same partition.

@1-2
Profile 17's first event hashes to partition 2, at offset 0. Profile 42's hashes to partition 1, at offset 0.

@3
Profile 17 again. Same key, same hash, same partition 2, now at offset 1, behind its earlier event. A consumer will always see this profile's events in the order they were written. That matters here because a rewind moves the position backwards, so you cannot merge positions with a maximum. The order has to survive.

@4-6
Profile 8 lands in partition 1 at offset 1. Profile 42 returns to partition 1 at offset 2, behind its own earlier event. And profile 17's third event goes to partition 2 at offset 2. A rewind can never be overtaken by an older heartbeat for the same profile.

@7
The consumer group. Each partition goes to exactly one consumer: Consumer 1 gets partitions 0 and 2, Consumer 2 gets partition 1. Partitions, not messages, are the unit of parallelism.

@8-9
Consumer 1 polls, tracking its own offset per partition, and commits after processing. Commit after processing means a crash in between replays records: at-least-once delivery.

@10
Now Consumer 2 dies. After its session timeout the group rebalances, and Consumer 1 takes over partition 1, resuming from Consumer 2's last committed offset. The group pauses during the rebalance, and anything Consumer 2 processed but had not committed is delivered again. That replay is why the design writes with a version: consumers set the write timestamp to ingestion time, so a replayed old event cannot overwrite newer data.

@11
Two limits to say out loud. At most three consumers can share this group, one per partition, so the partition count caps parallelism. And a hot key sends all its traffic to one partition, which one consumer must handle alone.

@12
The trade-off, stated the way an interviewer wants to hear it. Keying by profile gives per-profile ordering and horizontal scale with a simple, durable broker, at the price of no global order, parallelism fixed by the partition count, and rebalances that pause consumption. Choose keys that spread load, yet keep together the events that must stay ordered.
