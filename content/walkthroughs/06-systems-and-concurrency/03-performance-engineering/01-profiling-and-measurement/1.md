@0
A sampling profiler works by freezing the thread many times a second and writing down every frame on its stack, from main to the innermost call. This animation shows the stack such a sample would capture, at each moment of a small program. Main calls factorial of 4. Every call gets its own frame, holding its argument, its return address and any locals.

@1-4
Factorial of 4 cannot finish yet: it needs factorial of 3 first, so it pushes another frame and waits, with its own n of 4 parked in its frame. Factorial of 3 does the same, then 2, then 1. Each new frame records who to return to. Freeze the thread now, and the sample reads main, factorial, factorial, factorial, factorial: one line of the folded file.

@5
Factorial of 0 hits the base case and makes no further call. It will return 1. The stack is at its deepest, 6 frames counting main, so recursion depth is live memory.

@6-10
Now the frames unwind. Factorial of 0 returns 1 and its frame is popped. The value lands in the caller, which resumes right after the call and multiplies by its own n. Factorial of 1 returns 1, factorial of 2 returns 2, factorial of 3 returns 6, and factorial of 4 returns 24 to main.

@11
Factorial of 4 is 24. Five factorial frames were pushed and popped in last in, first out order. A profiler never sees this movie, only freeze-frames from it. A flame graph stacks thousands of those snapshots and merges identical prefixes. The width of a box is its share of samples, the x-axis is not time, and the exposed top edge of a box is its self time.
