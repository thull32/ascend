@0
Two producers put items into a bounded buffer that holds 2. Two consumers take them out. Put must wait while the buffer is full, and take must wait while it is empty. So there are two condition variables, not full and not empty, guarded by one mutex.

@1
P1 puts item 1 and P2 puts item 2. The buffer holds 2 of 2.

@2-3
Both producers come back for more, find the buffer full, and wait on not full. They stay parked, using no CPU. The consumers have not started taking yet, so for a tick nothing moves.

@4
The consumers arrive. C1 takes item 1 and C2 takes item 2, and the buffer is empty again. Each take notifies one producer on not full.

@5
The producers wake and put items 3 and 4, and the consumers take both. Everything flows.

@6-7
Then the consumers come back to an empty buffer, and both wait on not empty. Now the consumers are the ones parked, and the producers are paused, so again nothing moves.

@8
P1 and P2 put items 5 and 6. Each put signals not empty, so C1 and C2 wake and take them.

@9-10
From here it runs steadily: items 7 and 8, then 9 and 10, each put and taken in turn.

@11
The buffer decouples the two sides. Producers only wait when the buffer is full, consumers never spin on an empty buffer, and the capacity bounds memory. That bound is backpressure: the slow side sets the pace. This shape is every work queue, pipe and channel you will use. In Go, a buffered channel is exactly this, with no condition variables to write.
