@0
A counting semaphore with 2 permits guards a pool of 2 resources. Think of 2 database connections. Acquire takes a permit, or blocks if the count is 0. Release gives one back and wakes one waiter. Five threads each need a resource for two ticks.

@1
T1 acquires, and the count drops to 1. T2 acquires, and it drops to 0. T3, T4 and T5 each find no permits, and block in a first in, first out wait queue.

@2
T1 and T2 use their resources, and the other three wait.

@3
T1 and T2 release, and the count rises back to 2. Each release wakes one waiter, so T3 and T4 acquire, and the count is 0 again. T5 is still queued.

@4
T3 and T4 work.

@5-6
They release, and T5 is woken and acquires, leaving one permit spare while T5 works.

@7
T5 releases, and the count is back to 2.

@8
At most 2 threads were ever inside at once, and the rest queued in order. That is how connection pools and concurrency limits work. Unlike a condition variable, a semaphore remembers: a release with nobody waiting raises the count, so a wake-up can never be lost. A mutex is a semaphore with one permit plus ownership, meaning only the locker may unlock. Use semaphores for bounded resources and bounded parallelism, not for mutual exclusion.
