@0
Two consumers, C1 and C2, want an item, but the queue is empty. They could poll it in a loop, but that burns a core doing nothing. A condition variable lets a thread sleep until the predicate, here "the queue is not empty", may have changed. The rule to hold onto: check the predicate in a while loop, while holding the mutex.

@1
C1 locks the mutex and checks the predicate. The queue is empty.

@2
So C1 calls wait on the condition variable, passing the mutex. In one atomic step, wait releases the mutex and puts C1 to sleep. That atomicity matters: if the release and the sleep were separate, a signal could arrive in the gap between them and be lost, and C1 would sleep forever.

@3-4
C2 does the same: it locks the mutex, sees an empty queue, and waits. Both consumers are now on the condition variable's wait list, and the mutex is free.

@5-6
The producer, P, locks the mutex, which is free because both waiters released it, and pushes item 42.

@7
P calls notify one. C1 moves from the condition variable's wait list to the mutex's wait list. It is woken, but it cannot run yet, because P still holds the mutex. C2 keeps sleeping; notify all would have woken both.

@8-9
P unlocks, and C1 gets the mutex. C1 returns from wait holding it, and checks the predicate again in its while loop. The queue has 1 item, so it proceeds. Why check again? Because wake-ups can be spurious, and a faster consumer could have taken the item first. That is why it is a loop, never an if.

@10-11
C1 pops 42, unlocks, and carries on with the item. C2 sleeps on until the next notify.

@12
The lesson in one sentence: a condition variable is not the condition. It is a wait list attached to a mutex. The state lives in the predicate, and a notify only says something may have changed, so look again. The waiting side is lock, while not predicate wait, act, unlock. The changing side is lock, change the state, notify, unlock.
