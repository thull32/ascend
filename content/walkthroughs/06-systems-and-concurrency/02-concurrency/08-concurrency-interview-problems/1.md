@0
The bounded blocking queue, the most common concurrency interview question. Two producers put items into a buffer that holds 3, and two consumers take them out. Put must wait while the buffer is full, take must wait while it is empty. So there are two condition variables, not full and not empty, guarded by one mutex, and the invariant is that the count stays between 0 and the capacity.

@1-2
P1 puts item 1 and P2 puts item 2. Then P1 puts item 3, and the buffer is full at 3 of 3. P2 tries to put and finds it full, so it waits on not full.

@3-4
P1 comes back, finds the buffer full too, and waits. Both producers are parked on not full, using no CPU, while the consumers have not arrived yet.

@5
The consumers arrive. C1 takes item 1 and C2 takes item 2, leaving 1 of 3. Each take notifies the other side, on not full.

@6
Both producers wake. P1 puts item 4 and P2 puts item 5, and the buffer is full again. Meanwhile C1 takes item 3 and C2 takes item 4.

@7-9
C1 takes item 5 and the buffer is empty. C2 finds it empty and waits on not empty, and then C1 does too. Now the consumers are parked while the producers are elsewhere.

@10
P1 and P2 put items 6 and 7. Each put notifies not empty, so C1 and C2 wake, re-check that the buffer is not empty, and take them.

@11-12
From here it runs steadily: items 8 and 9, then 10 and 11, put and taken in turn.

@13
The buffer decouples the two sides. Producers only wait when it is full, consumers never spin on an empty one, and the capacity bounds memory. In an interview, say the invariant out loud, wait in a while loop under the mutex, and notify the other side after every successful operation.
