@0
Five philosophers sit around a table with five forks, one between each pair. To eat, a philosopher needs both neighbouring forks: P1 needs F1 on the left and F2 on the right, and so on round the table, until P5, who needs F5 and F1. The naive rule is: pick up your left fork, then your right.

@1
Everyone picks up their left fork at the same moment. P1 holds F1, P2 holds F2, and so on to P5, who holds F5.

@2
And nobody can move. P1 wants F2, which P2 holds. P2 wants F3, which P3 holds. Around the table it goes, until P5 wants F1, which P1 holds. The wait-for graph is a cycle through all five, and every philosopher is waiting for a fork that only a waiting philosopher can release.

@3
Every philosopher holds one fork and waits for the neighbour's: circular wait. Real programs hit exactly this with two mutexes taken in opposite orders.

@4
The fix is Dijkstra's resource hierarchy: number the forks, and always pick up the lower-numbered one first. For four philosophers nothing changes, because their left fork is already the lower one. Only P5 changes: it now reaches for F1 before F5. That one change is enough to break the cycle.

@5
Replay. P1 to P4 each take their first fork. P5 reaches for F1, finds P1 holding it, and blocks while holding nothing.

@6-8
So F5 is free, and P4 takes it. P4 holds F4 and F5, eats, and puts both down. P1, P2 and P3 are still waiting on their right-hand neighbours.

@9-11
F4 is free now, so P3 takes it, eats with F3 and F4, and puts them down.

@12-14
That frees F3 for P2, who eats and puts down F2 and F3.

@15-17
P1 finally takes F2, eats, and puts down F1 and F2. P5, which has been waiting for F1 the whole time, takes it.

@18-20
P5 takes F5, eats, and puts both forks down.

@21
Everyone ate, in 18 ticks. The waiting unwound like a chain, because the one philosopher who would have closed the loop was waiting while holding nothing. Two alternatives: a waiter, a semaphore with n minus one seats, so at most four philosophers try at once; or try-lock with backoff. All of them work by breaking one of the four Coffman conditions.
